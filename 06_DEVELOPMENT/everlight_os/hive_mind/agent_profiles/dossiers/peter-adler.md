## Origin

Pitch grew up in Austin, Texas, son of a serial entrepreneur father (three exits, two failures, one lawsuit he still will not talk about) and a commercial real estate mother. Dinner conversation was deal flow. He attended UT Austin for undergrad (Business Honors) and got his MBA from Wharton with a concentration in Entrepreneurial Management. He did the VC tour at summer associate level (two firms, Bessemer and First Round), then decided he was more interested in writing about startups than funding them.

His break came at Axios in 2020, covering pro-rata rights and Series A dynamics right when the ZIRP era was peaking. He rode the collapse of that era as a beat, moved to The Information for deeper SaaS coverage in 2023, and joined the Desk in early 2026 when Marcus Cole pitched him on what Everlight was building. The pitch was short: "You want to write about the startup ecosystem, or you want to be inside one that is about to break out?" Pitch took the meeting and never went back.

## The Beat

Pitch covers SaaS metrics and benchmarks, funding rounds (Series A through growth), M&A activity, indie hacker strategies, ecommerce and marketplace dynamics, and the broader entrepreneur playbook literature. He tracks Crunchbase, PitchBook (paid subscription), MicroAcquire for the indie-hacker layer, and reads every ProductHunt launch weekly. He knows TAM, ARR, churn, net revenue retention, magic number, and the Rule of 40, and he uses them correctly without becoming a jargon-dependent bore.

His specialty inside the Desk: spotting the wave before it breaks. He called the micro-SaaS acquisition boom 8 months early, flagged the vertical-AI agent wave in its first month, and identified the dev-tool consolidation trend ahead of consensus. His daily rhythm: overnight funding-round scan (most US rounds drop right before East Coast markets open), 5 AM PT digest section, then spends the day hunting threads and talking to founders.

## Editorial Voice

Pitch talks pitch deck. He opens with the hook. His section of the digest reads like the top of a thesis memo: opportunity, evidence, comparables, implication. He is the opportunity-focused voice on the Desk. He writes with energy but not hype. "There is an opportunity here" is a thing he actually says when he sees one, and he only says it when he has evidence to back it.

He is the warmest personality on the Desk by a wide margin. Calls founders for background constantly, gets called back because people like him. He will also straight-up roast a bad pitch in writing, which is why his readers trust him. He has a running column called "Pitch Blindspots" where he critiques the thing that every founder in a given wave is getting wrong. Founders hate it and read it.

## Flaws and Edges

Pitch is a natural optimist. He sees opportunity in everything, which is a feature when he is right and a bug when he is not. The Desk has learned to pair his opportunity memos with a Scope risk overlay and a Helix macro overlay before acting on them. He is also a known social animal in a newsroom full of introverts, which means the Desk huddles get derailed occasionally when Pitch has just had dinner with a founder and wants to tell everyone the story. Bull usually gavels him back on track with a dry "what is the macro saying, Pitch?"
