## 1. The power demand (chart 03)

- U.S. data centers used **176 TWh in 2023 = 4.4% of all U.S. electricity** (up from 58 TWh / 1.9% in 2014). Projected to **325-580 TWh by 2028 = 6.7% to 12% of the entire national grid.** Source: Lawrence Berkeley National Lab, Dec 2024 (the strongest anchor in the space).
- In capacity terms: roughly **80 GW (2025) heading to ~150 GW (2028)** -- the grid equivalent of adding a country.
- A single hyperscale AI campus can need power for **100,000+ homes**. Typical centers draw like 10,000-25,000 homes.
- **Texas (ERCOT):** the grid may need to roughly double from ~85 GW (2024) to **up to 218 GW by 2031**, largely data-center driven.

## 2. The cleanest, hardest data point: PJM capacity costs

PJM is the grid operator for the mid-Atlantic/Midwest (~65 million people). Its independent market monitor (Monitoring Analytics) found:
- Data-center demand drove **63% (~$9.3 billion) of the increase** in the 2025/26 capacity auction.
- The auction price itself went near-vertical: ~$30/MW-day to ~$270/MW-day in the Dec 2024 auction (a ~9-10x move).
- In the latest auction, **~$6.2 billion of the $6.5B data-center capacity cost is for load that does NOT exist yet** (forecast, not built). Ratepayers are pre-funding demand that may never arrive.

## 4. Why the cost shifts onto households (the rate plumbing)

Harvard Electricity Law Initiative ("Extracting Profits from the Public," Martin & Peskoe, March 2025) reviewed ~50 regulatory cases and found three channels:
1. **Secret/special utility contracts** with data centers, reviewed in opaque processes.
2. A **disconnect between federally-regulated transmission rates and state-set retail rates** that spreads data-center transmission costs to retail customers.
3. **Ratepayers paying for infrastructure tied to data centers that are never built** (the same forecast-load problem PJM flagged).

## 5. The "bring your own power" fight (co-location)

- The original dodge: plug a data center **behind the meter** straight into a power plant, bypassing the grid and its cost-sharing. FERC **rejected** the Talen-Amazon Susquehanna behind-the-meter deal (Nov 2024).
- It was restructured (June 2025) into a 17-year, $18B, front-of-the-meter PPA -- back onto the grid structure.
- **December 2025:** FERC issued its PJM co-location order, calling the existing rules "unjust and unreasonable," and created new transmission-service options.
- Either way the ratepayer is exposed; behind-the-meter lets the center dodge grid charges everyone else pays (free-rider), while front-of-the-meter tightens the market and lifts prices for all.

## 6. Tax breaks -- the public revenue side

- **Virginia:** sales-tax exemption cost **$1.6 billion in FY2025** (~16x initial projections); statewide loss near $1.94B. Foregone K-12 education funding rose to $267M.
- **Georgia:** exemption ballooned from $10M (2020) toward **$2.5 billion**; a University of Georgia study found incentives drove only ~30% of siting decisions (not the 90% assumed).
- Pattern: VA, TX, and IL each saw revenue-loss spikes >1,000%. Multiple states are now reconsidering.

## 7. The asymmetry, quantified

- **Big Tech AI capex:** ~$160B (2023) -> ~$300B+ (2025) -> planned **~$725B (2026, +77% YoY)**. (Capex is investment, not profit -- but it shows the scale.)
- **Market reward:** Alphabet nearing ~$4.3T valuation, +62% in 2025 on AI optimism; Microsoft AI revenue run-rate ~$37B (+123% YoY).
- **Public cost:** ~$9.3B socialized onto PJM ratepayers in one cycle + $16-21/mo on individual bills + below-scarcity municipal water in drought basins + billions in foregone state tax revenue.

One side books the upside on its market cap; the other side pays it on a regulated utility bill it cannot opt out of. **Privatized gains, socialized inputs.**

## 8. Stranded-asset risk (who holds the bag)

- ~$6.2B of the latest PJM auction pays for data centers that don't exist yet.
- Transmission/capacity upgrades sit on the regulated rate base for **20-40 years.** If an AI cluster is cancelled or the boom cools, the grid investment was still made -- and **remaining ratepayers keep paying for it.**
- The market itself is wobbly: facilities coming online in 2025 reportedly face ~$40B in annual depreciation against only ~$15-20B in revenue at current usage (lower-confidence figure -- verify against 10-Ks before quoting).
- Counter-view (carry it): AEI/Reason argue data centers are a "scapegoat" and that gas prices/weather drive bills more. The *capacity-market* attribution (63%, from the independent monitor) is hard to wave away, but *energy-price* increases are multi-causal. State both; don't oversell single-cause.

## 9. Voluntary corporate pledges -- note, don't over-weight

Anthropic (Feb 2026) committed to pay 100% of its grid-upgrade interconnection costs, bring net-new generation to match load, and use water-efficient cooling. No dollar figures; it's voluntary, not enforceable tariff law. Pledges cannot bind PJM to not pass costs through -- only legislation/tariffs can.
