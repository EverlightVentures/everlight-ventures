Section 1

Getting Started Checklist

Phase 1: Legal (Week 1)

The foundation. Most of this is one afternoon at the county clerk.

✓ RBS Certification Completed
◈ Business license — Yolo County Clerk, ~$50-100/yr
◈ DBA (Doing Business As) — County Clerk ~$40 (only if using a business name different from your legal name)
◈ EIN — Free from IRS (irs.gov, takes 5 minutes online)
◈ Consider LLC — $70 CA filing fee, franchise tax $800/yr but waived year 1
◈ Business bank account — Open after you have your EIN. Keeps personal and business money separate.
◈ MAST Permit (Washington) — Required for WA events. Online, ~$10, few hours. Get it before your first Vancouver booking.

Good news: Since you provide bartending service only (no alcohol sales), you do not need an ABC license, a liquor permit, or any special beverage permits in CA or WA. Your RBS cert (CA) + MAST permit (WA) + a business license is the foundation. That is it. You are already halfway there.

Phase 2: Insurance (When You Can Afford It)

Real talk: Insurance is important but not cheap. You can start booking friends-and-family events and small private parties without it. As you build income, get covered -- some venues will require it. Start with a per-event policy ($100-200 per gig) to keep costs low while you grow.

Your goal: get insured as soon as revenue allows. It's a safety net, not a barrier to starting.

◈ General liability insurance — $1M per occurrence / $2M aggregate
◈ Liquor liability — usually bundled with your GL policy
◈ Per-event options (budget-friendly): FLIP $100-200/event, Thimble, or Next Insurance $300-500/yr
◈ Certificate of Insurance (COI) — a one-page proof you can email to any venue or client on demand

You do NOT need everything on day one. Here is the smart order.

Start Here ($200-400)

Buy these first. This is all you need for your first booking.

◈ Bar tool kit (shaker, jigger, strainer, muddler, bar spoon)
◈ Cutting board + sharp knife
◈ Ice bucket + tongs
◈ Quality cups (clear plastic, 9oz + 16oz)
◈ Napkins, straws
◈ Speed pourers (6-pack)
◈ Bar mat (rubber, keeps things clean)
◈ 6ft folding table + black tablecloth
◈ Large cooler (for ice)
◈ Trash bags

Level Up ($300-600)

Month 2-3. Reinvest from your first few bookings.

◈ Portable bar (folding, with shelf)
◈ LED lights (color-changing strip, battery powered)
◈ Menu sign + easel
◈ Glassware set (coupes, rocks glasses, flutes)
◈ Blender (for frozen drinks)

Go Premium ($500-1,000)

Month 4+. You are established. Time to wow.

◈ Branded signage (logo, banner, neon)
◈ Smoke gun (cocktail presentations)
◈ Large ice molds (spheres, cubes)
◈ Branded apron (embroidered with your name/logo)

Phase 4: Payments & Booking (Week 3)

Keep it simple. You can upgrade tools later as you grow.

◈ Payment: Venmo, Zelle, Square (pick at least two)
◈ Invoicing: Square Invoices or Wave (both free)
◈ E-signatures: HelloSign or DocuSign (for contracts)
◈ Scheduling: Google Calendar or Calendly (free tier works)
◈ Client tracking: Google Sheet — columns: name, event date, package, deposit paid, balance paid, notes

Phase 5: Marketing

You do not need a website on day one. You need visibility.

Instagram (THE channel for bartenders)

Post cocktail videos, event setups, behind-the-scenes prep. Use local hashtags (#WoodlandCA #SacramentoEvents #MobileBartender). Reels of you shaking a cocktail will outperform any ad. This is your portfolio.

Google Business Profile (free)

Gets you on Google Maps. When someone searches "mobile bartender near me," this is how they find you. Add photos, hours, your phone number, and service area.

Thumbtack / The Bash / GigSalad

Event marketplace platforms. You create a profile and people looking for bartenders find you. Great for getting your first 5-10 bookings while your organic marketing builds.

Business Cards

Leave them at every event, on the bar top, with every person you talk to. Vistaprint or Canva — 250 cards for under $20.

Partner with Event Planners & Venues

Reach out to local event planners, wedding coordinators, and venue managers. Offer to be their go-to bartender. One good partnership can fill your calendar.

Section 2

Your First Booking

Step by step. Every booking follows this flow.

1

Inquiry Comes In

Someone texts, DMs, or calls. Respond within 1-2 hours. Speed wins bookings. Even a quick "Hey! I would love to help — let me grab a few details" keeps the lead warm.

2

Qualify the Event

Ask: What is the date? Where is the venue? How many guests? What is your budget? What kind of event (birthday, wedding, corporate, etc.)? Use the Client Questionnaire in Section 3.

3

Send Pricing + Menus

Send your Pricing & Menus page (you already have this). Let them choose a package and customize their menu. Recommend a package based on their guest count and event type.

4

Send the Contract

A simple 1-page agreement: date, time, location, package, total price, deposit amount, cancellation policy. Send via HelloSign or DocuSign for easy e-signatures.

5

Collect 50% Deposit

No deposit, no date hold. This protects your time. Accept Venmo, Zelle, or Square. Mark the date as booked on your calendar immediately.

6

Design Menu + Send Shopping List

Finalize their drink menu. Send a customized shopping list with exact quantities and brand recommendations (use the template in Section 5). Give them 1-2 weeks to shop.

7

Collect Balance 7 Days Before

Send a friendly reminder: "Hey! Just a reminder that the remaining balance of $XXX is due by [date]. Venmo or Zelle works great!" Do not work an event with an unpaid balance.

8

Day Of: Deliver an Amazing Experience

Arrive early. Set up clean. Look sharp. Smile. Make great drinks. Be the energy of the room. Use the Day-Of Timeline in Section 4. Take photos for your Instagram.

9

Follow Up

Send a thank-you text that night or the next morning. Request a Google review 3 days later. Offer a referral discount. Templates for all three are in Section 6. This is how one booking becomes five.

T - 30 minFinal prep. Cut garnish, prep syrups, test a shake, arrange bottles. Take a photo of the setup for Instagram.
T - 15 minTouch up presentation. Verify everything is stocked. Breathe. You are ready.
EVENT STARTFirst drinks flowing. Smile. Greet every guest. Set the energy.
MidwayCheck ice levels. Restock garnish. Wipe down the bar. Swap out trash bags if needed.
T - 30 minLast call announcement. Let the host know. Keep it warm: "Last round coming up, everyone!"
EVENT ENDStop service gracefully. Thank guests as they pass by.
T + 15 minBegin breakdown. Pack tools, wipe surfaces, bag trash, collapse table.
T + 60 minBar area clean. Everything loaded. Thank the host. Leave the space better than you found it. Depart.

Section 11

10 Pro Tips

1. Deposit first, always.

No deposit, no date hold. This is non-negotiable. It protects your time and shows you run a real business. If someone pushes back on a deposit, they are not your client.

2. Photograph everything.

Your bar setup before the first guest arrives. Your best cocktail mid-pour. The crowd having a great time. Instagram is your portfolio. Every event is content.

3. Create YOUR signature drink.

Something with your name on it. Something people talk about the next day. "Have you had Patricia's [drink name]?" That is word-of-mouth marketing that money cannot buy.

4. Leave cards at the bar.

A small stack, fanned out, right next to the napkins. Every guest is a potential booking. Make it effortless for them to remember you.

5. Master 3-5 wow moments.

A flamed orange peel. A dry-ice pour. A perfectly layered shot. A smoke gun reveal. You do not need to be a flair bartender — you just need a few moments that make people pull out their phones.

6. Never overserve.

You have your RBS for a reason. Protecting people is part of the job. Offer water, slow the pace, and know when to cut someone off gracefully. This protects you, the host, and the guest.

7. Over-prepare everything.

Cut more garnish than you think you need. Bring more ice than the math says. Have backup supplies. The one time you run out of limes at a 60-person event is the time you will never forget.

8. Track every dollar.

Income, expenses, mileage ($0.67/mile deduction for 2024). A simple spreadsheet now saves you thousands at tax time. Every tool purchase, every bag of ice, every mile driven to a venue is a write-off.

9. Set the vibe.

You are not just pouring drinks. You are the energy of the room. Greet every guest. Learn names. Compliment the host. Play music if they want it. People hire bartenders for the experience, not just the cocktails.

10. Quality clients only.

Your pricing filters for you. The people who value premium service will pay premium rates and treat you well. Do not race to the bottom on price. You are not the cheapest bartender in town — you are the best experience in town.
