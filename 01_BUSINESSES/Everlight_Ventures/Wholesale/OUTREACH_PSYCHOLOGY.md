  Everlight brand default: confident, respectful, concise

## SECTION 1: PERSUASION PSYCHOLOGY -- PRINCIPLES + PHRASING

### 1.1 Cialdini's Seven Principles Applied to Distressed Homeowners

Cialdini identified six original principles (Reciprocity, Commitment/Consistency,
Social Proof, Authority, Liking, Scarcity) and added a seventh -- Unity -- in his
2016 book "Pre-Suasion." All seven apply differently depending on the seller's
distress type. Source: https://www.influenceatwork.com/7-principles-of-persuasion/

**RECIPROCITY**
Give useful value before asking for anything. The seller feels a natural pull to
reciprocate with their time and attention.
- USE FOR: tax-delinquent leads (give a redemption timeline explanation they may
  not know), probate leads (explain probate simplified vs. full administration).
- PHRASING EXAMPLE: "One thing most people don't realize: in Shelby County, the
  tax sale timeline typically runs [X] months before a lien auction. If you're
  still inside that window, there are paths available that close quickly. I want to
  walk you through them at no cost, no obligation."
- RULE: Give information that is genuinely useful even if they don't sell to you.
  Fake reciprocity (giving useless info framed as help) backfires and feels
  manipulative.
Source: https://nowbam.com/the-art-of-persuasion-in-real-estate-applying-cialdinis-principles/

**COMMITMENT / CONSISTENCY**
People act consistently with prior statements and decisions. Once a seller says
"yes, I've been thinking about selling," they're more likely to follow through.
- USE FOR: follow-up sequences (Round 2+), individual homeowner leads.
- PHRASING EXAMPLE (follow-up): "When we talked last month, you mentioned you'd
  been weighing your options. I just wanted to check back -- has anything shifted
  for you?" This anchors to their own prior words, not your agenda.
- RULE: Never manufacture commitment ("you said you wanted to sell" when they
  didn't). Only reference what they actually said.

**SOCIAL PROOF**
Distressed sellers feel alone and embarrassed. Normalizing their situation reduces
shame and opens the door.
- USE FOR: tax-delinquent leads, absentee owners.
- PHRASING EXAMPLE: "We've worked with several homeowners in [ZIP/NEIGHBORHOOD]
  in similar situations -- people who just needed a clean exit without the hassle
  of listings, showings, and waiting. Most said the relief of having a done date
  was worth more than squeezing the last dollar."
- RULE: Keep social proof anonymous and general. Never name other sellers. "Several
  homeowners in your area" is enough and does not feel invasive.
Source: https://eliteagent.com/the-six-principles-of-persuasion-in-real-estate/

**AUTHORITY**
Sellers trust people who demonstrate market knowledge. Citing real data positions
Everlight as a knowledgeable partner, not an opportunist.
- USE FOR: any lead where the seller may be skeptical of the price.
- PHRASING EXAMPLE: "Based on Shelby County assessor data and recent cash sales
  in [ZIP], properties in this condition and price band are moving at $X-$Y. Here
  is how I got to the number I'm offering you."
- RULE: Show your math. Transparency on method builds authority without arrogance.
  Do not cite authority you do not have ("we're the #1 buyer in Memphis" without proof).
Source: https://www.cognitigence.com/blog/cialdini-7-principles-of-persuasion

**LIKING**
People say yes to people they like. Warmth, genuine interest in their situation,
and shared local context create liking fast.
- USE FOR: probate, elderly long-tenure owners, any situation with emotional weight.
- PHRASING EXAMPLE: "I grew up [near Memphis / in the South] and I know that
  homes carry more than square footage -- they carry stories. I am not here to rush
  you past that."
- RULE: Only use if authentic to the sender persona. Piper can own this naturally.
  Do not force it through a brand-voice sender -- it reads hollow.

**SCARCITY**
Real deadlines create urgency without manipulation IF the deadline is real.
- USE FOR: tax-delinquent (real tax sale date), probate (estate administration
  deadlines), buyers with competing offers (genuine, not manufactured).
- PHRASING EXAMPLE (tax delinquent): "Shelby County's tax sale for properties
  in [TAX_YEAR] delinquency is scheduled for [ACTUAL DATE]. Once that date passes,
  your options narrow significantly. Our offer is open now and we can close before
  that deadline."
- RULE: NEVER fabricate urgency. "We can only hold this offer for 24 hours" is a
  manipulation tactic that damages trust and credibility. Use real scarcity only.
Source: https://cxl.com/blog/cialdinis-principles-persuasion/

**UNITY (Pre-Suasion, 2016)**
We act favorably toward people who are "of our kind" -- same community, same values,
same shared experience. This is the most underused lever in RE investor outreach.
- USE FOR: long-tenure elderly owners (generational community), probate (family
  duty, legacy), inherited vacant lots (family story).
- PHRASING EXAMPLE: "As a local buyer, not a national fund or an algorithm, I care
  about what happens to this neighborhood. I want this property to go to someone
  who will take care of it -- and I want you to walk away with dignity, not just
  a check."
- RULE: Only use when genuinely true. Everlight is local-first and values-led --
  this is authentic. Do not use unity framing if your buyer network is anonymous
  national funds.
Source: https://cialdini.com/

### 1.2 Loss Aversion, Endowment Effect, and Anchoring

**LOSS AVERSION**
The psychological pain of losing $X is roughly 2x the pleasure of gaining $X.
(Kahneman & Tversky; applied to RE in NBER Working Paper w28796.)
In practical terms: sellers resist accepting "less" not because the number is
actually bad but because it feels like a loss relative to a phantom number they
have in their head (what they paid, what they think it's worth, what a neighbor
sold for in 2022).

Key tactical insight from MIT/NBER research: loss-averse sellers anchor to their
original purchase price and hold out even when the market has moved. Your job is
NOT to fight their anchor -- it is to REPLACE it with a better reference point.
Source: https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/77150/Geltner_Loss%20aversion.pdf

Strategy -- REPLACE THE ANCHOR:
1. Do NOT open with your offer number. Open with an explanation of current market
   conditions and carrying costs. Let those data points become the new anchor.
2. Then show what a traditional listing nets AFTER: agent commission (5-6%),
   repairs (average $15-25k for distressed Memphis homes), carrying costs
   (mortgage/taxes/insurance for 90-180 day listing), and uncertainty.
3. Then present your cash offer as the NET comparison, not gross vs. gross.

Framing that works:
- BAD: "I'll pay you $X for your $Y-assessed property." (Immediately anchors loss)
- GOOD: "Most sellers in your situation net $[LOW] after a traditional sale takes
  90 days and $[COST] in repairs. We close in [DAYS] and you net $[OFFER_NET].
  That's the actual comparison."

**ENDOWMENT EFFECT**
Owners overvalue what they own. This is amplified when there is emotional history
(30 years in a home, inherited from a parent). The endowment effect inflates their
internal "what it's worth to me" number well above market.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endowment_effect

Do NOT fight the endowment effect with logic. Acknowledge the emotional value
first, then gently introduce the "exchange mindset."
- PHRASING EXAMPLE: "I understand this home means more to you than a dollar
  amount -- that's clear. What I can offer is a way to honor that history by
  giving you a clean, certain exit so you can move forward on your own terms.
  The number I'm offering reflects the market reality, not the home's meaning
  to your family."

**ANCHORING**
The first number mentioned sets the psychological baseline for everything after.
Anchor HIGH, then walk toward the offer.
Anchor sequence: ARV (after repair value) first -> subtract known discount factors
-> land on offer. The gap between ARV and offer feels smaller when the seller
has seen the deductions build up vs. just receiving the final number cold.
Source: https://oldetownbrokers.com/psychological-factors-in-real-estate-how-understanding-seller-behavior-can-give-buyers-a-competitive-edge/

### 1.3 Distressed Seller Psychology -- Pain Points and Language

Research from PropertyRadar, USLeadList, and RealEstateSkills identifies five
core emotional states driving distressed sellers:
Source: https://www.propertyradar.com/blog/the-complete-guide-to-distressed-properties

1. **SHAME** (tax delinquency, pre-foreclosure)
   They feel they failed at homeownership. They avoid opening mail, avoid the
   phone, avoid the problem. Your outreach must DESTIGMATIZE before making any ask.
   - Language that works: "Getting behind on taxes doesn't mean you made bad
     decisions -- it means life happened."
   - Language that backfires: "We noticed you have unpaid taxes" (accurate but
     feels like an accusation), "before it's too late" (panic-based), any
     mention of their exact delinquent balance (surveillance feeling).
   - NOTE: Research on shaming tax delinquents (ResearchGate 2018) found that
     shame-based messaging INCREASES compliance only for small debts (under $2,500)
     and has no significant effect on larger amounts. For real estate, avoid shame.
     Source: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/328667232_Shaming_tax_delinquents

2. **OVERWHELM** (absentee owners, landlord fatigue, multi-heir probate)
   They have too many decisions to make and not enough bandwidth. They need
   someone to make the path forward simple, not more information-dense.
   - Language that works: "One call. One number. If the numbers work, we handle
     everything else."
   - Language that backfires: Long emails with multiple options, complex
     contingencies, too many questions.

3. **FINANCIAL PRESSURE** (tax delinquent, behind on mortgage)
   They need a specific number and a specific timeline. Vagueness increases anxiety.
   - Language that works: Be specific. "$[X] wired to you at close. [Y] days
     to closing. You pay nothing at settlement."
   - Language that backfires: "competitive offer," "fair price," "as-is value"
     without a number. Vague language signals you're going to low-ball.

4. **FAMILY CONFLICT** (multi-heir probate, divorce, partnership dissolution)
   Multiple decision-makers who may not agree. Framing the sale as a resolution
   to conflict -- not just a transaction -- is powerful.
   - Language that works: "A clean sale removes the disagreement and gives
     everyone a clear share. That's often the fastest path to everyone moving on."

5. **ABSENTEE FATIGUE** (out-of-state owners, accidental landlords)
   Managing a distant property is draining. Every month it sits, it costs money
   and mental energy.
   - Language that works: "Every month that property sits, you're carrying [taxes
     + insurance + risk]. We can close that out."
   - Do the math for them -- give the monthly carrying cost number.
     Source: https://usleadlist.com/resources/tax-delinquent-property-leads

### 1.4 Rapport and Empathy Framing

The line between "we understand" and "creepy/stalkerish" is precise:

COMPASSIONATE (good):
- "We know situations like this can feel overwhelming."
- "A lot of families in [general area] are dealing with similar challenges right now."
- "There's no judgment here -- we just want to understand what would actually help."

PRESUMPTUOUS / CONDESCENDING (bad):
- "We know you're struggling." (You don't. You're assuming and projecting.)
- "We can save you from foreclosure." (Rescue framing is condescending.)
- "I understand how embarrassing this must be." (You made their shame explicit.)

SURVEILLANCE / CREEPY (never):
- "We saw you're $[X] behind on your taxes." (Specific financial data feels like
  reading their mail.)
- "We know you haven't lived there since [YEAR]." (Implies tracking.)
- "We noticed your last mortgage payment was [DATE]." (This is genuinely alarming
  to a recipient.)
Source: https://www.dmrmedia.org/blog/distressed-seller-leads

Empathy structure that converts:
1. Acknowledge the situation exists WITHOUT naming their specific numbers.
2. Normalize it as a common human experience.
3. Position yourself as an option, not a rescuer.
4. Give them an exit ramp (explicit opt-out).

### 1.5 Personalization Psychology -- How Much Is Too Much

Research finding: "Letters that include the homeowner's name, property address,
and specific details see up to 35% higher response rates." (captivateclick.com)

But a 2024 Cisco Privacy Benchmark Study found 80%+ of consumers feel "nervous"
about how companies use their personal data, and nearly half said over-personalization
made them actively distrust a brand. Email open rates dropped 23% as AI-personalization
became widespread.
Source: https://zakelfassi.com/blog/2025/2025-08-20-the-cold-swarm-ai-personalized-pitch-dead-on-arrival

The sweet spot: public-record facts about the PROPERTY plus area context. Personal
facts about the OWNER cross the line.

SAFE PERSONALIZATION (use freely):
- Their name (publicly recorded)
- The property address (public record)
- General lead-type signal ("inherited," "tax situation," "out-of-area owner")
  -- reference the CATEGORY, never the exact data point
- Neighborhood/ZIP area stats (anonymous, non-accusatory)
- Ownership tenure framed as respect ("you've invested X years here")

CREEPY PERSONALIZATION (never use):
- Their other addresses or contact info ("we also found you at...")
- Their exact delinquent balance
- Their mortgage balance or lender
- Their other debts, liens, or judgments
- Information about family members
- Their employment or income
- How many times they've been contacted (implies database tracking)
Source: https://partnerwithez.com/blog/distressed-property-sellers/

### 1.6 Negotiation Psychology -- Handling Counteroffers

When a seller counters above our number, the goal is to validate their position
emotionally while holding our math.

**Feel-Felt-Found pattern (sales training classic, widely documented):**
"I understand how you feel [validate]. Many sellers I've worked with felt the
same way at first [normalize]. What they found is that after running the numbers
including repairs, timeline, and carrying costs, the cash path actually put more
in their pocket [redirect to math]."

**Ackerman method adapted for acquisitions:**
Start at 85% of your close target. Move in decreasing increments (each concession
smaller than the last). Each increment should come with a reason, not be free:
"I went back to my numbers and I can move to $X if we can agree on [closing date /
you take the appliances / we handle the survey]."
Source: https://luxlifemiamiblog.com/mastering-real-estate-negotiation-as-a-buyer-in-a-softening-miami-real-estate-market/

**Silence as a tool:**
After presenting a counter, stop writing. The research is consistent: in written
negotiation, the first person to fill the silence loses leverage.
Source: https://www.fortunebuilders.com/p/the-psychology-of-real-estate-negotiation/

**Emotional labeling (Chris Voss technique):**
If a seller is frustrated: "It seems like the uncertainty around timeline is
really important to you." This is not an offer -- it is an invitation to talk.
It creates trust and opens information.

**When to stand firm:**
After 4 rounds (established in rex_master_pipeline.py), hold the number and
reframe around certainty: "I've done the math three ways and this is where my
numbers land. I know it's not your number, but I want to be straight with you
rather than pretend I have more room that I don't. The offer is real and it's
here whenever you're ready."

### 2.1 Shelby County Official Sources (free, no auth required)

**Shelby County Assessor -- Property Search**
URL: https://www.assessormelvinburgess.com/propertySearch
Data: Assessed value, last sale date, last sale price, owner name and mailing
address, parcel number, property class, square footage.
USE: Derive tenure (today - last_sale_date = years held), assessed value for
our number anchoring, mailing vs. property address to flag absentee.
Source: https://www.pubrecord.org/shelby-county-property-records-tennessee/

**Shelby County Register of Deeds -- GIS Search**
URL: https://gis.register.shelby.tn.us/
Data: Deed history, instrument type (quitclaim, warranty, transfer on death),
last recorded deed date, grantor/grantee.
USE: Detect family/quitclaim transfers (probate signal), absentee mailing
address, last sale instrument type (quitclaim = family transfer = likely distress).
Source: https://www.shelbycountytn.gov/1514/FileSearch-Property-Documents

**TNMap TN Property Viewer**
URL: https://tnmap.tn.gov/assessment/ (statewide, includes Shelby County)
Data: Parcel boundaries, ownership, assessment data.
USE: Cross-check assessor data, fill gaps.

**Shelby County Tax Records (delinquency)**
URL: https://www.shelbycountytn.gov/3410/Public-Records
Data: Tax delinquency status, year, amount (when publicly available through
annual delinquent tax lists).
USE: Confirm lead_type = tax_delinquent and approximate timeline.

**Why tenure matters psychologically:**
A 30-year owner has deep endowment effect. A 2-year owner has less emotional
attachment and is more transactional. Tenure also implies equity: longer tenure
usually means more equity and more room for a deal, and more pain in carrying
costs per month. Frame differently:
- Short tenure (under 5 yrs): "you're not that far from even on this" -- appeal
  to moving on cleanly.
- Long tenure (15+ yrs): appeal to legacy, relief, and certainty. They have
  options the market doesn't give young owners.

### 2.3 How Distress Signals Map to Narrative

| Distress Signal | What It Tells You | How to Frame It |
|---|---|---|
| Tax delinquent 1 year | Financial pressure, possibly income shock | "Tax situation" -- timeline urgency, solution framing |
| Tax delinquent 2+ years | Deep financial pressure or willful neglect/absentee | Add scarcity (tax sale timeline) |
| Absentee mailing | Property not lived in, distant management burden | "Managing from a distance" fatigue frame |
| Quitclaim transfer | Family/estate transfer, often distressed | Probate/inherited burden frame |
| Probate case | Estate administration, heir coordination | Relief, simplicity, family resolution frame |
| Vacant/no utility | No occupant, carrying cost with no return | Cost-per-month math, opportunity cost |
| Teardown signal | Physical deterioration, high repair cost | "As-is" cash offer advantage, no repair ask |
| Long tenure + delinquent | High equity, emotional attachment + financial stress | Highest leverage: they have equity to unlock |

## SECTION 3: THE NON-CREEPY LINE -- EXPLICIT RULES

### What to reference freely (public record + area stats):
- Owner's name (from public deed record)
- Property address (public record)
- That you are a local cash buyer and came across the property
- General lead category ("I work with homeowners navigating inherited properties")
  -- NEVER the specific instrument (e.g., "I saw the quitclaim deed recorded on...")
- The property's Shelby County assessed value (public record)
- Neighborhood / ZIP area stats (anonymous market data, not specific to their lot)
- Ownership tenure framed as respect ("someone who has invested many years here")
- That you buy properties in their area / neighborhood (general geographic context)

### What to NEVER reference (even if you have it):
- Their exact tax delinquent balance ("you owe $X in back taxes")
- Their mortgage balance or lender
- Other liens, judgments, or debts
- The specific case number for their delinquency, foreclosure, or probate
  (referencing a case number in an email feels like a legal notice, not a human)
- Their other properties, assets, or financial situation
- Any data about their family members, heirs, or co-owners by name
- How many prior outreach attempts you have made to them
- Any indication you have been watching / monitoring the property
- Their contact info sources ("we found your email through skip-trace")
- Any behavioral data (how often they check the mailbox, etc.)

The RULE OF THUMB: if the homeowner could read your email and think "how did they
know that?" -- do not include it. If they could read it and think "that's just
normal local knowledge" -- you are safe.

Research basis: 80%+ of consumers feel nervous about how companies use personal
data; awareness of surveillance mechanisms "shatters the illusion of relevance,
replacing it with discomfort." (Cisco Privacy Benchmark Study 2024; zakelfassi.com)
