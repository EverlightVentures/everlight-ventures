Section OneExecutive summary

Everlight Ventures runs an autonomous Tennessee real estate wholesale pipeline. We source distressed property leads from the Shelby County Assessor's public records, vet each against an institutional cash-buyer's purchase criteria, send a personalized pre-call email under a state-routed agent identity, negotiate price across three structured rounds via an automated arc engine, lock the deal with a cryptographically-signed contract package executed under the federal E-Sign Act, and disburse all funds through a RESPA-compliant title firm. Every event is hash-chained in an immutable audit ledger. The end-to-end cycle is 14 days. The infrastructure is self-hosted, runs on commodity hardware, costs zero in third-party SaaS subscriptions, and has been rebuilt on existing internal repositories instead of paid alternatives like DocuSign, Plaid, or Apollo.

Section TwoThe four parties + how everyone wins

Wholesale is a 4-sided market: the distressed-property owner who wants out, the cash investor who wants the property, us as the assignment broker, and the title firm that handles the closing escrow. The deal only closes when each side gets value greater than their walk-away alternative. Here is what each party is paying for and what they walk away with.

Seller

The owner of the distressed property

  • $9,500 cash at closing (typical TN tax-delinquent SFR)
  • No back-tax check — we cover it at the title firm
  • No agent fees, no listing, no showings
  • 14 days from contract to wired funds
  • RESPA-protected closing through licensed title agency
Buyer / Assignee

The cash investor (Mid South Homebuyers / Chris)

  • Property at 55-60% of county appraisal
  • Standard wholesale margin after rehab + carry
  • No back-tax burden (we absorb)
  • Repeat-buyer relationship: same title firm, same paperwork, same pace
  • Deal flow: 17+ properties/mo at scale
Assignor

Everlight Ventures (us)

  • $3,500 net per deal (assignment fee minus title concession)
  • $250 EMD recovered at close
  • 14-day cycle, 100% pass-through to operator's K-1
  • Repeatable per address — daily 100-lead intake
  • Brand premium: Marquise voice, branded contracts, audit chain
Closing agent

Mid-South Title (Memphis, TN)

  • $677 in closing fees per deal (closing + title insurance + recording)
  • Escrow income on funds in transit
  • RESPA-clean documented closings
  • Volume relationship — we route every TN deal here
The win-win in one number
Total deal value created per closing: $13,090. Distribution: $9,500 to seller, $3,500 to us, $1,800 to county trustee (back tax), $677 to title firm, $250 EMD refund cycle. Every party leaves with cash they didn't have before, on a 14-day schedule, with paperwork that holds in court.

Section ThreeA-to-Z process flow (16 stages)

Each stage names the target audience, the role each party plays, what the operator does (almost nothing — most stages are autonomous), and the specific backend infrastructure that fires.

1
Lead intake (Day 0, 8:00 AM PT)
▶ target: TN address spreadsheet▶ role: autonomous

The daily orchestrator wakes via phone-side cron, reads the next 100 unscraped TN addresses from tn_addresses.csv, checks the Resend daily budget (must have ≥ 100 sends remaining in the bulk category), and queues each address for scraping.

Touches: 03_AUTOMATION_CORE/01_Scripts/daily_lead_pipeline.py (orchestrator) ◆ Wholesale/lead_sources/tn_addresses.csv (input) ◆ content_tools/resend_budget.py (gate)
Caps: 100 addresses/day max, 2,200/month at default Resend cap
2
Shelby Assessor scrape (Day 0, 8:00–8:08 AM)
▶ target: assessormelvinburgess.com▶ role: autonomous

For each address, the scraper hits /propertySearch, parses the parcel ID, then hits /propertyDetails?parcelid={id}, saves the response HTML to owner_downloads/inbox/{parcel}.html. Rate-limited at 5 seconds per request (well under the assessor's tolerance). 100 addresses takes ~8 minutes.

Touches: shelby_assessor_scraper.py (NEW wrapper) ◆ wholesale_property_scraper.py (existing scraper foundation, reused) ◆ owner_downloads/inbox/ (HTML saved here)
Source URL: https://www.assessormelvinburgess.com/propertyDetails?parcelid={id}
3
Parse + extract owner data
▶ target: assessor HTML files▶ role: autonomous

The existing parse_assessor_mhtml.py parser (which we already used to produce 32 parsed records) extracts the structured data: owner name, mailing address, year built, beds/baths, lot size, total appraisal, sales history (deed types, dates, prices), tax-sale markers (TS2202 flag = 4+ years delinquent). Outputs JSON to owner_downloads/parsed/{parcel}.json.

Touches: parse_assessor_mhtml.py --live (existing parser, +1 flag for live HTML) ◆ owner_downloads/parsed/ (32 records already there from manual phase)
Schema: 28 fields per parcel (owner, mailing, beds, baths, sqft, year_built, appraisal, sales_history, etc.)
4
Vet against buyer's buy-box (Chris's filter)
▶ target: parsed JSON▶ role: autonomous

Each parcel JSON runs through lead_vetting.matches_chris(intel): city == MEMPHIS, ZIP in Chris's 15-zip target list, property class residential or vacant lot, appraisal in $5K-$80K range, tax-delinquency status known. Pass → continues to next stage. Fail → logs reason, moves on. Typical pass rate: 70-80% (the input list is already pre-filtered TN addresses).

Touches: lead_vetting.py (NEW, ~80 LOC) ◆ Wholesale/config/chris_buy_box.json (criteria source) ◆ deal_execution_log.sqlite (logs vetting result)
Filter logic: 5 criteria, all must match. Fail-fast.
5
OSINT deep-dive on owner
▶ target: parsed parcel + owner name▶ role: autonomous

The existing seller_intel_deepdive.py (the same script that produced the Cipher batch) enriches each passing parcel: detects out-of-state mailing (high motivation), long-term ownership (20+ years), multi-deed history (investor pattern), heir patterns (estate signals). Writes seller_intel/{parcel}/intel.json + intel.md. These signals feed the personalized pitch hook.

Touches: seller_intel_deepdive.py (existing, 14-priority deep-dive proven on Cipher batch) ◆ Wholesale/seller_intel/{parcel}/ (output) ◆ osint_api/investigators/property_records.py
Signals captured: ownership_years, tax_delinquent_years, multiple_sales_in_history, absentee_owner, estate_indicator, llc_owner
6
Generate personalized M1 pitch (cold intro)
▶ target: seller (e.g. Mikal Hakeem)▶ role: autonomous, voiced as Marquise Reed

Pitch tailoring picks the strongest signal-driven hook from pitch_tailor.tailor_for_seller(intel). For an investor pattern owner, the hook reads "Pulled the deed history on the parcel and the pattern reads like portfolio activity, three zero-dollar QC entries plus nine priced sales." The pitch is rendered in Marquise Reed's actual firmware voice (loaded from .claude/agents/marquise_reed_acquisitions.md) — Memphis cadence, "y'all," soft southern dialect, no corporate AI tells.

Touches: osint_api/pitch_tailor.py (NEW, signal → hook) ◆ osint_api/voice_extractor.py (loads agent firmware) ◆ osint_api/arc_send.py:m1_intro() ◆ content_tools/branded_mailer.py (CAN-SPAM-compliant Resend wrapper)
Compliance gates fired: phrase_scrub (banned: "list", "commission", "your agent"), DNC list, TN cold-email rules, branded gold template
7
Send M1 via state-routed agent alias
▶ target: seller's verified email▶ role: branded send via Marquise alias

Send goes from marquise@everlightventures.io (TN-routed agent alias) — Rich's name never appears on first-touch outbound. Marquise alias forwards inbound replies via ImprovMX to Rich's Gmail. Branded mailer wraps the body in the gold-on-dark template, applies the per-state legal disclaimer for TN, and decrements the Resend daily counter. The send produces a Resend message_id which is logged to the immutable audit chain as event email_sent.

Touches: content_tools/branded_mailer.send_branded_email() ◆ content_tools/resend_guard.py (blocks owner/internal addresses) ◆ content_tools/resend_budget.py (decrements 100/day) ◆ Wholesale/audit/deal_execution_log.py:log_event()
Audit row written: {event:"email_sent", actor:"Marquise Reed", artifact_ref:"resend:<msg_id>", row_hash:<sha256>, prev_hash:<previous_row_sha>}
8
Inbound reply detection (any time, autonomous)
▶ target: Gmail inbox via IMAP▶ role: autonomous, every 2 minutes

The phone-side IMAP poller (phone_imap_poller.py, runs via cron every 2 min during business hours 8 AM–9 PM PT) reads new mail. For each new message, the deal-arc router checks if the sender matches a known counterparty in any active deal_meta. If yes, it classifies the reply (accept / counter / question / stop / neutral) and looks up the deal's last stage from the audit log.

Touches: 03_AUTOMATION_CORE/01_Scripts/phone_imap_poller.py (existing 386-line layer-1 poller) ◆ compliance/inbound_watch_daemon.py (existing 944-line Oracle daemon, available when Oracle returns) ◆ arc_send.classify_reply() + arc_send.next_step()
Cron line: */2 8-21 * * * python3 phone_imap_poller.py --once
9
M3: Round 1 opening offer (low anchor)
▶ target: seller (after they ask for a number)▶ role: autonomous, Marquise voice

Marquise opens at $6,500 cash on a $23,800-appraisal property. Frame: "this is opening, not last." The intentional low anchor sets the negotiating range and gives the seller a counter to come back at. Per the doctrine, the seller's first counter is expected — the system is built for it.

Touches: arc_send.m_negotiate_seller(round_n=1) ◆ Logs email_sent with amount_usd=6500 + notes="M3 negotiation round 1 offer $6,500"
10
M5: Round 2 negotiated meet
▶ target: seller (after their counter)▶ role: autonomous, Marquise voice

Seller counters (typically $10K-$15K). Marquise meets at the midpoint between opening and final target — for the reference deal, that's $8,000. Frame: "stretching to meet you." If the seller accepts, jump to contract package (M7). If they counter again, advance to round 3 (M7_final).

Touches: arc_send.m_negotiate_seller(round_n=2, counter_amount=<extracted>) ◆ Counter amount auto-extracted from inbound body via regex \$\s*[\d,]+
11
M7: Round 3 final offer (last pass)
▶ target: seller (if still negotiating)▶ role: autonomous, Marquise voice

Marquise sends the final round at our target ($9,500). Frame: "last pass, at the wall." If accepted, contract package fires. If the seller still counters, the system escalates to Rich via escalation_to_operator event in the audit log — wall-hit means human override needed.

Touches: arc_send.m_negotiate_seller(round_n=3) ◆ arc_send.should_escalate() writes Slack alert if seller still counters
12
M7_contract: Auto-render + deliver 3-document package
▶ target: seller, esign-form ready▶ role: autonomous

On seller's "yes": the contract renderer (contract_renderer.py) auto-fills 3 HTMLs from deal_meta.json + global title-firm config — PSA, Schedule A (TN SB 909 wholesaler disclosure), EMD wire acknowledgment. Each gets a token-gated signing URL via the e-sign service on port 2302. Marquise sends the package: three click-to-sign links.

Touches: osint_api/contract_renderer.py (NEW, 6-doc renderer from deal_meta) ◆ osint_api/esign_server.make_token() (HMAC-signed JWT-style token, 7-day TTL) ◆ 09_DASHBOARD/reports/deals/{key}/{01_PSA,02_Schedule_A,03_EMD}.html ◆ arc_send.m7_contract()
13
Seller signs (UETA + E-SIGN Act compliant)
▶ target: seller, on phone or desktop browser▶ role: seller action — single click + sign

Seller opens the signing URL → sees the contract preview in an iframe (loaded via token-gated /doc/{token}/{filename} route — no other party can view) → draws their signature on an HTML5 canvas (or types name) → checks the legally-required intent box → submits. Server captures: typed name, signature PNG, IP, user agent, timestamp, document SHA-256, signature SHA-256. Generates a PDF signature certificate. Burns the signature image into a signed copy of the contract (01_PSA_signed.html) so the executed file shows the signature on the document itself, not just in a separate ledger. Emails signer + operator the cert.

Touches: osint_api/esign_server.submit_signature() ◆ osint_api/signature_burner.py (NEW, embeds drawn PNG into signed_html) ◆ osint_api/pdf_certificate.py (reportlab PDF cert) ◆ 09_DASHBOARD/reports/deals/{key}/01_PSA_signed.html ◆ 09_DASHBOARD/reports/deals/{key}/signatures/01_PSA_*.pdf
Audit rows: sig_received, cert_generated, signed_copy_generated, doc_viewed
Statute citations: 15 U.S.C. § 7001 (federal E-SIGN Act), Tenn. Code Ann. § 47-10-101 et seq. (TN UETA)
14
Wire EMD to Mid-South Title escrow
▶ target: Mid-South Title IOLTA account▶ role: operator initiates from bank app

Within 3 business days of signed PSA, Rich initiates a $250 wire from his existing bank to Mid-South Title's escrow. Operator opens /wire/{deal_key} on the e-sign server, selects "EMD" wire type, enters bank confirmation number + amount, optionally uploads the bank screenshot. The wire confirmation file is SHA-256-pinned and the event is appended to the immutable audit chain. EMD funds are held by the title firm in IOLTA escrow — never touch operator's accounts (advance-fee statute compliance).

Touches: osint_api/esign_server.submit_wire() (POST /wire/{deal_key}) ◆ 09_DASHBOARD/reports/deals/{key}/wires/{wire_type}_{conf_num}_{ts}.{pdf|png}
Audit row: {event:"wire_sent", artifact_ref:"wire:EMD:<conf>", artifact_sha256:<file_hash>}
15
Buyer arc: C1 → C3 → C5 → C7 (parallel to seller, Hammer voice)
▶ target: cash buyer (Chris @ Mid South Homebuyers)▶ role: autonomous, Hammer Knox alias

The moment the seller signs PSA, the C-arc fires. Premium-anchor doctrine: total spread opening-to-final is $300-500 max, NOT the wide $1,400+ spread used on the seller side. Chris is institutional capital and respects sellers who don't fold. Round 1 (C1) opens at $13,500 (just $410 above target). Round 2 (C3) walks to $13,300. Round 3 (C5) lands at $13,100. If Chris pushes the standard 50/50 title-fees ask, Hammer takes it (~$300 absorbed) and frames as "standard between us, no fight." Anything over $500 ask is escalated to Rich.

Touches: arc_send.c_negotiate_buyer() ◆ arc_send._buyer_round_offer() with max_buyer_spread=500 cap ◆ arc_send.c_assignment() sends Assignment Agreement esign URL
Audit rows: {event:"email_sent", notes:"C1 buyer round 1 offer $13,500"} etc.
16
Closing (Day 14): Wires release + deed records
▶ target: Mid-South Title closing▶ role: title firm executes per signed contracts

T-1 day: Hammer sends Chris the settlement statement preview (auto-rendered 05_Settlement_Statement_Preview.html with line-by-line money flow). Chris reviews. T+0 (closing): Mid-South Title receives Chris's balance wire, disburses: $9,500 to Mikal (seller), $3,500 to Everlight (assignment fee + EMD refund), $1,800 to Shelby County Trustee (back tax), $677 to Mid-South Title (closing fees). Deed records to Mid South Homebuyers, LLC. Done. Operator logs final wire confirmation + closing certificate.

Touches: 09_DASHBOARD/reports/deals/{key}/05_Settlement_Statement_Preview.html ◆ esign_server.submit_wire() for each closing-day wire (BUYER_BALANCE, ASSIGNOR_PAYOFF, BACK_TAX) ◆ arc_send for post-close confirmation emails to all parties
Audit rows: wire_received (×3-4), email_sent (post-close summary). Final verify_chain() confirms tamper-resistant integrity.

★ Tax math clarity (answer to "am I eating that cost?")

Section SixNegotiation doctrine

The negotiation arc is not a script — it's an asymmetric anchoring strategy informed by the psychology of each counterparty.

Seller side (Marquise → distressed property owner)

  • Anchor LOW. Open at 27% of appraisal ($6,500 on $23,800 appraisal).
  • Wide spread expected. Walk $3,000 from open to final ($6,500 → $9,500). Sellers are emotionally attached to one property and need to feel they "won the bidding."
  • Three rounds maximum. M3 (open) → M5 (meet) → M7 (final). Each round's tone changes: opening is "honest start," meet is "stretching to you," final is "at the wall."
  • Question handler does not advance the round counter. If seller asks "what's the inspection period?", Marquise answers without burning a negotiation round — buys time, builds trust.
  • Wall-hit at M7 escalates to Rich via Slack. The system never auto-keeps-walking past round 3.

Buyer side (Hammer → Chris at Mid South Homebuyers)

Premium-anchor doctrine (locked 2026-05-13)
Total spread opening-to-final on the buyer side is $300-500 max, NEVER $1,000+. Chris is institutional capital with repeat-deal flow — big concessions train him to ask for bigger ones next time. Small spreads protect brand premium and per-deal margin.
  • Anchor TIGHT. Round 1 (C1) opens at $13,500 — just $410 above the $13,090 target.
  • Narrow walks. Round 2 (C3) at $13,300, round 3 (C5) at $13,100. Total spread $400.
  • Title 50/50 push: take it ONLY if it absorbs ≤ $300. Anything more, push back.
  • Multi-deal package leverage. If Chris is taking 4+ deals, allow $400-500 per deal in flexibility — but only as a package term, not per-deal.
  • Anything over $500 single-deal concession ask escalates to Rich.

Concession scripts (Hammer, in order of preference)

  1. Standard ($300): "Done. Half on title, my side absorbs $300. Standard between us, no fight."
  2. Package ($400, multi-deal only): "I can do $400 off on this one because we're packaging four together. Wouldn't do it as a one-off."
  3. Escalate (any single ask > $500): "That's outside what I can authorize on a single deal. Let me loop Rich in if you want the package terms changed."

Cryptographic chain-of-custody

Every event in the system writes a row to deal_execution.sqlite. Each row's row_hash is computed as SHA256(prev_row.row_hash + JSON-canonical(this_row)). Tampering with any prior row breaks the chain on the next verify_chain() run. This is the same pattern used by certificate transparency logs and blockchain pre-image proofs.

Court-defensibility test
For any disputed signature: the audit log proves who signed, when, from what IP, with what intent box checked, against what document SHA-256, with what signature SHA-256. The signed PDF certificate generated by reportlab is independently auditable. The hash-chained ledger is independently verifiable. This is the same level of evidentiary support as a DocuSign envelope, achieved with self-hosted code.

Section EightWhy this scales

The architecture has two scaling axes: more leads per state (vertical) and more states (horizontal). Both are unblocked by the design choices we already made.

Vertical scale (per-state, more leads)

  • Resend cap is the hard ceiling at 100/day default. Resend Pro plan ($20/mo) lifts this to 5,000/mo (~227/day).
  • Shelby Assessor scrape capacity at 5s/req = 720 properties/hour. We're nowhere near throttling at 100/day.
  • Negotiation arcs run in parallel. Each deal has its own state in the audit log. 100 simultaneous negotiations fit comfortably on a single phone.
  • Settlement statement + contract rendering is per-deal. No shared bottleneck.

Horizontal scale (multi-state expansion)

  • state_gates.json already encodes per-state legal rules (TX, CA, NC, IL hard blocks; OH, AZ, IN soft constraints).
  • STATE_TO_CLOSER in marketing_pipeline.py maps every U.S. state to a closer agent (Marquise/Piper/Rex/Justine).
  • Per-state scrapers are the only state-specific code. Adding Knox County (Knoxville) or Davidson County (Nashville) means writing a new {county}_assessor_scraper.py wrapper. Everything downstream is identical.
  • Statutory disclosures are per-state. Schedule A for TN is SB 909; equivalent disclosures exist in OH (HB 132), TX (SB 1577), etc. Each state gets its own template.

Multi-LOB expansion (within entity structure)

The Nevada LLC + TN subsidiary structure (memo: LLC_NEVADA_FORMATION_2026-05-13.md) anticipates the wholesale LOB sitting alongside Onyx POS, HiveMind SaaS, future gambling, and other ventures — each as a separate disregarded sub or NV Series cell.
