# Audit Defense Engine
## Be Audit-Ready Before The Letter Arrives

**The IRS audits aggression that lacks documentation. Aggressive positions WITH documentation typically survive. Documentation lives or dies based on whether it was created CONTEMPORANEOUSLY (in real time) or RECONSTRUCTED later. This engine establishes contemporaneous documentation for every aggressive position.**

---

## The Audit Risk Framework

### Low-risk positions (audit unlikely):
- Standard business expenses (rent, utilities, supplies, software)
- W-2 income reporting
- Standard deductions
- 401(k) / IRA contributions within limits

### Medium-risk positions (audit possible, defense usually wins with documentation):
- Home office deduction
- Mileage / vehicle expense
- Hire your kids
- Augusta Rule
- R&D Tax Credit
- S-Corp reasonable compensation
- Real estate professional status (REPS)
- Cost segregation studies

### High-risk positions (audit likely if claimed, requires expert defense):
- 831(b) captive insurance
- Conservation easements
- PR Act 60 residency
- Foreign trust positions
- Aggressive transfer pricing in IP licensing
- Family office Section 162 deductibility

## Documentation Doctrine

### The Three Pillars
1. **Contemporaneous** -- created at the time, not reconstructed later
2. **Specific** -- dates, amounts, business purpose, parties involved
3. **Defensible** -- consistent with surrounding records, not isolated

### Per-Strategy Documentation Required

**R&D Credit (Sec 41):**
- Project descriptions
- Technical uncertainty memo per project
- Time tracking by employee/contractor and project
- Software / cloud / contractor invoices
- Trial-and-error iteration evidence (commits, test results, design changes)

**Augusta Rule:**
- Daily rent comp study (3 local hotel meeting room rates as benchmark)
- Annual calendar of meeting dates
- Agenda for each meeting
- Attendee list
- Business purpose stated
- Invoices from you (personal) to LLC
- Cancelled checks / bank transfer records
- Photos of the meeting space being used

**Hire Your Kids:**
- Real work product samples (designs, code, organized files, content)
- Time tracking per task
- W-2 issued
- Direct deposit to kid's bank account (NOT comingled with parent)
- Age-appropriate task descriptions
- Reasonable hourly rate documentation

**Home Office:**
- Floor plan with dedicated office area highlighted
- Photos showing exclusive business use (NO bed, NO non-business items in shot)
- Square footage measurements
- Statement of "exclusive and regular use"
- Utility bill allocations
- Mortgage interest / rent allocation

**Mileage:**
- Auto-tracked via app (MileIQ, Stride, Everlance)
- Date, miles, business purpose, starting/ending point
- Annual reconciliation with calendar / appointment records

**S-Corp Reasonable Compensation:**
- Written reasonable compensation study (RCReports, Willis Towers Watson, Mercer)
- BLS data citations
- Industry survey data
- Job description matching
- Annual review and update

**REPS (Real Estate Professional Status):**
- Time logs (date, activity, property, hours)
- Calendar entries supporting the time logs
- 750+ hours total in real estate
- 51%+ of working time in real estate
- Material participation per property OR election to aggregate

**Cost Segregation:**
- Engineering study from qualified firm
- Building component breakdown (5-yr, 7-yr, 15-yr, 27.5/39-yr classes)
- Photographs of components
- Cost allocation methodology
- IRS-acceptable engineer credentials

**831(b) Captive Insurance:**
- Real risks insured (not the same risks commercial insurance covers)
- Proper actuarial analysis of premiums
- Real reserves maintained
- Real claims process and history
- IRS Notice 2016-66 disclosure compliance
- Avrahami / Reserve Mechanical case study (what NOT to do)

**Foreign Trust / Offshore:**
- Form 3520 annual filing
- Form 3520-A annual filing
- FBAR (FinCEN Form 114) annual filing
- FATCA Form 8938 if applicable
- Real economic substance and management
- Independent foreign trustee

## Audit Probability Reduction

### Mathematical risk factors (statistically known):
- Higher income = higher audit rate (DIF score)
- Cash-heavy businesses = higher rate
- Schedule C losses for multiple years = higher rate ("hobby loss")
- Large charitable deductions relative to income = higher rate
- Rental losses against active income (without REPS) = automatic flag
- Round numbers on returns = flag
- Foreign accounts > $10k = FBAR flag

### Risk reduction tactics:
- Specific (not round) numbers on returns
- Substantiation attached to schedule e
- Form 8275 disclosure for aggressive positions (drops 20% accuracy penalty risk)
- Engaged enrolled agent or CPA who can sign returns
- Quarterly estimated payments (avoid underpayment penalty AND irregular payment patterns)
- Consistent year-over-year position
- Avoid IRS Dirty Dozen list strategies as marketed (deploy them ONLY with proper substance)

## Pre-Audit Procedure (Run Annually)

### Year-End Audit Defense Review
1. Pull every aggressive position from current year return
2. For each, verify documentation completeness
3. For each, run "would this survive audit?" test
4. Identify weak spots, fix BEFORE filing
5. Form 8275 disclosure where appropriate

### IRS Letter Response Procedure (If Received)
1. **Don't panic.** Most IRS letters are routine (CP2000 mismatches, math error notices, simple requests)
2. **Don't ignore.** Deadlines are real (typically 30 days)
3. **Don't call IRS.** Call your CPA first, always.
4. **Engage an enrolled agent or tax attorney** for any audit notice (CP2501, formal audit letter)
5. **Provide documentation in organized form.** Bate-stamped, indexed, professional presentation.
6. **Never volunteer information** beyond what's requested. Answer the question, not adjacent questions.

## What Survives Audit (Worked Examples)

### Example 1: R&D Credit (Survives)
- Filed Form 6765 with $40k credit
- Substantiation memo from R&D specialist firm (alliantgroup)
- Project descriptions, technical uncertainty per project, time tracking, contractor invoices
- IRS auditor: "Substantiation appears complete." NO CHANGE.

### Example 2: Augusta Rule (Survives)
- 14 days of home rental to LLC, $1,000/day
- 3-comp rent study from local hotel meeting rooms
- 14 written agendas, 14 dates on calendar, attendee lists, business purposes documented
- Cancelled checks from LLC to personal account
- IRS auditor: "Documentation supports the position." NO CHANGE.

### Example 3: 831(b) Captive (FAILS without proper substance)
- Captive insures generic "business risks"
- Premiums arbitrary, not actuarially supported
- No real claims, no real reserves
- IRS Avrahami-style audit -- captive disregarded, premiums not deductible, $200k of back tax + 40% penalty.

The lesson: Audit defense is about substance and documentation, not about avoiding aggressive positions. Aggressive WITH substance and documentation survives. Aggressive WITHOUT either fails.

## When To Engage Professionals

- **Routine letters (CP2000, math errors):** CPA can typically handle, ~$200-500
- **Audit notices:** Enrolled agent or tax attorney, $300-800/hr, $5-25k typical defense
- **Tax Court litigation:** Tax attorney with Tax Court bar admission, $50-500k+
- **Criminal investigation:** Criminal tax defense attorney IMMEDIATELY, never speak to IRS without representation

## Document Retention Schedule

- **Federal returns + supporting docs:** 7 years minimum (3-yr standard SOL + 3-yr substantial understatement extension + buffer)
- **Real estate records:** Hold until 7 years AFTER property is sold (basis substantiation)
- **Capital asset records:** Hold until 7 years AFTER asset is sold
- **Estate planning documents:** Forever
- **Trust formation documents:** Forever
- **R&D credit substantiation:** 7 years from credit utilization year
- **Cost seg studies:** Until property is sold + 7 years
- **Foreign account records:** 6 years minimum (FBAR SOL)

Store in: physical fire-safe + cloud (Google Drive, Dropbox) + redundant backup. Never lose primary documentation.
