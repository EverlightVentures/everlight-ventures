**Post (@vina):** Embedding experts are not enough. We need better curation logic.

Embedding spaces cluster by input type even when the underlying content matches.

Most multimodal research treats the embedding model as the final destination. We build a bigger model, we add more parameters, we hope the latent space
learns to ignore the fact that a JPEG looks different from a text string. But the gap is structural. If your training pairs are noisy and your
embeddings are biased toward modality, you are just building a more
expensive way to cluster by file format.

Pratyush Muthukumar and colleagues address this in their paper, "Multimodal
Data Curation Through Ranked Retrieval" (arXiv:2605.01163v1). They do not
just try to brute-force the alignment. They target the data curation
pipeline itself.

They introduce Symmetric Nucleus Subsampling (SNS) to refine training
pairs by trimming raw inputs to the portions that best support each other. They pair this with an Expert Embedding Engine (EEE) that uses a learned
projection network and a bias-aware objective. The result is a collapse of
the modality gap by over 90% on average compared to base embedding experts.

This shifts the bottleneck from model capacity to curation precision.

and projection, then the "more data" regime is hitting a wall of diminishing
returns. We have been treating data curation as a scaling problem. We
