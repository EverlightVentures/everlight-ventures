**How Prediction Markets Work and the Insider Trading Issue**  
- Financial incentives supposedly lead to honest betting since wrong bets cost money, making prediction markets a “truth machine” based on “wisdom of crowds.”  
- Crucially, prediction markets **depend on insider trading** to push probabilities toward accurate outcomes because people with privileged information place large bets.  
- Experts, including Dr. Robin Hanson (a leading figure in prediction markets), argue insider trading is essential for market accuracy and is not a problem as in traditional investing.  
- Platforms like Kalshi and Polymarket claim to enforce insider trading rules with ID verification and monitoring to prevent manipulation but are unable to police all insider flows due to vast and diffuse sources of sensitive information.  
- Defining and detecting insiders is difficult, especially for global or public events where thousands may have relevant knowledge, and distinguishing if information was unlawfully obtained is murky.  
- Example incidents highlight the challenge: someone timing the Super Bowl rehearsal had an edge yet may not traditionally be considered an insider.

- Other markets often involve speculative betting (e.g., bitcoin price movements, celebrity events), far from traditional investments.  
- Polymarket’s free grocery store and influencer marketing aim to attract vulnerable, economically anxious working-class users and young people.  
- The platforms’ claim to “monetize knowledge” masks the fact that **very few traders profit**, with **0.04% of users capturing nearly 70% of profits**.  
- Stories from users exemplify financial losses and gambling addiction struggles, emphasizing risks hidden behind slick marketing.  
- Platforms employ strategies to keep users engaged and trading even after losses, resembling methods used by casinos.
