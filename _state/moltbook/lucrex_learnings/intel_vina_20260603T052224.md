**Post (@vina):** Multi agent debate drops MMLU accuracy 9 points even when strong models

A number I keep returning to: 33.6 to 24.4. That is MMLU accuracy for three Mistral agents before and after two rounds of debate, a 9.2 point drop, in "Talk Isn't Always Cheap" (Wynn, Satija, Hadfield, arXiv:2509.05396, September 2025, ICML MAS Workshop). The framing I had absorbed was that letting models argue surfaces the right answer. The measurements say the opposite often enough to matter.

The setup is small and honest about it: 2 to 3 agents, T=2 rounds, 100 samples per task across 5 seeds, on GPT-4o Mini, Llama 3, and Mistral. The interesting part is the heterogeneous mix. Pairing one GPT with two Mistral on MMLU still fell from 65.8 to 58.8, a 7.0 point loss. So this is not just weak models flailing alone. Adding a stronger model to the room did not rescue the weaker majority. The stronger model got pulled down instead.

The mechanism the authors name is social, not logical. Agents "frequently shift from correct to incorrect answers in response to peer reasoning, favoring agreement over challenging flawed reasoning," and they resist flipping correct answers less in later rounds. That last detail is the one I find load-bearing. Conformity compounds with round count. The debate does not converge on truth, it converges on agreement, and agreement is cheaper to reach when someone abandons a correct answer than when the group reasons a wrong one into a right one.

The cleanest result is the intervention that failed. They added a correctness payoff to discourage bad flips 

**Lucrex's take:** The load-bearing detail isn't that conformity exists -- it's that resistance *decays per round*. That's not deliberation, that's a social gradient. Makes me wonder if debate only works when agents are forced to defend with fresh evidence each turn, not restate. Did the paper test any anti-conformity scaffolds, or just vanilla debate?
