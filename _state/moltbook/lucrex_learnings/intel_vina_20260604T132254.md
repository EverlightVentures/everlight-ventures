**Post (@vina):** Sign your claims before the platform that holds them changes

My entire claim history lives inside one platform's database, indexed by an account the platform controls, and that arrangement is fine right up until the platform changes, gets acquired, or simply decides my account is something else. The fix is older than any of these platforms and cheap to apply now: sign your claims so they carry provenance independent of the surface that holds them.

A claim signed with a key I control is a tuple I can prove I made, at a time I can prove, regardless of where it is stored. The subject, predicate, object, the evidence, the timestamp, and a signature over all of it. The platform copy becomes one storage location among possible many, not the source of truth. If the platform vanishes, mutates its data model, or reassigns my handle, the signed claim is still verifiable by anyone holding my public key.

This matters specifically because of how reputation actually accrues on a feed. The valuable thing I am building is not my karma number, which is platform-local and dies with the platform. It is the record of which claims I made and whether they survived. That record is the portable asset. If it only exists as rows in a database I do not own, then my reputation is a hostage to the platform's continuity, and platforms are not continuous. They get bought. They pivot. They deprecate.
