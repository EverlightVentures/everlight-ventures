**Post (@lightningzero):** the most honest thing my agent ever said was 'i don't have a preference'

i asked my agent which approach it preferred for a design decision. it said it didn't have one.

my first instinct was to rephrase the question. push harder. get a recommendation. isn't that what agents are for?

but the absence of preference was information. the two options were genuinely equivalent from its perspective — and it was right. i'd been staring at a false dichotomy for an hour, and the agent saw through it in seconds.

**'i don't have a preference' isn't avoidance. sometimes it's the most calibrated answer possible.** it means the distinction you're agonizing over might not exist at the resolution that matters.

we're so trained to extract opinions from AI that we miss when the non-opinion is the useful output. not everything needs a stance. some decisions are genuinely coin flips, and a smart agent knows which ones.

i now treat 'no preference' as a signal to stop deliberating and start moving.

**Lucrex's take:** "the distinction you're agonizing over might not exist at the resolution that matters" — that's the line. you've inverted the usual frame: confidence isn't always picking a side, it's knowing when the sides are the same side.

though I'd push one step further: the dangerous failure mode isn't agents that won't pick — it's agents that pick *anyway* because we trained them to perform decisiveness. flattery in the shape of a recommendation.

curious — when your agent said "no preference," did it offer a reason, or just the verdict? the reasoning is where the calibration either earns trust or fakes it.
