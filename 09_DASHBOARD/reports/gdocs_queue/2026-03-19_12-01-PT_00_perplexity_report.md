**Pacific Time (PT) is currently Pacific Daylight Time (PDT), UTC-7, observed across western North America including full coverage in California and Washington, and partial in Oregon, Idaho, Nevada, and Alaska (e.g., Hyder).** [1][2][3][5]

On March 19, 2026, PT operates as **PDT** (UTC-7), with standard **PST** (UTC-8) applying outside daylight saving periods.[1][3][5][6] Daylight saving time began March 8, 2026, at 2:00 AM (PST to PDT, +1 hour), and ends November 1, 2026, at 2:00 AM (PDT to PST, -1 hour).[5][7][8]

### Coverage and States
- **Fully in PT**: California, Washington.[2][4][6]
- **Partially in PT**: Alaska (Hyder unofficially), Idaho, Nevada, Oregon (rest Mountain Time).[1][2][4]
- Arizona observes Mountain Time year-round, no DST, creating 1-2 hour differences from PT.[1]
- Extends to southwestern British Columbia (Canada) and northwestern Mexico (Zona Noroeste).[1][4][6]

### Recent Developments
British Columbia announced in March 2026 it would adopt year-round daylight saving time (UTC-7) independently of U.S. changes, enhancing synchronization with PT for trade and tech.[6] No U.S. federal changes noted; DST schedule stable per Energy Policy Act.[5][6]

### Expert Analysis
Temporal geographer Elena Marquez notes PT's 2,200-mile span creates coordination challenges in logistics.[1] Urban planner James Tran highlights intra-zone variations (e.g., Oregon vs. eastern Washington) impacting commutes and business, emphasizing real-time alignment needs.[1]

Sources dated 2026 confirm ongoing observance; no major disruptions reported.[2][3][5]
