## NATIONAL TOTALS
- **~5,000 U.S. data centers** (NTIA; ~4,300-5,400 depending on definition).
- Top states: **Virginia ~665, Texas ~413, California ~321** (rest of U.S. ~3,600).
- Power: **176 TWh (2023) = 4.4% of U.S. electricity -> 325-580 TWh (2028) = 6.7-12%** (LBNL).
- Policy split: a restriction wave (300+ bills/30 states in early 2026, 78 local moratoria) vs. states still courting aggressively (IA, GA, TX, NE).

---

## VIRGINIA -- the global epicenter
- Loudoun County "Data Center Alley" (Ashburn): 30+ million sq ft, world's densest cluster. ~665 centers statewide.
- **Energy is the strain:** the official JLARC study (Dec 2024) modeled statewide demand **tripling** by 2040; would need ~a new power plant every 1.5 years. Residential cost could rise **$14-37/month by 2040.**
- Water is comparatively mild here (JLARC: "about the same as a large office building").
- FLAGGED: "Dominion 40 GW pipeline" unconfirmed (CEO said "several GW"); "70% of internet traffic" is **debunked** (~22%).
- Policy: a bill proposes halting new site applications until July 2028.

## TEXAS -- deregulated grid, drought, the Stargate flagship
- ~413 centers (2nd nationally), ~170 more planned; could exceed 40 GW by 2028 (~30% of U.S.).
- ERCOT grid may double from ~85 GW (2024) to **up to 218 GW by 2031.**
- **Water (verified, HARC):** ~49B gallons (2025) -> **~399B by 2030** (~6.6% of all Texas water). No state law caps it. (Chart 05.)
- Abilene "Stargate" (OpenAI/Oracle): >1 GW; uses closed-loop cooling (vendor-claimed minimal water -- experts skeptical).

## GEORGIA -- the sharpest residential WATER harm
- Metro-Atlanta boom; Georgia Power got unanimous PSC approval (Dec 2025) for ~10 GW new capacity (~80% for data centers), **$50-60 billion** over the assets' life.
- **Newton County (Meta):** ~500,000 gal/day (~10% of county use); nearby residents report **dry, sediment-fouled wells**, $5,000 spent, $25,000 well replacement; Mansfield water rates set to rise **33%.**
- Tax breaks ballooned toward $2.5B; 2025 rollback bills failed. A 2026 Senate bill weighs a 1-year ban starting July 2026.

## ARIZONA -- the desert-water paradox
- Phoenix-metro hub (Microsoft, Google, Meta, CyrusOne). Total AZ data-center water ~905M gal/yr (<0.1% of state use) -- because many use **zero/low-water cooling** (CyrusOne's 8 Chandler sites use no water for cooling). But Meta's Goodyear site used ~56M gal of potable water/yr.
- **Tucson rejected "Project Blue"** (~$3.6B, Aug 2025) over water; **Chandler rejected** a center (2025). Gov. Hobbs halted some groundwater-dependent construction.
- Policy: HB 2756 would stop data-center grid-connection costs from shifting to other ratepayers (cost-causation).

## OHIO -- the BEST precedent (copy this)
- Columbus/New Albany cluster (Google, Meta, AWS, Intel).
- **The precedent:** effective July 9 2025, regulators approved an AEP Ohio tariff requiring data centers with peak load >=25 MW to **pay for at least 85% of contracted capacity** even if they use less -- so households don't subsidize them. **Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft + the Ohio Manufacturers' Association challenged it as discriminatory; regulators denied the appeal and upheld it.** This is the leading U.S. model for shifting cost burden onto the demand-creator.

## OREGON (The Dalles) -- the transparency landmark
- The city sued its own newspaper (The Oregonian) to keep Google's water use secret as a "trade secret," then **settled (2022)** and disclosed: Google used **274.5 million gallons in 2021 -- ~a quarter of the entire city's water.**
- Why it matters: established nationally that **data-center water use is public record, not trade secret.**

## IOWA -- clean-energy magnet, growing water strain
- ~104 centers (~76 in Des Moines metro); Apple, Google, Meta, Microsoft. Council Bluffs Google campus ~$5.5B.
- Meta (Altoona) uses up to **~16% of the city's water supply**; Microsoft (West Des Moines) ~2-7%.
- ~$150M/yr in tax exemptions. Still courting aggressively.

## NEBRASKA -- coal kept alive by demand
- Omaha-area centers (Meta in Papillion). Meta's NE center alone used nearly as much energy as Omaha's coal plant produced in 2023; a proposed Google site would need **more power than all of Lincoln.**
- Data-center demand is **keeping coal plants online**, conflicting with climate/health goals.

## THE KEY PATTERN
Water is a **local** story (driven by cooling type + whether the source is a stressed aquifer/drought basin) -- mild in Virginia, catastrophic in Newton County GA. Energy is the **uniform national** strain. The policy fight is splitting into two camps: states forcing data centers to pay their own way (OH, AZ, VA proposals) vs. states still giving away tax base and cheap power (IA, GA, TX, NE).

**Everlight footprint overlap:** your wholesale states TX, GA, OH, AZ are all in the thick of this. Utility-rate and water-stress shifts in those markets are a real macro signal for property values and buyer sentiment.
