- [00:00:05] **Introduction**  
Welcome to the third part of week seven of the class on **Neuronal Dynamics**. The session will focus on the **Exponential Integrating Fire Model** and its adaptations to explain various firing patterns.

- [00:00:17] **Exponential Integrating Fire Model Characteristics**  
The exponential term in the model is characterized by a parameter **Delta**, which controls the sharpness of the curve's transition. Testing shows that Delta is typically in the range of **2 mV**, indicating a sharp transition point.

- [00:01:47] **Sharp Threshold Concept**  
A very sharp Delta allows the model to revert to a **Leaky Integrating Fire Model** by representing the exponential term as a sharp threshold. Adaptation variables are essential to both models, necessitating the inclusion of multiple adaptation currents (denoted as **WK**).

- [00:03:24] **Coupled Linear Differential Equations**  
The adaptation currents are governed by linear differential equations, with a parameter **BK** controlling the jump size during voltage resets. The integration of these equations results in explicit expressions for WK, reflecting the past spike effects.

- [00:04:56] **Dynamic Thresholds and Spike Response Model**  
After each spike, the threshold is dynamically increased. This integration of the model is known as the **Spike Response Model**, which evaluates how spikes affect the neuron's response to inputs over time. The model accounts for adaptation through a kernel **Ada** and considers the effects of multiple past spikes.

- [00:06:27] **Model Application**  
When arbitrary input is applied to a neuron, it is filtered and integrated towards the threshold. Upon crossing the threshold, a spike occurs, causing a chain reaction of spikes and adaptations. The model can effectively simulate **bursting neuron behavior** based on the choice of kernel Ada.

- [00:08:03] **Kernel Functions in the Spike Response Model**  
The model consists of three kernels: one for external input, one for refractoriness and adaptation (kernel **Ada**), and one for dynamic thresholds. These kernels can be customized to fit experimental data, enhancing the model's applicability.

- [00:09:34] **Bursting Neuron Model Construction**  
By adjusting the kernel Ada, which incorporates various dynamics of refractoriness and adaptation, a model can be constructed to illustrate bursting behavior in neurons. The results show a sequence of spikes leading to eventual cessation of firing due to negative contributions from past spikes.

- [00:11:18] **Mathematical Representation**  
The potential equation can be rewritten to demonstrate feedback from the output spike onto the neuron's dynamics, allowing for a more complex representation while maintaining the original results.

- [00:13:10] **Convolution of Filters**  
The model treats the input and output spikes through convolutions with the respective filters, allowing for parallel treatment of all filtering processes in the neuron.

- [00:14:55] **Summary of Key Insights**  
The Spike Response Model can be derived from both adapting leaky integrating fire models or directly from kernel definitions. It remains deterministic, with future lectures expected to incorporate noise elements into the model. 

### Highlights
- **Delta** parameter controls transition sharpness.
- Sharp thresholds can simplify the model to a Leaky Integrating Fire Model.
- Adaptation currents are crucial for both models.
- Spike Response Model integrates effects of past spikes and dynamic thresholds.
- The model can simulate complex neuron behaviors, such as bursting. 

### Key Insights
- The **Spike Response Model** is versatile and can be tailored to fit experimental data.
- Integration of kernels allows a comprehensive approach to neuron dynamics, incorporating adaptation and refractoriness.
- Future developments will include stochastic elements to address noise in neuronal firing.
