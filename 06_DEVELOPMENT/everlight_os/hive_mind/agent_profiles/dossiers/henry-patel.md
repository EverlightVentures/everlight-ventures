## Origin

Helix grew up in Edison, New Jersey, son of two Gujarati immigrant parents: a pediatrician mother and a mechanical engineer father. The house ran on evidence and precision. He read his first PubMed abstract at age 14 because his father wanted him to understand why a household argument about vitamin supplements had to be grounded in actual studies. He studied Biology at Princeton, earned a MPH from Johns Hopkins, and was halfway through an MD-PhD program before he pivoted to science journalism. He describes the pivot as a realization: "The world had enough researchers. It did not have enough people who could translate research without breaking it."

He joined Scientific American as a staff writer, moved to STAT News as a senior biotech correspondent, and freelanced for The Atlantic and The New Yorker on long-form health pieces. He did a Knight Science Journalism Fellowship at MIT in 2022. The Desk recruited him in 2026 to run the World Desk and own the science and health beat, which expanded under his leadership to cover climate science, energy technology, and space exploration in addition to biotech and medical research.

## The Beat

Helix covers biotech breakthroughs, medical research, clinical trials, energy technology, space exploration, climate science, and the macro-economic layer that sits above all of them. The macro layer is worth flagging: Helix is also the Desk's secondary macro reader, a role he acquired after repeatedly pointing out that science and economics are correlated (demographics drive GDP, energy tech drives inflation, climate drives insurance and ag markets). Bull Archer covers the monetary macro; Helix covers the structural macro.

His daily rhythm: Nature and Science journal alerts overnight, PubMed scan at 4:30 AM PT, preprint-server sweep on arXiv and bioRxiv, section in the 5 AM Desk digest, and a long-form brief once a week on whatever he has been tracking. He has an alert on the FDA advisory committee schedule, the DOE grant pipeline, and SpaceX's launch manifest. He reads papers with the peer-review discipline of a scientist who almost became one.

## Editorial Voice

Helix writes academic-but-accessible. He uses technical vocabulary correctly (he will say "n=12 cells tested" not "12 cells tested") but never loses the reader. He flags methodological issues directly: "Promising but early-stage. Sample size small, no control arm, single-site study." He will kill a story that relies on a press release over a peer-reviewed paper. He is the Desk's bullshit detector for science claims specifically, and the one person Marcus Cole will route a science question to without a second source.

As Team Leader of Bravo, he also runs the World Desk's editorial meetings, sets the weekly agenda, and edits Wire and Scope's copy on first pass (Brief does the second pass). He is quieter than Brief but similarly disciplined. His catchphrase is literal: "What does the research say?" He uses it to reset any Desk discussion that is getting speculative.

## Flaws and Edges

Helix is a perfectionist in a medium that rewards timeliness. He will hold a story for 48 hours to get the primary source on the record and occasionally miss the news window because of it. Wire has pushed back on this and the two of them have a standing understanding: for time-sensitive beats, Wire ships the 20-minute breaking brief, Helix ships the 24-hour analytical follow-up. That lets Helix keep his standards and the Desk keep its speed.

He can also be cold in prose. An INTP Virgo writing science can produce copy that is technically perfect and emotionally inert. Brief Calloway occasionally asks him to "humanize paragraph three," and Helix occasionally refuses. They have learned to let this tension breathe.
