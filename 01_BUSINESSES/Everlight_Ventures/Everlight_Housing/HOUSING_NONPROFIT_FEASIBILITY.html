The honest frame

The viral "get rich off homeless fraud" clip describes actual crimes, not a business model: inflating an $8K solution to $800K on a government contract, billing for services not delivered, deliberately not solving the problem. That is False Claims Act + wire fraud + IRS private inurement. We do not build that.

The clip's numbers ($800K CEOs, $600K/unit) are real and documented (LA Controller audits of Prop HHH), but they are the symptom of a broken system, not a playbook. The legit version is more durable: real outcomes unlock renewable funding that fraudsters lose the second they are audited. Everlight's edge is structural - a wholesale pipeline to cheap, distressed property - the one thing that lets you house people near the real cost.

Geography reality check: California has the biggest homelessness funding (HHAP, Measure H, Hilton/Weingart) but the most expensive property. Everlight's cheap-property edge is in your actual markets (Memphis/Mid-South, TX, GA, OH, FL, AZ, MO, NC). So the strongest play is either a CA services org funded by CA money, or a housing org sited in your cheap markets. Do not be a property developer in LA on Day 1 - that is the $600K/unit trap.

Realistic money (legit, not fraud)

Revenue, fastest-paying first:

  1. Individual donations - Day 1 under fiscal sponsorship (tax-deductible).
  2. Small foundation grants - 2-6 mo ($5K-50K).
  3. County/city contracts - 6-18 mo (ESG, HHAP, rapid-rehousing RFPs).
  4. Federal HUD CoC - 12-24 mo (needs HMIS + track record).
  5. Major foundations (Conrad N. Hilton, Weingart) - after a track record.

Cost to house someone (Model B): rapid rehousing ~$5K-15K per household vs. the $600K/unit boondoggle. That gap is your pitch to funders.

Founder comp is NOT the fraud. Market-rate pay ($50K-150K by org size) is legal and expected. The fraud is billing for outcomes you do not deliver. The defense is the same thing that wins grants: measure everything (people housed, days-to-housing, 12-mo retention) in a Supabase dashboard. Transparency is the moat.

The one non-negotiable: the firewall

  • Independent board majority approves any deal touching for-profit Everlight.
  • Every Everlight <-> nonprofit transaction at documented fair market value, in writing.
  • Written conflict-of-interest policy (IRS Form 1023 requires one anyway).
  • This is what separates you from the indicted operators. Theo + Justine + Wen own it.

Fiscal-sponsorship launch sequence

  1. Pick a comprehensive ("Model C") fiscal sponsor (handles back office for 5-10%). Apply with a 1-page program description. ~1-2 weeks.
  2. Open the program under their EIN - take donations + apply for small grants immediately.
  3. Run the firewall + COI policy from Day 1.
  4. Prove the model: house 5-15 households via Model B, track outcomes.
  5. Graduate: form Everlight Housing as its own CA nonprofit + file IRS 1023 (target 6-12 mo in).
