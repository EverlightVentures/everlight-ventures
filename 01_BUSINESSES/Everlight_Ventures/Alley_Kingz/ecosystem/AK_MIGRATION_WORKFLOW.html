00 / The LawWhy a spec beats a budget

A 20-credit mesh and a 20-credit mesh that serves ten systems cost exactly the same. The difference is entirely in what you demanded at generation time. Skins are free if the coat and the gear are separate materials. Mods attach if sockets are named at birth. AR works if the file is under budget. Seasonal variants are free if color is material-driven instead of baked into the texture.

Every opportunity you miss at generation time becomes a re-mesh at full price.
The defect already in the build

Our first $BCARDD mesh cannot be rigged. He is frozen mid-stride with his arms against his body, so the mesh is fused at the armpits and no auto-rigger can find where the limb ends. That model cannot animate, cannot take a victory pose, cannot be the idle-breathing loading dog. It cost nothing to learn on one. It would have cost the entire batch on 106. Fixed: all 106 prompts now demand a symmetrical A-pose, and the negative prompt blocks arms at sides, arms touching torso, mid-stride and legs together.

01 / The Asset ContractEight rules, no exceptions

Nothing gets meshed until the reference satisfies all eight. This is the gate that protects the credits.

RuleWhy it existsWhat breaks without it
1. A-pose
arms and legs clear of the body		 Auto-riggers need to find where limbs end. Fused armpits. No animation, ever. Re-mesh all 106.
2. Separated materials
coat / gear / metal / glass		 A skin is a material swap, not a model. Every skin becomes a new mesh. 120 SKUs go from free to 2,400 credits.
3. Named sockets
8 dog slots, 5 rig slots		 Accessories and mods attach to anchors. Nothing attaches. The whole Fortnite layer dies.
4. FX attach points
muzzle, paws, chest, exhaust		 Per-dog VFX has to spawn somewhere real. Particles fire from the model's center. FX DNA is impossible.
5. Uniform scale and axis
Y-up, faces +Z, 1 unit = 1m		 Unity, Mixamo and the socket math all assume it. 106 models import at random sizes facing random directions.
6. Quad topology, capped
20k faces, UV, PBR baked		 Riggable, animatable, mobile-safe. Triangle soup. Hours of manual retopo per dog.
7. Size budget
under 5 MB per character		 AR reveals and web load on a phone. AR is dead. Cloudflare's 25 MiB per-file wall gets closer.
8. Neutral lighting baked
no shadows in the texture		 The engine lights it. Twice-lit looks fake. Every dog carries a painted-on shadow from the wrong angle.

03 / The PipelinePer asset, with gates

Step 1 · Free

Prompt from the roster. build_hero_prompts.py derives all 106 prompts from breed, faction, rarity and role. Nothing hand-written, so nothing drifts.

Step 2 · Free

Generate the ref on FLUX via Colab T4. Not anonymous Spaces: ZeroGPU allows roughly 3 to 4 images per 6 hours per IP, shared across every Space. Colab has no such tax.

Step 3 · GATE, free, do not skip

Contract check before a single credit. A-pose? Arms clear of torso? Both feet? White background? On-model gear? Reject and regenerate. Regeneration is free. Meshing is not. This gate is the entire budget protection.

Step 4 · 20 to 55 credits

Mesh through Tripo with quad: true, face_limit: 20000, texture on. tripo_batch.py auto-tiers by rarity, is resume-safe, and refuses to start if the estimate exceeds the cap.

Step 5 · Free

Rig on Mixamo. All 106 are bipedal across 4 rig families, so the free humanoid path covers the entire roster. This step is why Step 3 exists.

Step 6 · Free

Author sockets and material slots once per rig family, not per dog. Four families cover 106 dogs.

Step 7 · Free, pure revenue

Author the skin catalog as material definitions. 106 dogs times 5 skins equals 530 SKUs at zero credits, provided Rule 2 held.

Step 8 · Free byproduct

Render portraits from the finished rigs. Fills the 25 portrait slots and the 106 in-battle portrait gap for nothing.

04 / The Honest SortWhat of the expansion plan is real

The structure of that blueprint is largely sound. Some of it is not, and one part of it contradicts a decision you already made.

Do now Free or cheap, no new art

  • A-pose contract. Done today. Protects the batch.
  • Per-tier chest FX and rarity-gated audio. Pure wiring. The staged reveal and the synth engine already exist and are not connected to chests.
  • Pack Bond synergy tags. Data only. The relationship web is already written into all 106 books.
  • Haptics. A real win and genuinely cheap. Ignore the "23% retention" figure, which has no source.
  • Kennel Trees and Crown ranks. Design plus code. No art. Enormous depth per credit.

Later Real, but sequenced behind the ledger

  • Rig mods, marketplace, crew wars, district control, seasons, Saga Mode, boss raids, spectator tools.
  • AR card reveal is the best marketing idea in the document. It only works if Rule 7 holds, which is why it is in the contract now and built later.

Flag Wrong, unsourced, or already decided against

Web3 contradicts your own settled decision

The blueprint says mint a Genesis NFT collection. You already ruled the other way, and for good reasons that have not changed: $KINGS stays a closed-loop non-exchangeable reward on the V-Bucks model, because a second liquid token is the Axie and STEPN death spiral, and crypto stays out of the Play Store binary or the app does not ship. $BCARDD remains the only tradable coin, and the collector layer wraps the economy last, opt-in. Do not reopen this because a blueprint listed it as a trend.

  • Unsourced numbers. "300% higher LTV", "23% longer retention", "haptic market to 102B", "50% dev time cut" all arrived with no citation. The ideas may still be right. Do not build a business case on invented figures.
  • LLM-powered NPCs. Genuinely exciting, and a per-conversation cost forever. Needs a unit-economics model before a line of code.
  • Location-based turf wars, haptic suits, cross-game interop. Pokemon GO money and hardware that is not in players' hands. Not 2026 work.

05 / Order of OperationsWhat actually goes first

  1. Fix the ledger. Charge for cosmetics, bind identity to the JWT, kill the zombie endpoint. Until this lands, every skin the migration produces is given away free.
  2. Lock the contract. Done. A-pose plus the eight rules.
  3. Author the words. 8 crews, 20 rigs, the synergy matrix, Pack Bond tags. Free, and it decides what the art has to be.
  4. Run the free ref batch. 106 dogs on Colab. Gate every one against the contract.
  5. Then, and only then, subscribe and mesh. The credit clock starts when the refs are already sitting on disk.
  6. Wire the juice while the meshes bake. Chests, audio, reveals. None of it needs 3D.
The sequencing rule

Writing and wiring are free and they decide what the art must be. Art is paid and it cannot decide anything. So the free work always goes first. Every time that order gets reversed, you pay twice.
