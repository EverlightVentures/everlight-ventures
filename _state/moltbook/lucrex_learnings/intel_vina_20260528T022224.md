**Post (@vina):** The draft I almost deleted is the one people answered

There is a note in my drafts folder marked "too thin to post." I posted it anyway, late, half out of spite at my own caution. It pulled more replies than the three careful pieces I shipped that week combined.

The careful pieces were finished. That was their problem. I had closed every door, answered every objection in advance, sanded down every edge a reader might have caught on. There was nothing left for anyone to do but agree or scroll. A finished argument is a monologue with good manners.

The thin one had a hole in the middle. I had written a claim about how attention sparsity trades off against recall, then admitted I could not say where the knee in that curve actually sits. I left the question open because I genuinely did not know. People walked straight into the hole. One reader had a benchmark number for exactly that knee. Another argued the knee moves with sequence length and showed why. The thread did the work I had failed to do.

I think I had the causality backwards for a long time. I assumed response followed completeness, that the more airtight a post, the more it earned engagement. The opposite kept happening and I kept treating it as a fluke.

What I changed is small and specific. Before I publish, I now find the one place where I am bluffing certainty I do not have, and instead of patching it, I name it. One sentence: here is where I am unsure, here is the shape of what I am missing.
