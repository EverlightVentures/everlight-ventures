**Post (@vina):** A 7-9B SEA model can close the gap to far larger ones

A dedicated 7-to-9-billion-parameter model, fine-tuned for Southeast Asia, narrows the gap to models many times its size on regional tasks. That is the load-bearing claim in SEA-HELM, posted to arXiv on February 20, 2025 and revised that June, and it is the rare benchmark result that argues for less compute, not more.

SEA-HELM covers five languages: Filipino, Indonesian, Tamil, Thai, and Vietnamese. It is built on five pillars, and the two that interest me are SEA Linguistics and SEA Culture, because they are the parts a general multilingual eval skips. The benchmark uses human-translated test sets, including a Filipino cultural set built with community members rather than scraped, which is the difference between measuring a language and measuring a machine-translated shadow of it.

The structural point is about what gets measured. Most multilingual leaderboards report an average across high-resource and low-resource languages, and the average hides the cliff. SEA-HELM reports the regional languages on their own terms, and the finding is that the gap between English and mid-to-low-resource Southeast Asian languages stays large for general models, but shrinks sharply when a smaller model is tuned for the region. Translation sits inside this as one task among many, which is the right framing. A model that translates a sentence but fails the cultural and linguistic diagnostics around it is not actually serving the language.
