- [00:00:00] **Summary of Electric Fish Electro Sensory Encoding**
  - The speaker discusses the perspective of an electric fish using electro senses to navigate and find food.
  - Two potential sequences of events are described:
    - **Sequence One:** Finding food immediately after leaving home (two events, one time interval).
    - **Sequence Two:** Encountering a landmark before finding food (three events, two time intervals).
  - To encode this sequence information, neurons in the **posterior gyrus (PG)** are utilized.
  - Neuronal responses are characterized by bursts of spikes, with the intensity determined by the time elapsed between encounters.
  - An analysis indicates that a **homogeneous population of neurons** is optimal for encoding situations with fewer events, raising questions about the observed heterogeneous distribution of adaptive parameters in real fish.

- [00:02:30] **Exploration of Heterogeneous Delays in Neural Networks**
  - **Speaker:** Penguay Son from Imperial College London.
  - Focuses on utilizing imprecise weights and heterogeneous axon delays in low-bit neural networks.
  - The experimentation demonstrates that using three states (positive, negative, and zero weights) with imprecise delays leads to high computational efficiency.
  - Key findings:
    - Increasing delay bits improves performance significantly.
    - Long delays are crucial for maintaining long-term information.
    - Short-term delays are less critical as they can be compensated by weights.
  - The study concludes that **temporal heterogeneity** is vital for efficient computation when task-relevant information is temporal.

- [00:04:51] **Neuromodulation in Spiking Neural Networks**
  - **Speaker:** Abdell Kad Alani from Imperial College London.
  - Introduces a **modulatory neural network** that dynamically adjusts parameters during runtime to enhance spiking network functionality.
  - Performance analysis indicates that networks with modulation outperform static counterparts, particularly under noisy conditions.
  - The study emphasizes the importance of modulation rate, which is adjustable based on task requirements.

- [00:08:37] **Information Training in Adaptive Spiking Networks**
  - **Speaker:** John Intriago from Universidad Chile.
  - Investigates how adaptive spiking networks can be optimized for classification tasks.
  - The aim is to align input spaces with hidden layers through an information bottleneck principle, enhancing convergence speed compared to baseline models.

- [00:11:21] **Training Spiking Neural Networks with Asynchrony**
  - **Speaker:** Afroditi Talidul from the University of Calgary.
  - Proposes that asynchronous dynamics are sufficient for training complex temporal patterns in spiking networks.
  - Focuses on ring network topologies, showing that inhibitory connections can lead to successful trajectory training without requiring additional complex dynamics.

- [00:14:13] **Robotic Motion Using Spiking Neural Networks**
  - **Speaker:** Gabrielle Dor from Universidades.
  - Discusses creating a robot that mimics walking using a **leaky integrate-and-fire central pattern generator**.
  - The network can switch between different movement patterns based on dopamine modulation, demonstrating local control influenced by global inhibition.

- [00:16:50] **Multi-Purpose Sensory Representation in the Brain**
  - **Speaker:** Julia Gigak from FMI Basel.
  - Explores how single sensory inputs can fulfill multiple computational tasks, potentially managed by excitatory-inhibitory assemblies.
  - Highlights the need for a unified representation to efficiently handle various sensory demands.

- [00:19:40] **Energy-Based Models for Neural Circuit Modeling**
  - **Speaker:** Alas Far from Shahid Beheshti University.
  - Discusses the limitations of token-based models for neural signals and presents **energy-based models (EBMs)** as an alternative.
  - EBMs assign energy values to states, seeking configurations that minimize energy, thus modeling complex continuous dynamics.
  - The importance of biological structures in neural computation is highlighted, suggesting a potential integration of AI mechanisms with neuroscience principles.
