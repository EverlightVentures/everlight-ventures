# Due Diligence Period -- The Walk-Away Clause

**Purpose:** Marquise asked "no inspection? that's fine but I need a quality assurance period in case we need to legally back out." This is the standard real-estate term for that: **Due Diligence Period (DD).**

The DD clause gives BUYER (us) the unilateral right to terminate the PSA for ANY reason during a defined window, with full EMD return. It's our exit ramp.

---

## Standard DD period by property type

| Property type | DD length | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Vacant lot, low-value | 7 days | Title check + buyer-search only |
| Vacant lot, higher-value (>$25k) | 10 days | Add survey check |
| SFR, no rehab | 14 days | Title + condition + buyer-search |
| SFR, needs rehab | 21 days | Add contractor walkthrough |
| Multi-family | 30 days | Add tenant-status review |

**Default for our PSA boilerplate:** **14 days** (covers vacant + most SFR).

---

## The clause itself (drop into PSA, after signature line)

```
4. DUE DILIGENCE PERIOD.

Buyer shall have a Due Diligence Period of fourteen (14) calendar days from
the Effective Date of this Agreement (the "DD Period") during which Buyer,
at Buyer's expense, may inspect, investigate, and review any matter
relating to the Property, including but not limited to:

  (a) title status, exceptions, and any clouds on title;
  (b) survey, boundary, and access matters;
  (c) physical and environmental condition of the Property;
  (d) zoning, land use, and code enforcement;
  (e) any liens, judgments, or encumbrances of record;
  (f) Buyer's confirmation of an end-buyer or assignee for assignment of
      this Agreement;
  (g) any other matter at Buyer's sole discretion.

During the DD Period, Buyer may terminate this Agreement by providing
written notice to Seller (email is sufficient), with a full refund of
the Earnest Money Deposit to Buyer, no questions asked, and neither party
shall have further obligation to the other except as expressly stated in
this Agreement.

If Buyer does not deliver written termination notice prior to the
expiration of the DD Period, the Agreement shall continue in full force
and Buyer's right to terminate under this Section 4 shall expire.
```

---

## Why item (f) -- "buyer confirmation of an end-buyer" -- matters most for us

Item (f) is the wholesale-specific kill switch. It says: **if we can't find Chris (or a backup) to take the assignment within 14 days, we walk and get our EMD back.**

Without (f), we're locked into closing the deal ourselves -- which would require us to come up with the FULL purchase price (e.g., $1,800) plus all closing costs (~$680) plus title fees -- and we don't have $2,500 lying around. (f) gives us the contractual ability to walk if no buyer materializes.

This is why the DD clause is non-negotiable for sole-prop wholesalers. It's the difference between "professional wholesaler" and "personally on the hook."

---

## When seller pushes back on 14 days

Some sellers say "why 14 days? Sounds like you're not serious."

**Hammer's response:**
> "It's standard real estate due diligence -- exists in 99% of contracts on the market. It just covers title issues, survey, that kind of thing. We've never used it to back out without cause. If anything, it protects you too: when DD expires, we're locked in on our end and can't bail."

**True statement:** 14 days IS standard. We CAN'T back out without cause -- because we have to claim cause in writing for record. We're just preserving the right to find the buyer.

---

## EMD locked vs. EMD at-risk timeline

```
Day 0  -- PSA signed, EMD ($100) deposited at Mid-South Title escrow
Day 1-14 -- DD Period. EMD refundable to BUYER (us) on written termination notice.
Day 15 -- DD Period expires. EMD now at-risk -- forfeited to Seller if Buyer
          fails to close (subject to dual-remedy clause below).
Day 14-21 -- Closing window. EMD applied toward purchase price at close.
```

**Recovery if seller breaches:** EMD returned to Buyer, plus optional specific-performance lawsuit (per dual-remedy clause).

**Recovery if buyer (us) breaches after DD:** EMD forfeited to Seller as liquidated damages.

## Real-world DD termination patterns (when we'd actually walk)

1. **Chris ghosts and no backup buyer signs by Day 12** -- terminate Day 13 citing "Buyer unable to confirm assignee per DD Section 4(f)." EMD returns.
2. **Title finds a $5k IRS lien on a $1,800 lot** -- terminate citing "title cloud unresolved per DD Section 4(a)."
3. **Survey reveals lot is 1/3 size on assessor record** (rare but happens) -- terminate citing "survey discrepancy per DD Section 4(b)."
4. **Code enforcement file shows $4k in unpaid violation fines** -- terminate citing "encumbrances per DD Section 4(e)."
5. **Heir reveals during DD that probate is open and they don't have signing authority** -- terminate citing "Seller authority not confirmed per DD Section 4(g)."

**What we don't terminate for:** "we changed our mind." That's bad-faith and creates reputation risk in Memphis investor community. We always have a citable cause.
