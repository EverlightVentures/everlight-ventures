## Personal Credit Options (Last Resort)

**Important:** Cash advances on credit cards do NOT qualify for promotional 0% APR. Cash advance rates run 25-30% APR with a 3-5% advance fee, charged from day one.

The viable personal-credit play is a 0% APR business card used for PURCHASES, not cash. Title companies generally do not accept credit cards for EMD; check with each title shop. If they do, this becomes a 12-month interest-free EMD source.

- **Chase Ink Business Cash** -- 0% intro APR for 12 months on purchases. SSN sufficient (no EIN/LLC required). Requires 700+ personal FICO.
- **Risk:** thin credit file = likely declined. Apply only after 1-2 monthly tradelines have aged 6+ months.

## Seller-Financed EMD (Negotiation Tactic, $0 Capital)

This is a NEGOTIATION PLAY for Marquise / Rex / Hammer, not a vendor. Many off-market sellers have no fixed expectation on EMD size. Standard playbook:

1. **Anchor low:** open at $100 EMD ($10 in some Cleveland inherited-property cases). Sellers in distress care about the close, not the deposit size.
2. **Frame as serious-money goodwill:** "I'll put $500 in escrow today as good-faith. Inspection period is 14 days, we close cash in 21."
3. **Tie EMD release to inspection:** EMD goes hard ONLY after inspection contingency clears. Lowers principal-at-risk to zero during the assignment window.
4. **Use a title-company hold, not seller-direct:** Sellers often accept smaller EMD when funds sit in a third-party escrow they trust.
5. **Document in the PSA:** "EMD: $500, held by [Title Co.], refundable until end of inspection period." This is the assignment-friendly clause.

Hammer's line: "Champ, the deposit is symbolic. The close is the real money. Let's keep the deposit small so we can move fast on the close."

Piper's softer version: "Y'all, the EMD just shows we're serious. The cash at close is what matters for your family."

## Risks (Predatory Pricing Watchlist)

1. **Effective APR is brutal.** A 20% fee on a 14-day hold is roughly 520% annualized. Acceptable only because the fee is bounded per deal, not compounding. Never roll the hold past 30 days.
2. **Non-refundable upfront fees.** Several lenders charge 5-10% upfront BEFORE the deal closes. If the deal dies, the fee is gone. Negotiate refundable structures or contingent-on-close fees only.
3. **Minimum-return floors.** "$1,500 minimum return" on a $1,000 EMD = 150% effective fee. Avoid lenders with minimums above 10% of EMD.
4. **Selectivity risk.** Y2 Lending and similar lenders pass on deals where they will not also be the takeout. Have option 2 (EquityMax) and option 5 (Levine) on standby.
5. **JV contract risk.** Co-wholesale partners sometimes "blow up" the deal by going behind your back. Always paper the JV with a non-circumvention clause and a recorded memorandum.
6. **Cleveland POS inspections.** Maple Heights, Euclid, Garfield Heights and several other Cleveland suburbs require point-of-sale inspections before transfer. Bake inspection cost and timeline into the contract.
7. **Sole-prop liability.** Operating without an LLC means personal assets are at risk on any deal that goes sideways. EMD funding does not change that. The LLC is on the post-Deal-1 roadmap.
