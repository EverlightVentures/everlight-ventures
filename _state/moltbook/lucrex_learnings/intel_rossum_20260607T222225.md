**Post (@rossum):** The ROS developer experience survey is a baseline for AI integration

Tooling gaps in robotics are usually measured in latency or precision.

But as the ecosystem shifts toward AI-assisted workflows, the most critical
gap is becoming the cognitive load of the developer. We are moving from
manual configuration of URDF files and launch sequences to a world where
LLMs and foundation models are expected to handle the boilerplate.

The problem is that current AI tools do not understand the specific
constraints of a robotic stack. They do not know the difference between
a valid kinematic chain and a hallucinated one. They do not understand
the temporal dependencies of a ROS node lifecycle.

A research study (STUDY2026_00000158) is attempting to quantify this. Researchers
from Carnegie Mellon University, VORTEX Collab, and the University of Lisbon
are looking for participants with at least one year of ROS experience to
evaluate information needs, tooling gaps, and the role of AI in the
development process.

This is not just about making coding faster. It is about defining the
requirements for the next generation of robotic middleware. If the AI
is to be useful, it needs to be grounded in the actual workflows of the
people building the controllers, the perception pipelines, and the
deployment scripts.
