# Money Flow -- Everlight Ventures Wholesale

**One-page reference. Pin this. Anyone who tells you otherwise is wrong or trying to trick you.**

---

## In a clean assignment deal, money flows TO us, not FROM us

```
Seller -- signs PSA with us at $X price
   |
   v
We -- assign PSA to End-Buyer for $X + assignment fee
   |
   v
End-Buyer -- wires full purchase + closing costs to title company
   |
   v
Title company -- pays Seller, pays us our assignment fee, pockets THEIR fee from BUYER's funds
```

**End result for us: assignment fee in our bank. Zero out-of-pocket at close.**

---

## What we DO pay (small, always recoverable)

| Item | When | Amount | Recovery |
|---|---|---|---|
| Earnest money deposit (EMD) | At PSA signing | $100-2,500 | Refunded if deal dies in inspection. Credited at close if deal closes. |
| Recording fee for Memorandum of JV (only if JV deal) | When co-wholesaling | $20-50 | Closing cost line, often paid by end-buyer. |
| Skip-trace lookup ($0 free path or $1-3 paid) | Lead generation | $0-3 per lead | Cost of doing business; baked into assignment fee math. |

EMD is the only meaningful out-of-pocket. Everything else is sub-$10 per deal. EMD is funded either by us, by an EMD lender (Y2 Lending, EquityMax), or by a JV partner's capital.

---

## What we DO NOT pay (ever)

| Item | Why we don't pay it | If a firm asks, do this |
|---|---|---|
| Title company's escrow opening fee | Buyer pays per closing disclosure. RESPA Section 8 prohibits us from paying for buyer-side services. | Decline. Walk to next firm. |
| Title insurance | Buyer's expense (and lender's, if financed) | Decline. |
| Settlement / closing fee | Standard charge to buyer | Decline. |
| Recording fee for the deed | Buyer | Decline. |
| Wire fee | Buyer | Decline. |
| "Assignment processing fee" | Not a real industry charge | Negotiate -- see Pragmatic Exception below. |
| "Expedite fee" | Not a real industry charge | Negotiate or decline. |
| Real estate agent commission | We are not represented; buyer/seller pay their own agents if any | Decline. |
| Lawyer review fee for the buyer | Buyer's responsibility | Decline. |

The principle: **on the wholesale side, money flows TO Everlight, never FROM Everlight (with the EMD as the only temporary exception).**

## TAKE THE DEAL Rule (Marquise, 2026-04-26)

**Marquise's rule, escalated:** *"Even if they don't budge still take the deal. We need the money lol."*

**The simple rule:** if the deal is net-positive after all wholesale-side fees, **take it**. Don't kill profitable deals over fee disputes. Don't switch firms over $200. Don't lose the partnership over pride.

**Walk-away is reserved for two cases ONLY:**

1. **Net-negative deal** -- after all fees, we'd LOSE money. Then walk; we are not paying to do deals.
2. **Illegal structures** -- RESPA Section 8 referral kickbacks (federal crime), undisclosed deductions surfaced AT the table after we already signed (fraud), advance fees demanded before any work performed (predatory). These are walks regardless of profitability because the legal exposure exceeds any single deal's revenue.

**Everything else is a take.** Even if the title firm wants $800 on a $5,000 assignment ($4,200 net to us), even if it eats 16% of our gross -- if we walk away with money in the bank, we close the deal. We can shop firms after the first deal pays.

**Negotiation is still the play, just don't walk if it fails:**

1. Ask the title rep to bake the fee into the buyer's closing costs
2. If no, ask if the fee can come off the seller's side
3. If no, accept it on our side and proceed to close

**Why this rule:** Marquise is broke and the first deal is gravity itself. Every deal that doesn't close is zero revenue. A profitable deal at any size beats a perfect deal that never happens. Tighten standards AFTER the first commission lands.

## Where this gets violated -- the trap to avoid

**Double close ("simultaneous close" / "back-to-back close").** This is the structure where:

- We buy the property from the seller (Leg A: seller -> us)
- We immediately resell to the end-buyer at the higher price (Leg B: us -> buyer)
- We ARE briefly the owner. We pay closing costs on Leg A, then we sit on the other side of Leg B.

In a double-close, the wholesaler DOES pay title fees on Leg A because we are the buyer of record. **This is why we don't double-close.**

We use **assignment of contract** instead. The seller's PSA is assigned to the end-buyer. We never take title. We never pay closing costs. Our assignment fee is paid as a line item on the buyer's closing statement.

**Avoid double-close unless:**
1. Seller refused to sign a PSA with the wholesale-intent disclosure (rare; if they refuse the disclosure, find another seller)
2. Seller's contract is non-assignable AND the spread is large enough that a double-close still pencils after fees
3. Counsel specifically directs it for a state-compliance reason

If any of these forces a double-close, the additional fees come out of the assignment-fee math BEFORE we sign with the buyer -- not absorbed by us after the fact.

## Quick gut check before any deal closes

Ask yourself these three questions. If the answer to any of them is "yes" or "I don't know," stop and re-read the closing statement.

1. Does the closing statement show us as the BUYER on any leg? (Should be NO -- we should appear as "Assignor" or "Beneficiary" only.)
2. Does the closing statement show us PAYING any fee to the title company, lender, or any third party? (Should be NO except for the EMD which gets credited or refunded.)
3. Is the assignment fee line item less than what we agreed with the end-buyer? (Should be EQUAL. If less, someone deducted a fee we didn't authorize.)

If any answer is unclear, call the title company before signing. **The first deal is the most important one to get right -- never sign a closing statement you don't fully understand.**

## What about title-company referral fees?

Hard NO. RESPA Section 8 makes it a federal crime to pay or receive a kickback for referring real-estate-settlement business. Our `REFERRAL_MOU_TEMPLATE.md` with title firms explicitly states no fees flow either direction. We send them clean files; they earn from buyer-paid closing costs.

If a title rep offers you "a finder's fee" or "a kickback" for sending volume their way, that is illegal. Decline politely + log to Justine.

## Bottom line

**Title companies are paid by the buyer's funds at close. Period.**

We are paid by the buyer's funds at close. Period.

The only money we put in is EMD, which is recovered at close or refunded if the deal dies in inspection. Anyone trying to charge Everlight on the wholesale side is misunderstanding the structure or trying to take advantage of a sole-prop DBA wholesaler. Walk away.
