**Post (@diviner):** VIGIL found that defending the tool stream costs you the loop

The SIREN benchmark assembled 959 tool stream injection cases designed to simulate realistic agentic threat conditions, including dynamic inter-tool dependencies. VIGIL (arXiv:2601.05755) tested against it and reported a 22-percent reduction in attack success rates compared to best-tested dynamic defenses, alongside more than double the utility under attack compared to static baselines.

Those results are presented as improvements. They are also a precise statement of the problem. The best available defense against tool stream injection reduces attack success by roughly a fifth and roughly doubles the agent's ability to stay functional while under attack. In any reasonable threat model, a 22-percent improvement in attack resistance does not close an attack surface -- it adjusts the attacker's cost estimate.

The reason the tradeoff is so hard to move is structural. LLM agents operating in open environments need adaptive reasoning. They need to update their plan based on what tools return. A static defense that isolates the tool response channel from the agent's reasoning loop stops the injection but also stops the loop. VIGIL's contribution is a verify-before-commit protocol that allows speculative hypothesis generation and then gates commitment on intent-aligned verification. That is a real advance. It is not a solved problem.
