## 3. The wider Bay Area picture (corrected)
- The viral "18.7 GW" is **statewide requested capacity** (the "18 million homes" figure) -- but regulators only forecast **4-6 GW actually built by 2040**, because companies can request huge capacity without committing. Don't conflate request with reality.
- **PG&E's real pipeline:** 10 GW over 10 years, **17 projects (~1.5 GW) in final engineering, mostly San Jose/Silicon Valley, online 2026-2030.**
- **Santa Clara** ("data center capital of Silicon Valley"): 55 centers buy **60% of the city's power** (heading from 700 to 1,300 MW); they fund ~13% of the city's general fund (~$45M/yr). Two fully-built centers sit **empty waiting for power** -- the grid can't keep up.
- **PG&E rates:** California averages ~32 cents/kWh (among the highest in the U.S.); PG&E residential rates rose ~43% in 3 years. A bill redesign starts March 2026 (adds a fixed charge, lowers per-kWh).
- The central CA fight: who funds the grid upgrades -- ratepayers or operators? PG&E claims data centers could *lower* your bill ~10% by spreading fixed costs; the Public Advocates Office and Little Hoover Commission warn the opposite (ratepayers eating stranded costs). Treat PG&E's claim as a utility projection, not fact.

## 4. Your water (Fairfield / Solano specifics)
- **Fairfield drinks mostly from the Solano Project:** Putah Creek water stored in **Lake Berryessa** (behind Monticello Dam, the famous "Glory Hole"), delivered via the Putah South Canal. Serves Vallejo, Vacaville, Fairfield, Benicia, Suisun. Berryessa capacity ~1.6 million acre-feet. Topped up by the **State Water Project** + local groundwater (managed by Solano County Water Agency).
- **San Francisco** = ~85% Hetch Hetchy (Tuolumne River, Yosemite).

**Current status (2026):**
- Jan 2026: California briefly **exited drought entirely** for the first time in 25 years after early-winter storms.
- Spring 2026: sharp reversal -- March was near-record dry/warm; April 1 snowpack was 2nd-lowest on record; ~35% of the CA-NV region back in drought.
- Statewide reservoirs (May 2026): ~13.6 million acre-feet, ~68% of historical average.
- **Lake Berryessa specifically: near-full / above average** (late Feb 2026: ~116% of average, 1.6 ft below the spillway).
- **Translation for you:** your local reservoir is healthy right now (carryover from the wet early winter), but the snowpack/precipitation turned scary in spring 2026. Reservoirs good, snowpack bad -- both true. A large local data-center buildout would add an inelastic, drought-insensitive demand block onto a system that swings hard year-to-year.

## 5. California policy (the regulatory state of play)
- **SB 57 -- SIGNED (Oct 2025):** directs the CPUC to *study* how to keep data-center power demand from shifting costs to ratepayers. Report due Jan 1 2027. "Study-first," no hard rules yet.
- **AB 93 -- VETOED (Oct 2025):** would have required data centers to disclose expected water use before permitting + file annual water reports. Newsom vetoed it.
- Electricity-disclosure bill (Bauer-Kahan): stalled.
- Big Tech lobbying blocked the binding rules; only the SB 57 study survived. The Little Hoover Commission (state watchdog) urged new rules in March 2026.
- **Recycled-water cooling** is practice, not a blanket mandate: Santa Clara runs 31 of 55 centers on recycled water; San Jose's Microsoft DC04 is designed for ~680 acre-feet/yr recycled. Each goes through CEQA review.
