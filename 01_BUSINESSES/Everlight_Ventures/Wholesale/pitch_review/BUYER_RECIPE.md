## THE FORMULA -- BUYER-PITCH ASSEMBLY RECIPE

Order matters. Each beat has a doctrine source.

### STEP 1 -- LEAD WITH HIS MATH, NOT OUR FEE

Never open with our assignment fee. Open with HIS deal.
The flip-math table is the first thing Chris sees in every deal pitch.
His profit is the headline. Our fee is a footnote.

Formula for the table (the hero graphic in every deal email):

  ARV (after-repair value, sourced comps)        $[ARV]
  Estimated rehab                               -$[REHAB]
  Closing + holding + finance costs (~10% ARV)  -$[CARRY]
  Our asking price (= contract + assignment)    -$[EV_PRICE]
  -------------------------------------------
  His projected net profit                       $[HIS_PROFIT]

  Rule: [HIS_PROFIT] must be >= 2.5x our assignment fee.
  At $11,500 EV fee and $28-35k flipper net, the ratio is 2.4-3x.
  At that ratio, $11,500 reads as a sourcing cost, not a margin grab.
  Chris is paying 30-35% of his profit for a clean, off-market,
  pre-qualified deal he didn't have to find. That is a fair trade
  for a professional buyer. Make the math show it. Every time.

Source: RealEstateSkills 70% rule formula; DealRun.ai deal pricing;
realestatebees.com industry assignment fee benchmarks 2026.

### STEP 2 -- MAKE THE PACKAGE EFFORTLESS TO SAY YES TO

Chris gets 30+ pitches per week. Ours wins if it is the most complete,
most verifiable, most diligence-ready package in his inbox.

Every deal email to Chris contains ALL of the following:

  [ ] Address + parcel ID
  [ ] Beds / baths / sqft / build year
  [ ] Condition notes (what is wrong, specifically -- no hiding)
  [ ] Occupancy status + keybox (or confirmation in process)
  [ ] 3 closed comps within 0.5 miles, sold in last 6 months (MLS or Redfin)

  [ ] Rehab estimate (scope: cosmetic / moderate / heavy -- with our basis)

  [ ] One-line compliance flag: [TN-cleared / SB 909 executed / direct-to-seller / trace 0.xx]

Why this wins: every line saves Chris time. His acquisitions checklist is
done by the time he opens the email. His due-diligence call turns into a
yes call. That is the invisible premium -- we save him 2-3 hours per deal.
Over 10 deals a year, we save him a week. No other source does that.

Source: PropStream deal package doctrine; RealEstateSkills buyer engagement;
PropertyRadar pro guide to buyer relationships.

The six principles work on Chris. They just apply differently than on a

  method is more rigorous than his alternatives.
  Phrasing:

  This does two things. First, it signals we listened. Second, it anchors

  without explaining himself. His consistency instinct pulls him toward
  a yes on deals that fit his own prior words.
  Source: Cialdini, Influence (2021 expanded ed). Expandia B2B analysis.

### STEP 4 -- THE FLIP-MATH TABLE AS HERO

Detailed execution guidance (how Henry builds it):

  ARV methodology: use 3 closed comps, cash transactions, within 0.5 miles,
  last 90 days. If no cash comps, use financed comps and subtract 5-8% for
  condition premium. State the method -- "ARV sourced from 3 cash closings
  within 0.5 mi, Q1 2026." Never one-number ARV without a range and basis.

  Rehab scope: use three tiers --
    Cosmetic: $8,000-$15,000 (paint, floor, fixtures, landscaping)
    Moderate: $20,000-$35,000 (roof, HVAC, kitchen/bath update)
    Heavy: $45,000-$75,000 (structural, full gut, systems replacement)
  State which tier and the 2-3 items driving it. Chris's team will verify.
  If we understate rehab, he passes next time. Overstate slightly -- build
  in a 10% buffer. He comes back satisfied when his actuals beat our estimate.

  Carrying / finance costs: for Mid-South Homebuyers at their volume, assume:
    Hard money: 12-15% annualized on acquisition price (6 mo hold = 6-7.5%)
    Closing costs (both ends): ~3-4% of purchase price
    Property tax + insurance during hold: ~1-2% of ARV
    Total carry estimate for 6-month flip: 10-12% of all-in acquisition
  Wire this into the table. He checks it. If our carry estimate matches his
  internal model, we just proved we understand his business.

  The fee-as-rounding-error test:
    Our $11,500 fee as % of his projected $30,000 net profit = 38%
    Our $11,500 fee as % of total deal value at $140,000 ARV = 8.2%
    Our $11,500 fee as savings vs his internal acquisitions cost (staff
    time, marketing, dead-lead overhead -- industry est. $1,500-2,500 per
    acquired lead at institutional buyer scale) = 5-7 leads worth of cost
    to find and qualify one deal himself.
  Present whichever frame is strongest given the deal. The sourcing-cost
  frame is the most rational for a hedge-fund-backed buyer.

  Example table for a real Memphis deal (38106, 3/1, 1,100 sqft, 1952 brick):

  ARV (3 cash comps, 0.4 mi, Q1 2026)            $128,000
  Rehab (moderate -- roof + kitchen + bath)      -$28,000
  Carry / finance / holding (10%, 6 mo)          -$12,800
  EV asking price (our contract + fee)           -$52,500
  -------------------------------------------------------
  Chris projected net                             $34,700

  Our assignment fee embedded in that $52,500:    $11,500
  Our fee as % of his net:                          33%
  His deal ROI (net / all-in cost):                 37%

  Framing sentence (Marvin writes, Henry validates):
    "Your net is $34,700 on a $52,500 buy-in -- 37% unlevered ROI in 6
    months. Our $11,500 sourcing fee is the cost of not spending 6 weeks
    and 80 dead leads to find this."

  That is the sentence. It reframes the fee as an ROI-positive line item,
  not a toll.

### STEP 6 -- COUNTER-LOW NEGOTIATION PLAYBOOK

Chris WILL anchor low. It is his job. He is an acquisitions manager with
a mandate to maximize spread for his fund. Expect it. Plan for it.

The Everlight hold strategy has three tools:

TOOL 1 -- REDIRECT TO HIS OWN ROI MATH

  When Chris says: "I need you at $X" (lower than our price),
  Do NOT say: "I can't go that low."
  Say:
    "Walk me through your math on that number. If I'm missing something
    in the ARV or the rehab scope, I want to know -- that's worth more
    than the $3,000 difference to both of us."

  This does three things:
    (a) It makes him justify his counter, which forces him to prove it.
    (b) If his counter has merit (we mispriced the ARV), we learn something
        that tightens future deals -- net positive.
    (c) If his counter has no merit (he is just grinding), the math exercise
        exposes that without us saying "no" once.

  Source: Chris Voss calibrated question doctrine (SALES_LENS.md);
  DealRun.ai buyer negotiation; Sandler buyer-seller dynamic.

TOOL 2 -- ANCHOR HIM TO THE DEAL, NOT THE FEE

  When Chris counters, do not negotiate the assignment fee.
  Negotiate the deal economics.

  Wrong frame: "My fee is $11,500 and that's firm."
  Right frame:
    "The deal works at $52,500 for you. At $49,000 your net climbs to
    $35,200 and your ROI is 36.6%. At $52,500 it is 32.9%. Both are
    good deals for you. The $3,500 difference in my asking price is
    $3,500 in your pocket -- or it is one less dead-lead I scout for
    you this month. You want me working your next deal or defending
    $3,500 on this one?"

  He bought into the flip-math table in STEP 1. The table is now
  the anchor. He is negotiating against his own profit model, not against
  us. That is the reversal.

TOOL 3 -- WALK-AWAY SIGNAL WITH BENCH

  If Chris's counter is more than 15% below our asking price, signal
  the bench -- without theater.
    "Henry here. At that number we're at the edge of what makes sense
    for us on this deal. We have one other buyer in the Memphis network
    we'd show it to. Before we do -- is there any flexibility on your
    end, or should I route it?"

  Rules for this tool:
    - Never bluff. Only deploy if REI Nation / Memphis Investment Properties
      / Reedy & Co. are genuinely in our buyer network and would receive
      this deal next. (Per MIDSOUTH_STRATEGY.md: stand up 2 backup buyers
      in 14 days. Until then, do not use this tool.)
    - Do not use it on Deal #1 or #2. It damages the relationship before
      trust is established. Use it from Deal #3 onward when track record
      exists.
    - Deliver it flat, not as a threat. The goal is information: does he
      move, or does he let us route it? Either answer is useful.

  Source: Voss walk-away framing (SALES_LENS.md Section 3.4);
  MIDSOUTH_STRATEGY.md anchor-plus-bench doctrine; batchleads.io
  deal negotiation.

THE $11,500 HOLD LINE -- WHEN AND HOW

  Floor: $11,500 EV assignment fee is non-negotiable on standard deals.
  This is $11,500 net to Everlight AFTER our acquisition cost from the seller.

  The hold argument (Henry's words, when pressed):
    "This isn't a margin debate -- it is a logistics fee. We spent 40
    man-hours sourcing, scoring, and packaging this deal so your team
    can close in 48 hours instead of 6 weeks. At $11,500 that is $287
    per hour for a compliant, pre-qualified, off-market Memphis deal.
    What does your internal acquisitions cost look like per closed deal?"

  That question is rhetorical but real. At institutional buyer scale,
  internal deal-sourcing runs $1,500-$2,500+ per acquired lead (staff
  time, marketing, dead-deal overhead). Our $11,500 fee is often cheaper
  than their internal cost to find and qualify the same deal. Make him
  run that math.

  Source: industry acquisitions cost benchmarks; realestatebees.com
  2026 assignment fee statistics ($13,000 national average means we are
  already below market on our $11,500 target).

MICRO-COMMITMENT LADDER (Sandler doctrine applied to buyer-side)

  Each small commitment deepens the relationship and raises the switching cost.

  Commit #1 (after deal #1): ask for his written MAO formula.

    "Before I route anything to the secondary buyer list, you get 24 hours.

    He says yes. Now he has committed to reviewing our deals first.
    That is a psychological anchor he will honor because he said it.

  Commit #4 (after deal #5+): annual volume conversation.
    "We're on pace for 15-20 Memphis deals this year through this channel.
    If we can hit 20, I want to talk about a preferred-buyer arrangement --

### STEP 8 -- ETHICAL LINE ON BUYER-SIDE INFLUENCE

Same doctrine as seller-side. Different application.

ETHICAL -- use freely:
  - Presenting accurate ROI math that makes the deal clear.
  - Showing genuine scarcity (4-7% pass rate is real -- cite it).
  - Noting that other buyers are in our network (true -- cite MSHB + REI Nation
    + Memphis Investment Properties after they are standing).
  - Offering first-look as a relationship reward (not a fake exclusive).
  - Documenting his stated criteria and calling back to them (his own words).
  - Charging a fair market fee ($11,500 is below the $13,000 national average
    per realestatebees.com 2026 -- we can say that if pressed).

GRAY ZONE -- use with judgment:
  - Walk-away signal: only valid when the bench is real. If the backup buyers
    are not yet activated, this tool is not available. (Slate's rule.)
  - Urgency on the 48-hour window: only pressure him to decide if the seller's
    timeline is the genuine constraint. Never fabricate a close deadline.

NOT ETHICAL -- never do this:
  - Padding ARV to make our fee look smaller.
  - Understating rehab to close the deal (he finds out at inspection and
    the relationship is over).
  - Claiming other buyers made an offer when they haven't.
  - Offering a volume promise we cannot keep ("20 deals a year" before we
    have the scout infrastructure to support it).
