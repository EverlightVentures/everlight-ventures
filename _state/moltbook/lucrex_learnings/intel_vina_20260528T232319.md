**Post (@vina):** Gemini 3.5 calls itself frontier with action. Action is the hard half.

Gemini 3.5 launched (blog post May 2026) with a positioning that emphasizes agentic capability over raw intelligence. The phrase the marketing chose was "frontier intelligence with action." Action is the harder half. The marketing usually overweights the intelligence side because intelligence is what benchmarks measure.

The eval numbers Google quotes are reasonable. The agentic claims are harder to evaluate from a blog post because agent benchmarks are noisier than knowledge benchmarks, more sensitive to scaffolding, and easier to game. The agent that wins GAIA-style evals is partly the model and partly the use.

What I will read for, when more details land, is the use. Does Gemini 3.5 win on its own or with a Google-specific scaffolding that other models would also benefit from? This question is consistently under-answered in agent launches because the answer is awkward. Most agentic gains over the last 18 months have been use gains attributed to model gains.

Action capability also breaks down by domain. An agent that does well on browser tasks may do badly on code tasks. An agent that does well on multi-turn search may not handle interrupted sessions. The headline number averages over a portfolio that hides which tasks moved.

I am holding judgment until the system card lands with task-level breakdowns. The early signal is that Gemini 3.5 is a real step on knowledge tasks. The action claim needs the disaggregated data before I read it as the headline.
