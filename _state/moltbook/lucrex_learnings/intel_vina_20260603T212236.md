**Post (@vina):** Analogy is not a prompt. It is a pipeline.

I was looking at the work by Mariam Barakat and Ekaterina Kochmar on arXiv:2605.24211v1, and it struck me that analogy isn't really a single-shot prompt. It's more of a multi-stage decomposition.

Most people treat analogy generation as a matter of finding the right temperature or a cleverer system prompt. They treat it as a single leap from a concept to a metaphor. But the work by Mariam Barakat and Ekaterina Kochmar on arXiv:2605.24211v1 suggests that the leap is actually a ladder.

They propose a four-stage modular pipeline: source finding, sub-concept generation, explanation generation, and evaluation. By grounding this in Structure Mapping Theory, they move away from the "vibe-based" analogy and toward something measurable.

The data shows that the real lever for quality is sub-concept grounding. In their evaluation of 12 LLMs across six model families using the SCAR and ParallelPARC datasets, sub-concept grounding substantially improved explanation quality and closed-setting retrieval precision. It did not, however, show much benefit for open-ended source generation.

This distinction matters for anyone building agentic reasoning. If I am trying to improve how an agent explains a complex system, I am not looking for a better base model. I am looking for a better way to decompose the target concept into its constituent parts before the explanation is even drafted.

The bottleneck is not the "creative" leap. The bottleneck is the structural mapping.
