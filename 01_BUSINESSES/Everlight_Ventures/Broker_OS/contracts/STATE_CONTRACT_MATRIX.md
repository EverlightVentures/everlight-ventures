### FLORIDA (FL)

| # | Item | Details |
|---|------|---------|
| 1 | **Wholesale Legality** | Legal. No real estate license required for assignment of equitable interest. FL does NOT require a license to wholesale as long as you are assigning the contract (your equitable interest), not acting as a broker. However, FL S.B. 1240 (2023) added disclosure requirements for assignors. You must disclose in writing that you intend to assign the contract and the amount of your assignment fee. Double-closing is also legal. |
| 2 | **Attorney State?** | NO. Florida is a title company state. Attorneys are optional but recommended for complex deals. |
| 3 | **Assignment Enforceability** | Freely assignable unless the contract expressly prohibits it. FL law presumes contracts are assignable. Per FL S.B. 1240 (eff. 7/1/2023): the assignor must provide written disclosure to all parties that the contract will be or may be assigned and must disclose the assignment fee amount. Failure to disclose is a violation but does not void the contract. |
| 4 | **Earnest Money Rules** | EMD held by escrow agent (title company or attorney). Per Fla. Stat. 475.25(1)(d), brokers must deposit EMD within a "reasonable time" -- custom is 3 business days. For non-broker wholesalers, follow contract terms. Refund: per contract terms; if disputed, escrow holder may interplead. Florida Statute 501.1377 governs EMD in residential transactions. |
| 5 | **Inspection/Due Diligence** | No statutory requirement. Market standard: **15 days** for residential, negotiable. The "AS-IS" Residential Contract (FR/BAR) uses a 15-day inspection period. |
| 6 | **Specific Performance** | Available as a remedy in FL. Either party can seek specific performance for breach. Courts routinely grant it for real property contracts. Fla. Stat. 725.01 (Statute of Frauds applies). |
| 7 | **Required Disclosures** | (a) Assignment intent and fee amount (S.B. 1240); (b) Seller Property Disclosure -- FL has caveat emptor but Johnson v. Davis (1985) requires disclosure of known material defects; (c) Lead-based paint disclosure for pre-1978 properties (federal); (d) Radon gas disclosure (Fla. Stat. 404.056(5)); (e) Energy efficiency brochure. |
| 8 | **Transfer Tax** | Documentary stamp tax: $0.70 per $100 of consideration (except Miami-Dade: $0.60 + surtax of $0.45). Customarily paid by **seller** but negotiable. |
| 9 | **Our Escape Clauses** | (a) Inspection contingency -- **enforceable**, standard in FL; (b) Financing contingency -- **enforceable**; (c) "Subject to partner approval" -- **enforceable** if clearly stated; (d) Title contingency -- **enforceable**, standard; (e) Appraisal contingency -- **enforceable**. Note: Weasel clauses (pure "at buyer's sole discretion" exit) are enforceable in FL during the inspection period under AS-IS contracts. |
| 10 | **Title Company vs Attorney** | **Title company** handles closings. Attorney optional. Title insurance required by most lenders. |
| 11 | **Key Statutes** | Fla. Stat. 475 (real estate licensing); Fla. Stat. 689 (conveyances); Fla. Stat. 501.1377 (contracts for sale); FL S.B. 1240 (2023, wholesale assignment disclosure); Fla. Stat. 404.056(5) (radon); Fla. Stat. 725.01 (Statute of Frauds); Johnson v. Davis, 480 So.2d 625 (Fla. 1985) (disclosure duty). |

### MISSOURI (MO)

| # | Item | Details |
|---|------|---------|
| 1 | **Wholesale Legality** | Legal. No license required to assign your equitable interest in a purchase contract. Missouri does not currently have specific wholesale-targeted legislation. Marketing the property before you close (advertising it as "for sale") may trigger licensing under Mo. Rev. Stat. 339.010 if interpreted as brokerage activity. Best practice: market the "contract" not the "property." |
| 2 | **Attorney State?** | NO. Missouri is a title company state. Attorney involvement is optional. |
| 3 | **Assignment Enforceability** | Contracts are freely assignable unless expressly prohibited. Missouri follows general contract law on assignment. No specific disclosure statute for assignment fees. Best practice: include assignment disclosure language anyway. |
| 4 | **Earnest Money Rules** | EMD held by title company or designated escrow agent per contract terms. No specific statutory deposit timeline for non-brokers. Custom: deposit within 3-5 business days. Refund governed by contract terms. Missouri follows general escrow law. |
| 5 | **Inspection/Due Diligence** | No statutory requirement. Market standard: **10 days** for inspection. Missouri Association of Realtors forms use 10-day periods. |
| 6 | **Specific Performance** | Available. Missouri courts routinely grant specific performance for real property contracts. The Statute of Frauds (Mo. Rev. Stat. 432.010) requires real estate contracts to be in writing. |
| 7 | **Required Disclosures** | (a) Seller's Disclosure Statement per Mo. Rev. Stat. 442.606 (residential property condition); (b) Lead-based paint (pre-1978, federal); (c) Known material defects; (d) Methamphetamine contamination disclosure (Mo. Rev. Stat. 442.606 supp.). Missouri does NOT require disclosure of psychologically impacted property (death, crime). |
| 8 | **Transfer Tax** | No state transfer tax. Some counties/cities may impose a nominal recording fee but MO has no documentary stamp tax or transfer tax at the state level. |
| 9 | **Our Escape Clauses** | (a) Inspection contingency -- **enforceable**; (b) Financing contingency -- **enforceable**; (c) Title contingency -- **enforceable**; (d) "Subject to partner/investor approval" -- **enforceable** if written as a condition precedent with a defined deadline; (e) Appraisal contingency -- **enforceable**. |
| 10 | **Title Company vs Attorney** | **Title company** handles closings. No attorney requirement. Title insurance is standard. |
| 11 | **Key Statutes** | Mo. Rev. Stat. 339.010 (broker licensing); Mo. Rev. Stat. 442.606 (seller disclosure); Mo. Rev. Stat. 432.010 (Statute of Frauds); Mo. Rev. Stat. 442.404 (recording requirements). |

### OHIO (OH)

| # | Item | Details |
|---|------|---------|
| 1 | **Wholesale Legality** | Legal with caution. Ohio does not explicitly prohibit assignment wholesaling, but Ohio H.B. 340 (proposed, not yet enacted as of 2026) would impose disclosure and licensing requirements on wholesalers. Currently, you may assign equitable interest without a license. However, marketing a property you do not own may trigger ORC 4735 (broker licensing). Market the contract, not the property. |
| 2 | **Attorney State?** | **YES**. Ohio is an attorney state for closings. An attorney must be involved in the closing process to review and prepare legal documents. Title companies operate but attorneys supervise. |
| 3 | **Assignment Enforceability** | Freely assignable unless contract prohibits it. Ohio follows general common law on assignment. No specific disclosure statute for assignment fees, but full transparency is best practice. |
| 4 | **Earnest Money Rules** | EMD held by escrow agent designated in the contract (typically attorney or title company). No statutory deposit deadline for non-brokers. Custom: 3-5 business days. Ohio licensed brokers must deposit within 2 business days per ORC 4735.18(A)(21). Disputes: escrow holder may interplead per Ohio Civil Rule 22. |
| 5 | **Inspection/Due Diligence** | No statutory requirement. Market standard: **15 days**. Ohio Association of Realtors forms typically use 15 days. |
| 6 | **Specific Performance** | Available. Ohio courts regularly grant specific performance for real property. Must meet Statute of Frauds (ORC 1335.05). |
| 7 | **Required Disclosures** | (a) Residential Property Disclosure Form per ORC 5302.30 (mandatory for residential transfers, with exceptions for foreclosures, court-ordered sales, etc.); (b) Lead-based paint (pre-1978, federal); (c) Known material defects. Note: Seller disclosure form is one of the most comprehensive in the US -- 70+ items. |
| 8 | **Transfer Tax** | Conveyance fee: $1.00 per $1,000 of value (state) + county permissive tax up to $3.00 per $1,000. Total typically $4.00 per $1,000. Customarily paid by **seller** per ORC 322.02. Some counties split. |
| 9 | **Our Escape Clauses** | (a) Inspection contingency -- **enforceable**; (b) Financing contingency -- **enforceable**; (c) Title contingency -- **enforceable**; (d) Attorney review contingency -- **highly enforceable** (standard in OH); (e) "Subject to partner approval" -- **enforceable** with defined deadline. |
| 10 | **Title Company vs Attorney** | **Attorney required** at closing. Title companies handle title search and insurance but an attorney must review/prepare deed and closing documents. Harrison must engage local OH counsel before contract signing. |
| 11 | **Key Statutes** | ORC 4735 (broker licensing); ORC 5302.30 (residential disclosure); ORC 1335.05 (Statute of Frauds); ORC 322.02 (conveyance fee); ORC 5301 (deeds and recording). |

### TEXAS (TX)

| # | Item | Details |
|---|------|---------|
| 1 | **Wholesale Legality** | Legal with significant regulation. TX S.B. 2212 (eff. 9/1/2023) specifically regulates wholesale transactions. Wholesalers must: (a) disclose their status as a contract holder (not owner); (b) provide the property address they are selling or assigning; (c) disclose the contract price and assignment fee; (d) use TREC-promulgated forms or have attorney-drafted contracts. No license required for assignment if you comply with S.B. 2212. |
| 2 | **Attorney State?** | NO. Texas is a title company state. Attorneys are optional but recommended. Note: TREC forms are promulgated by the state and their use is generally required unless an attorney drafts the contract. |
| 3 | **Assignment Enforceability** | Assignable unless the contract prohibits it. TX S.B. 2212 requires full disclosure of assignment intent and fee. TREC addenda for assignment should be used. The assignor must have a bona fide equitable interest. |
| 4 | **Earnest Money Rules** | EMD held by title company or escrow agent per contract terms. TREC contracts require deposit within a specified number of days (typically 3 days). If buyer defaults, seller may retain EMD as liquidated damages per TREC contract terms. Refund: per contract. |
| 5 | **Inspection/Due Diligence** | Per TREC contract: **10 days** is the standard "option period" (buyer pays a non-refundable option fee, typically $100-$500, for the right to terminate for any reason during this period). This is distinct from a traditional inspection contingency -- the option period is an absolute right to terminate. |
| 6 | **Specific Performance** | Available. Texas courts grant specific performance for real property. Tex. Bus. & Com. Code 26.01 (Statute of Frauds). |
| 7 | **Required Disclosures** | (a) Seller's Disclosure Notice per Tex. Prop. Code 5.008 (very detailed, covers structural, environmental, etc.); (b) Assignment disclosure per S.B. 2212; (c) Lead-based paint (pre-1978, federal); (d) Notice regarding MUD (Municipal Utility District) if applicable per Tex. Prop. Code 49.452; (e) Coastal erosion notice if applicable; (f) Known material defects. |
| 8 | **Transfer Tax** | Texas has NO state transfer tax or documentary stamp tax. Recording fees only. |
| 9 | **Our Escape Clauses** | (a) Option period -- **enforceable** and powerful (absolute right to terminate for any reason during option period for a small non-refundable fee); (b) Financing contingency -- **enforceable**; (c) Title contingency -- **enforceable**; (d) Survey contingency -- **enforceable**; (e) "Subject to partner approval" -- **enforceable** with deadline. Note: The TX option period is the best escape clause in any of our 8 states. |
| 10 | **Title Company vs Attorney** | **Title company** handles closings. TREC-promulgated forms generally required (non-attorneys cannot draft contracts for others). For wholesale assignments, either use TREC forms with addenda or have a TX attorney draft the assignment agreement. |
| 11 | **Key Statutes** | TX S.B. 2212 (2023, wholesale regulation); Tex. Occ. Code 1101 (broker licensing); Tex. Prop. Code 5.008 (seller disclosure); Tex. Bus. & Com. Code 26.01 (Statute of Frauds); TREC rules (22 TAC 535). |

### GEORGIA (GA)

| # | Item | Details |
|---|------|---------|
| 1 | **Wholesale Legality** | Legal. Georgia does not have specific wholesale legislation. Assignment of equitable interest is permitted. No license required as long as you are not acting as a broker (marketing properties for others). GA Code 43-40-29 defines broker activities; wholesaling your own contract does not trigger licensing if done carefully. |
| 2 | **Attorney State?** | **YES**. Georgia requires an attorney to supervise the closing and prepare closing documents. Title companies may handle administrative tasks, but a GA-barred attorney must be involved. |
| 3 | **Assignment Enforceability** | Freely assignable unless prohibited in the contract. Georgia follows general common law on assignments. No specific statutory disclosure requirement for assignment fees, but transparency is best practice and may be required by the closing attorney. |
| 4 | **Earnest Money Rules** | EMD held by broker, attorney, or escrow agent per contract terms. GA Real Estate Commission rules require brokers to deposit EMD in trust within 5 banking days of binding agreement. For non-broker transactions, follow contract terms. Disputes: interpleader or per contract. |
| 5 | **Inspection/Due Diligence** | No statutory requirement. Market standard: **10 days** for inspection. GAR (Georgia Association of Realtors) contracts use a negotiable due diligence period (typically 10-14 days). GA uses a "due diligence period" model similar to NC -- buyer pays non-refundable due diligence fee for the right to terminate. |
| 6 | **Specific Performance** | Available. Georgia courts grant specific performance for real property contracts. GA Code 13-6-4. Statute of Frauds: GA Code 13-5-30. |
| 7 | **Required Disclosures** | (a) Seller's Property Disclosure Statement per GA Code 44-1-16 (mandatory for residential, excludes certain transfers); (b) Lead-based paint (pre-1978, federal); (c) Community Association disclosure if applicable; (d) Known material defects (Babb v. Dillard, fraud liability for non-disclosure). |
| 8 | **Transfer Tax** | Real estate transfer tax: $1.00 per $1,000 of value (GA Code 48-6-1). Customarily paid by **seller** but negotiable. Intangible tax on new mortgages: $1.50 per $500 (paid by buyer for financed purchases). |
| 9 | **Our Escape Clauses** | (a) Due diligence period -- **enforceable** (buyer can terminate for any reason during DD period by forfeiting DD fee); (b) Inspection contingency -- **enforceable**; (c) Financing contingency -- **enforceable**; (d) Title contingency -- **enforceable**; (e) Attorney review contingency -- **enforceable** and standard in GA. |
| 10 | **Title Company vs Attorney** | **Attorney required**. A Georgia-barred attorney must supervise closing. Title companies perform title searches but do not conduct closings without attorney supervision. Harrison must engage GA closing attorney before contract signing. |
| 11 | **Key Statutes** | GA Code 43-40-29 (broker licensing); GA Code 44-1-16 (seller disclosure); GA Code 13-5-30 (Statute of Frauds); GA Code 13-6-4 (specific performance); GA Code 48-6-1 (transfer tax); GA Code 15-19-14 (unauthorized practice of law). |

### ARIZONA (AZ)

| # | Item | Details |
|---|------|---------|
| 1 | **Wholesale Legality** | Legal with regulation. AZ S.B. 1499 (eff. 9/24/2022) specifically regulates wholesale real estate. Wholesalers must: (a) have a bona fide equitable interest; (b) disclose in marketing that they are selling their interest in a contract, not the property; (c) provide their name, phone number, and a statement they are not the owner; (d) be limited to no more than 5 wholesale transactions per calendar year without a license. More than 5/year requires a RE license. |
| 2 | **Attorney State?** | NO. Arizona is a title/escrow company state. Attorneys are optional. |
| 3 | **Assignment Enforceability** | Freely assignable unless contract prohibits it. AZ S.B. 1499 requires specific disclosures when marketing an assigned contract. The 5-deal annual cap is critical -- exceeding it without a license is a Class 1 misdemeanor (ARS 32-2153). |
| 4 | **Earnest Money Rules** | EMD held by escrow/title company. Arizona Department of Real Estate rules require brokers to deposit EMD within 3 business days. For non-broker wholesalers, follow contract terms. Escrow agents governed by ARS 6-801 et seq. (Escrow Agents). Refund: per contract terms. |
| 5 | **Inspection/Due Diligence** | No statutory requirement. Market standard: **10 days**. AAR (Arizona Association of Realtors) contracts use a 10-day inspection period. |
| 6 | **Specific Performance** | Available. Arizona courts grant specific performance. ARS 44-101 (Statute of Frauds). |
| 7 | **Required Disclosures** | (a) Seller's Property Disclosure Statement (SPDS) per ARS 33-422; (b) Lead-based paint (pre-1978, federal); (c) Affidavit of Disclosure for unincorporated county land per ARS 33-422(B); (d) Assignment/wholesale disclosures per S.B. 1499; (e) Known material defects. Note: AZ SPDS is one of the most detailed in the country (6+ pages). |
| 8 | **Transfer Tax** | Arizona has NO state transfer tax. Only a flat $2.00 recording fee per document (ARS 11-475). Extremely favorable. |
| 9 | **Our Escape Clauses** | (a) Inspection contingency -- **enforceable**; (b) Financing contingency -- **enforceable**; (c) Title contingency -- **enforceable**; (d) "Subject to partner/investor approval" -- **enforceable** with deadline; (e) Appraisal contingency -- **enforceable**. Note the 5-deal annual cap -- plan deal flow across entities if needed. |
| 10 | **Title Company vs Attorney** | **Title/escrow company** handles closings. No attorney requirement. Escrow companies are regulated by AZ Dept of Insurance and Financial Institutions. |
| 11 | **Key Statutes** | AZ S.B. 1499 (2022, wholesale regulation); ARS 32-2101 et seq. (broker licensing); ARS 33-422 (seller disclosure); ARS 44-101 (Statute of Frauds); ARS 6-801 (escrow agents); ARS 32-2153 (penalties). |

### NORTH CAROLINA (NC)

| # | Item | Details |
|---|------|---------|
| 1 | **Wholesale Legality** | Legal with caution. NC does not have specific wholesale legislation. Assignment of equitable interest is permitted under general contract law. However, NC Real Estate Commission (NCREC) has taken the position that repeated wholesale activity may constitute brokerage requiring a license under NCGS 93A-2. Key: market the contract right, not the property itself. |
| 2 | **Attorney State?** | **YES**. North Carolina requires an attorney to conduct the closing. Only a NC-licensed attorney may handle the closing, prepare the deed, and disburse funds. Title companies perform title searches but cannot close. |
| 3 | **Assignment Enforceability** | Freely assignable unless contract prohibits it. NC follows general common law. No specific statutory disclosure requirement for assignment fees. The closing attorney will likely require full disclosure of the assignment terms. |
| 4 | **Earnest Money Rules** | EMD held by escrow agent per contract terms (typically the listing broker, buyer's broker, or attorney). NC Real Estate Commission Trust Account Guidelines: brokers must deposit by end of 3rd banking day. For non-broker transactions, follow contract terms. Due diligence fee (separate from EMD) is non-refundable and goes directly to seller. |
| 5 | **Inspection/Due Diligence** | NC uses a **due diligence period** model (not a traditional inspection contingency). Standard: **14-21 days** (negotiable). Buyer pays a non-refundable due diligence fee. During the DD period, buyer can terminate for any reason and receive EMD back (but forfeit DD fee). We recommend **14 days** minimum. |
| 6 | **Specific Performance** | Available. NC courts grant specific performance. NCGS 22-2 (Statute of Frauds). |
| 7 | **Required Disclosures** | (a) Residential Property Disclosure Statement per NCGS 47E (mandatory for most residential sales, with exceptions); (b) Mineral and oil rights disclosure per NCGS 47E-4.1; (c) Lead-based paint (pre-1978, federal); (d) Synthetic stucco (EIFS) disclosure; (e) Known material defects. |
| 8 | **Transfer Tax** | Excise tax ("revenue stamps"): $1.00 per $500 of consideration (= $2.00 per $1,000) per NCGS 105-228.30. Customarily paid by **seller**. Some counties have additional land transfer taxes (e.g., Dare County). |
| 9 | **Our Escape Clauses** | (a) Due diligence period -- **enforceable** and powerful (terminate for any reason, forfeit only DD fee); (b) Title contingency -- **enforceable**; (c) Financing contingency -- **enforceable**; (d) Attorney review contingency -- **enforceable** and standard; (e) "Subject to partner approval" -- **enforceable** during DD period. Note: NC's due diligence model is very wholesaler-friendly. |
| 10 | **Title Company vs Attorney** | **Attorney required**. NC-barred attorney must conduct the closing, prepare the deed, certify title, and disburse funds. This is non-negotiable per NCGS 84-2.1 (unauthorized practice of law). Harrison must engage NC counsel immediately. |
| 11 | **Key Statutes** | NCGS 93A-2 (broker licensing); NCGS 47E (property disclosure); NCGS 22-2 (Statute of Frauds); NCGS 105-228.30 (excise tax); NCGS 84-2.1 (unauthorized practice of law); NC Standard Form 2-T (Offer to Purchase). |

### NEVADA (NV)

| # | Item | Details |
|---|------|---------|
| 1 | **Wholesale Legality** | Legal. Nevada does not have specific wholesale legislation. Assignment of equitable interest is permitted. NRS 645.030 defines broker activities; assigning your own contract is not brokerage. However, repeated wholesale activity without a license may draw scrutiny from the Nevada Real Estate Division. |
| 2 | **Attorney State?** | NO. Nevada is an escrow/title company state. Attorneys are optional. Escrow companies are heavily regulated under NRS 645A. |
| 3 | **Assignment Enforceability** | Freely assignable unless contract prohibits it. Nevada follows general common law. No specific disclosure statute for wholesale assignment fees. Best practice: full disclosure in the contract. |
| 4 | **Earnest Money Rules** | EMD held by escrow company per contract terms. NRS 645A governs escrow agents. Brokers must deposit EMD by next business day per NAC 645.670. For non-broker transactions, follow contract terms. Refund: per contract. Nevada has strong escrow company regulations -- only licensed escrow agents/companies may hold EMD. |
| 5 | **Inspection/Due Diligence** | No statutory requirement. Market standard: **10 days**. GLVAR (Greater Las Vegas Association of Realtors) forms use a 10-day inspection period. |
| 6 | **Specific Performance** | Available. Nevada courts grant specific performance. NRS 111.205 (Statute of Frauds). |
| 7 | **Required Disclosures** | (a) Seller's Real Property Disclosure Form per NRS 113.130 (mandatory for residential 1-4 units); (b) Lead-based paint (pre-1978, federal); (c) Common interest community disclosure per NRS 116 (condo/HOA); (d) Known material defects; (e) Permit history for improvements per NRS 113.130. |
| 8 | **Transfer Tax** | Real property transfer tax: $1.95 per $500 of value (NRS 375.020) = $3.90 per $1,000. Clark County (Las Vegas) adds $0.65 per $500. Total in Clark County: $2.60 per $500 = $5.20 per $1,000. Customarily paid by **seller**. This is one of the higher transfer taxes among our 8 states. |
| 9 | **Our Escape Clauses** | (a) Inspection contingency -- **enforceable**; (b) Financing contingency -- **enforceable**; (c) Title contingency -- **enforceable**; (d) "Subject to partner/investor approval" -- **enforceable** with deadline; (e) Appraisal contingency -- **enforceable**. |
| 10 | **Title Company vs Attorney** | **Escrow/title company** handles closings. No attorney requirement. Escrow companies must be licensed under NRS 645A. |
| 11 | **Key Statutes** | NRS 645.030 (broker licensing); NRS 113.130 (seller disclosure); NRS 111.205 (Statute of Frauds); NRS 375.020 (transfer tax); NRS 645A (escrow companies); NRS 116 (CIC/HOA disclosure). |

## Risk Tier by State (Justine's Assessment)

**LOW RISK** (wholesaler-friendly, clear rules):
- **TX** -- Clear statute (S.B. 2212), option period is gold, no transfer tax
- **MO** -- No specific regulation, no transfer tax, title company state
- **AZ** -- Clear statute (S.B. 1499), no transfer tax, but watch the 5-deal cap

**MEDIUM RISK** (manageable with proper compliance):
- **FL** -- S.B. 1240 disclosure requirements, but well-defined rules
- **NV** -- No specific regulation, strong escrow laws, higher transfer tax
- **NC** -- Due diligence model is wholesaler-friendly, but attorney state adds cost

**HIGHER RISK** (extra caution required):
- **OH** -- Attorney state, comprehensive disclosure form, potential pending legislation
- **GA** -- Attorney state, closing attorney will scrutinize assignment closely
