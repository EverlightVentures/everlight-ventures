## The big picture: why state choice matters for wholesale income

Wholesale assignment income is **ordinary income** at the federal level (Schedule C if sole prop, or distributions from an LLC/S-corp). Federal rate at our band: 22%-32% depending on volume.

State income tax is the swing variable. Doing $200K of assignment fees in California (13.3% top bracket on income above ~$700K, but 9.3% kicks in at ~$70K) costs ~$18K-25K in state tax alone. Doing the same $200K in Texas costs **zero state income tax**. That's a 9-12% margin advantage just from where the wholesaler lives + structures.

But it's not just the headline rate. Watch:

1. **Where Marquise lives (residency for tax purposes).** The tax follows residence, not deal location, for assignment income.
2. **Where the LLC is formed.** Some states (DE, WY, NV) have business-friendly LLC regimes; some have franchise-tax minimums that bite at any volume.
3. **Where the property closes.** Doc stamp + transfer + recordation taxes are paid where the property sits, regardless of operator state.
4. **Whether wholesaling counts as "dealer" income.** Dealer = ordinary, no cap gains. Investor (hold + sell) = potential cap gains. Wholesalers are almost always classified as dealers.

## Property-side taxes (paid where the property sits, not where operator lives)

These taxes are paid at closing in the state where the property is located. Wholesalers typically negotiate which side pays in the PSA.

### Transfer / doc stamp tax (per $1,000 of consideration, approximate)

| State | Rate | Who typically pays |
|---|---|---|
| **TX** | $0 | N/A — no state transfer tax in TX |
| **FL** | $7 per $1,000 (doc stamp) | Seller, but negotiable |
| **TN** | $3.70 per $1,000 (state) + $0.115 per $100 (county) | Seller |
| **GA** | $1 per $1,000 | Seller |
| **MO** | $0 | N/A — Missouri has NO state transfer tax. County recordation only |
| **OH** | $1-4 per $1,000 (varies by county) | Seller |
| **AZ** | $2.20 per $250 (only on land transfers, not improved property) | Seller — minimal |
| **NC** | $1 per $500 | Seller |
| **AL** | $1 per $1,000 | Seller |

## How wholesale assignment income is taxed (federal, then state stacks on top)

### Federal classification

Wholesale assignment income is "dealer income" in the IRS view: **ordinary income** taxed at the operator's marginal rate. Schedule C if sole prop. K-1 from LLC/S-corp.

NO long-term capital gains treatment. This is true regardless of how long the wholesaler holds the equitable-interest contract before assigning. The IRS position is that flipping contracts (even unsigned options) is dealer activity.

### State classification

States generally follow the federal classification — wholesaling is ordinary income, taxed at the state's regular income tax rate. Not eligible for any cap gains preferential treatment.

### S-corp election (mid-volume optimization)

Once Marquise's wholesaling income exceeds ~$60K/year, the S-corp election becomes financially meaningful:

- LLC alone: 100% of profit subject to self-employment tax (15.3% on first ~$168K)
- LLC with S-corp election: pay yourself a "reasonable salary" (W-2, with FICA), distribute the rest as dividends (no self-employment tax)
- Savings on $200K of assignment income with reasonable salary of $80K: ~$18K/year in saved self-employment tax

**Operational note:** S-corp election requires monthly payroll runs and a reasonable salary. Worth it above ~$60K wholesale income.

### Where to form the LLC (state choice)

| State | LLC formation | Annual fee | Franchise tax | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **WY** | $100 | $50 | None | Best asset-protection LLC in the country. Charging-order protection by statute. Single-member LLCs respected (unlike DE) |
| **TX** | $300 | $0 (under $1.23M revenue) | Margin tax above $1.23M | Strong, no franchise minimum at our scale |
| **FL** | $125 | $138.75 (annual report) | None | Strong, slightly higher annual cost |
| **NV** | $425 | $200 | None | Strong but $625 setup cost |
| **DE** | $90 | $300 | None | Famous, but no advantage over WY for single-member |

**Best LLC home for asset-protection focused operator:** Wyoming. Form LLC in WY, then register as foreign LLC in operating states (TX, TN, etc.) when required.

### Foreign LLC registration triggers

When Everlight Ventures (CA-formed, currently inactive) wholesale-assigns properties in TX, TN, GA, etc., does the LLC need to register as a foreign LLC in those states?

**Answer:** Generally no for one-off transactions, yes if "transacting business" repeatedly. Each state has its own threshold. Per Bernard's earlier review:
- **TX:** registration threshold is "transacting business" — defined narrowly. Wholesale assignments through TX title companies generally do NOT trigger foreign LLC registration. SAFE for occasional volume.
- **TN:** similar — occasional wholesale activity does not trigger.
- **GA, FL:** similar.

If volume in any single state exceeds 10 closes/year, register as foreign LLC for safety. Cost: $100-300 per state.

## Tax credits + incentives (real estate context)

### Opportunity Zones (federal program, applies in any state)

Designated census tracts where investment qualifies for capital gains deferral + step-up + post-10-year exemption. Mostly relevant for buy-and-hold investors, not wholesalers — wholesalers' income is dealer income, not cap gains, so OZ benefits don't apply directly. Could matter if Marquise pivots to fix-and-flip or rental holdings.

### State historic rehabilitation tax credits

| State | Credit | Use case |
|---|---|---|
| **MO** | 25% state credit on qualified rehab (one of the strongest in the country) | Could matter for fix-and-flip if Marquise pivots |
| **OH** | 25% state credit | Same |
| **GA** | 25% state credit | Same |
| **TX** | 25% state credit | Same |
| **TN** | None at state level | — |

Not directly relevant for wholesale assignments but worth knowing if we ever take title.

### Veteran / first-responder seller incentives

Some states have programs that cover seller closing costs for veteran sellers. Useful in marketing copy: "If you're a veteran, you may qualify for state-level closing-cost assistance — let's talk."

### Energy efficiency credits (federal IRA)

Inflation Reduction Act energy credits transfer with property in some structures. Not relevant for assignment-only deals.

## Practical takeaways for Marquise (the operator-level summary)

1. **Live in TX, FL, or TN.** Save 5-13% of every wholesaling dollar over CA/NY/NJ/OR/MN.
2. **Form the LLC in Wyoming.** Best asset protection. Then register as foreign LLC in TX/TN/etc. at volume thresholds.
3. **Make the S-corp election once income clears $60K/year.** Saves ~$18K/year in self-employment tax at $200K revenue.
4. **Wholesaling = ordinary income, every state.** No cap gains play here. Optimize via state choice (TX/FL/TN) and entity structure (S-corp).
5. **Closing-friction-light states for assignment volume:** TX (zero transfer tax), MO (zero state transfer), AZ (minimal). Avoid heavy doc-stamp states for high-volume operations: FL ($7/k), TN ($3.70/k state + county).
6. **Don't operate in CA at scale.** Too expensive even for property in CA; the 9.3-13.3% income tax kills assignment fee margins.
7. **Property tax climate matters for buy-and-hold pivot only.** Wholesalers don't hold long enough to feel it. AL has the lowest, TX the highest.
8. **MO + OH historic credits** could matter if we ever take title for rehab — file flagged for that future scenario.
