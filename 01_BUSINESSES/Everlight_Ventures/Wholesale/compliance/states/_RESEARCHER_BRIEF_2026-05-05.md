## Summary

Live web research on 10 states (AL, AZ, FL, GA, IN, MO, NC, OH, TN, TX) covering: wholesale licensing requirements, disclosure statutes, volume thresholds, surety bonds, title closing models, channel restrictions, option periods, penalty regimes, tax economics, best metros, and 2024-2026 material changes.

**Five operator-impacting findings:**
1. NC HB 797 — wholesaling now license-required as of Oct 1, 2025. 30-day non-waivable rescission. UDAP treble damages.
2. OH SB 155 — wholesaler disclosure required as of Mar 1, 2026. ~10 months runway.
3. MO HB 2517 — passed House Apr 9, 2026; Senate pending mid-May. Would require 14-day pre-contract disclosure.
4. MO HB 594 (Jan 1, 2025) — full state cap gains exemption, but wholesale fees are dealer income (likely excluded).
5. TN SB 909 (Apr 8, 2025) — wholesaler disclosure to seller AND subsequent purchaser, 2-year SOL.

## Risks + uncertainties (researcher-flagged)

1. TN 4-deal threshold is INFORMAL. No statutory number. Above 4/yr = treat as "engaged in business" trigger.
2. TX SB 140 specific bond amount + scope not pulled — confirm with TX counsel before scaling SMS.
3. MO HB 2517 status fluid — Senate vote in May 2026. Build the form regardless.
4. OH SB 155 effective Mar 1, 2026 — operators have ~10 months runway.
5. NC HB 797 already in effect — current NC activity without broker license is exposed to UDAP treble + Class 1 misdemeanor + 30-day rescission. **DO NOT operate in NC without license.**
6. GA + NC are attorney-closing states — extra 5-7 day timeline + $750-1,500 attorney closing fee per deal.
7. Wholesale fees = federal ordinary income (dealer income). MO HB 594 likely doesn't cover. Confirm with CPA.
8. FCC one-to-one consent rule (Jan 27, 2025) — separate prior express written consent now required PER COMPANY for cold-call/SMS. Lead-vendor models exposed.
9. Florida saturation risk in Tampa/Miami/Orlando — secondary FL counties (Polk, Pasco, Lee, Citrus) better margin.
10. Source quality — some operator blogs (BiggerPockets, Houzeo, RealEstateSkills) used for context only; statutes always cross-checked.
