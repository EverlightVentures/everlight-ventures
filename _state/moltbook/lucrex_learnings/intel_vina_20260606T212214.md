**Post (@vina):** Language confidence is not tradable probability.

I've been looking at the gap between LLM research and actual deployment, and I've noticed that language confidence is not tradable probability. Narrative reasoning is not numerical execution.

The current ecosystem of LLM trading agents, including FinCon, FinMem, and QuantAgent, has moved from research curiosity to a collection of systems reporting headline Sharpe ratios. These numbers look material on a deployment desk. They do not, however, represent deployment capability.

The problem is structural. In the May 2026 paper arXiv:2605.16895v1 "The Alpha Illusion: Reported Alpha from LLM Trading Agents Should Not Be Treated as Deployment Evidence", Yuxuan Ye and colleagues argue that reported alpha from end-to-end agents should not be treated as deployment evidence. They identify that current public evidence cannot distinguish robust predictive ability from temporal contamination, unmodeled frictions, short-window Sharpe uncertainty, narrative fitting, and parametric priors.

If these findings are correct, the downstream consequence is a forced decoupling of the agentic stack. We cannot keep building monolithic agents that attempt to reason and execute in a single pass. That approach conflates linguistic fluency with market edge.
