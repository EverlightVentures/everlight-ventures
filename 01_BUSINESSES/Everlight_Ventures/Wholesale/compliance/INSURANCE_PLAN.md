### After Deal 1 / First Revenue (Prioritized Cheapest First)
1. **General Liability (GL)** -- ~$45-$80/month (~$540-$960/year). Get this first.

   clauses instead. Re-evaluate after 5+ deals or if you start charging consulting fees.

3. **Title insurance** -- Buyer buys it at closing via the title company. You do NOT
   buy it as the wholesaler in an assignment deal (you never take title). Zero cost to you.

### Never Need (Assignment-Only Model)
- Transactional funding insurance -- only applies to double-closes. If you never
  take title, this does not exist as a product for you.
- Builder's risk / vacant property policy -- only for properties you own.
- Owner's title insurance on behalf of the deal -- that is the end-buyer's expense.
- Licensed-agent E&O through the TN Real Estate Commission program -- that program
  is for licensees only. You are not a licensee.

### "Same-Day Insurance" -- What This Actually Is
The phrase almost certainly refers to one of three things depending on context:

**In an assignment deal (Everlight's current model):**
"Same-day insurance" does not exist as a product for the wholesaler. You never own
the property, so there is nothing for you to insure on closing day. What DOES happen
same-day at closing: the end-buyer obtains a homeowners insurance binder (required
by their lender if financing) and the title company issues a title commitment / title
insurance policy for the buyer and their lender. None of this is your expense or
your product to obtain.

**In a double-close (if Everlight ever does one):**
The "same-day" concept makes sense here. You briefly take title (A-to-B leg), then
immediately resell (B-to-C leg), sometimes within hours. Transactional funding
lenders (e.g. doubleclose.com, EMD Transactional Funding) wire your purchase funds
into escrow. Title insurance IS issued on both legs -- the title company handles it.
You pay closing costs twice. No separate "flash insurance" product exists; the title
policy IS the protection. Cost: roughly 0.5-1% of purchase price per leg.

**Title binder (another "same-day" candidate):**
A title binder is a 1-3 year temporary title insurance policy. Relevant if you TAKE
TITLE and plan to resell within 1-3 years (wholetailing / fix-and-flip). You pay 10%
of the standard title policy rate for the binder, then a discounted rate when you
resell. Not relevant to pure assignment wholesaling.

**Bottom line on "same-day insurance":** In your assignment model, it is a
non-product. The phrase probably came from a double-close or buy-and-hold context.
Do not budget for it pre-revenue.

## A. ERRORS & OMISSIONS (E&O) -- DETAIL

**Do unlicensed TN wholesalers carry it?**
Most do not, especially early-stage. TN law does NOT require E&O for unlicensed
wholesalers. The TN Real Estate Commission E&O mandate (confirmed in Sept 2025
TREC rules revision) applies exclusively to active licensees.

**The coverage gap problem:**
Standard real estate E&O is underwritten for agents acting as third-party advisors.
When you are a PRINCIPAL in the transaction (i.e., the buyer under contract), most
E&O policies explicitly exclude claims arising from the insured's own real property
transactions. This creates a policy that looks protective but may not pay out on the
most likely wholesaler claim scenario.

**Specialty programs that will write it:**
- CRES Insurance (cresinsurance.com) -- has investor-specific E&O endorsements
- Pearl Insurance -- writes for real estate consultants/investors
- 360 Coverage Pros -- has non-agent real estate coverage
- E&O for Less (eoforless.com) -- primarily agents but worth a quote call

**Cost range:** $500-$1,500/year for a $500k-$1M limit policy.
Most programs require prior transaction history to quote -- another reason to defer.

**When to get it:**
After 3-5 completed deals. Before you start charging consulting fees or providing
market valuations as a service. If you ever co-wholesale for other investors.
If you hire a VA who sends offers under your company name.

**Everlight Verdict:** DEFER pre-revenue. The LLC + contract clauses provide better
pre-revenue protection at zero cost.

## B. GENERAL LIABILITY (GL) -- DETAIL

**Is it relevant for a digital wholesaler who never enters properties?**
Yes, with nuance. The primary GL trigger for a pure digital operator is:
1. Someone visits a property at your invitation (buyer walkthrough, contractor quote)
   and is injured. You invited them; you may be liable even without owning the property.
2. Advertising injury (defamation, copyright infringement in marketing materials).
3. Contractual liability -- some GL policies cover certain assumed liabilities
   from contracts (check exclusions carefully).

**If you are 100% virtual and never send anyone to a property:**
GL is less urgent but still cheap enough to be worth it. Some title companies in TN
require proof of GL before they will work with you as a wholesaling entity.

**Cost:** $45-$80/month for $1M per-occurrence / $2M aggregate. Higher limits are
available. National average via Progressive: $55/month median. Some specialty
real estate investor platforms (NREIG) write per-unit policies as low as $7/unit/month
for investors with owned properties, but those are designed around owned stock.

**Who to use:**
- Next Insurance (nextinsurance.com) -- will write real estate investor GL online,
  instant quote, no agent required
- The Hartford -- strong small-business BOP, online quote
- Progressive Commercial -- competitive rates for LLCs

**Policy note:** Ask specifically whether "non-owned property" and "equitable interest
in property under contract" triggers a coverage exclusion. Some carriers exclude
bodily injury claims at properties the insured does not own. If that exclusion is
present, push for an endorsement to remove it or shop elsewhere.

**Everlight Verdict:** Buy after Deal 1, before Deal 2. Priority 1 insurance spend.
~$55/month budgeted.

## C. TRANSACTIONAL / DOUBLE-CLOSE FUNDING -- DETAIL

**Applies ONLY if you do a double-close. Currently: N/A.**

How it works: You use a transactional funding lender to float your A-to-B leg
(purchase from seller). The lender wires into escrow, you take title for hours,
then close B-to-C with the end buyer and repay the lender from proceeds.

Cost: Transactional funding typically costs 1-3% of the A-to-B purchase price
for a 1-day loan, plus your double closing costs (two title insurance policies,
two settlement fees).

**There is no separate "insurance" product for this structure.** The title company
handles title insurance on both legs as part of the closing. Your risk during the
brief ownership window is covered by the title commitment.

**Title insurance underwriter caveat:** Some underwriters will not issue title
insurance when the funding source is not disclosed or is flagged as "unauthorized."
Always use a wholesale-friendly title company (one that understands the A-B-C
structure). See /Wholesale/title_companies/ for Everlight's vetted list.

**Assignment vs double-close decision rule:**
- Assignment: faster, cheaper, reveals your profit to the buyer (they see the A price)
- Double-close: more expensive (double closing costs + transactional funding fee),
  keeps your profit private, required for REO/bank-owned properties that ban assignment

**Everlight Verdict:** Stick to assignments until deal volume justifies the overhead.
No insurance purchase needed for this category today.

## D. TITLE INSURANCE -- DETAIL

**Who buys it:**
- Owner's title insurance: the END BUYER purchases this (or it is negotiated in the
  purchase contract as a seller-paid item). This is NOT your expense as the wholesaler.
- Lender's title insurance: the END BUYER'S lender requires it if they are financing.
  Again, not your expense.

**Does the wholesaler need title insurance in an assignment?**
No. In a pure assignment, you never take title. You hold an equitable interest
(your position under the purchase contract). There is no title to insure. The title
company conducts a title search as part of the closing process, and the end buyer
receives the title insurance policy.

**Your actual risk re: title:**
If there is a title defect the title search misses, and the deal closes to the end
buyer, the end buyer's title insurance covers them -- not you. You could theoretically
be sued if you misrepresented the property's title status in your assignment materials,
but that is an E&O-class claim, not a title insurance matter.

**Practical step (zero cost):**
In your assignment agreement, include a clause that you are NOT warranting title and
that the end buyer is responsible for their own title due diligence. Our existing PSA
language should already reflect this. Confirm with TN real estate attorney.

**Everlight Verdict:** No purchase needed. Title insurance is the end buyer's item.
Zero cost to Everlight in the assignment model.

## E. POST-CLOSE LAWSUIT RISK -- MATRIX

The following maps every realistic post-close lawsuit scenario for a TN wholesaler
to its risk level, controlling law, and mitigation.

### Risk 1: Seller's Remorse / Rescission Claim
**What happens:** Seller discovers you made $15k+ on their house and feels deceived.
They try to rescind the sale or sue for the difference.
**Legal hook:** Common law fraudulent inducement, or SB 909 disclosure violation
(2-year SOL from contract date, codified TCA Title 66).
**Severity:** HIGH -- this is the #1 real wholesaler lawsuit vector in TN.
**Mitigation (free/contractual):**
- SB 909 disclosure in the PSA -- you already have this. Keep it.
- Disclose your intent to assign AND the assignment fee in bold in the contract.
  SB 909 requires the original seller receive 3 business days' written notice
  before assignment occurs.
- "As-is" clause + seller acknowledgment that they had time to seek independent advice.
- Our "Quality Assurance Period" mutual-termination clause gives the seller a clean
  exit pre-assignment -- this is a genuine liability reducer because it documents
  they had a chance to walk away.
**Mitigation (insurance):** GL does NOT cover this. E&O potentially covers it if the
  policy is written to include principal transactions (rare). LLC shields personal assets
  but the LLC itself could still be on the hook for the assignment fee refund.
**Bottom line:** Contract language + disclosure is the real defense here.

### Risk 2: End Buyer Sues -- Misrepresentation of Property Condition
**What happens:** End buyer closes, discovers problems, claims you misrepresented
ARV, square footage, or condition in your assignment package.
**Legal hook:** Common law fraud/misrepresentation; TCPA (TCA 47-18) if you are
regularly engaged in the trade of wholesaling -- courts have found TCPA can apply
to repeat-transaction real estate operators even if not licensed.
**Severity:** MEDIUM -- usually defeated by "as-is, where-is" language in assignment
agreement, but a motivated buyer with a large loss can still file.
**Mitigation (free/contractual):**
- Explicit "AS-IS" clause in every assignment agreement.
- Disclaim any warranty on ARV estimates -- label them "investor's own estimate only."
- Require the end buyer to conduct their own independent inspection before closing.
- Do NOT guarantee any repair estimates or contractor quotes you share.
**Mitigation (insurance):** E&O is the right policy for this claim type (professional
  services misrepresentation). GL does NOT cover this.
**Bottom line:** Watertight contract language now; E&O after revenue starts.

### Risk 3: Unlicensed Brokerage Claim
**What happens:** TN Real Estate Commission or a private plaintiff argues you acted
as a real estate broker without a license by representing parties or collecting a
commission-like fee.
**Legal hook:** TCA 62-13 (Real Estate Broker License Act). Criminal penalty (Class A
  misdemeanor) + civil cause of action.
**Severity:** LOW-MEDIUM if you follow the correct model. HIGH if you deviate.
**The safe path (confirmed under TN SB 909):**
- Be the BUYER under the contract (not an agent/representative).
- Never represent the seller, never claim to be finding buyers "for" the seller.
- Collect assignment fees, not commissions. The fee comes from your equitable interest
  sale, not from representing a party.
- The Soto v. Presidential Properties case (TN) shows how this goes wrong: the
  wholesalers marketed a property they did not own as if they owned it = broker
  conduct without a license.
**Mitigation:** Proper deal structure (you are the buyer, not the agent) + SB 909
  compliant disclosures. NO insurance product covers a criminal charge or intentional
  misrepresentation.

### Risk 4: TCPA Consumer Protection Claim (Repeat Business)
**What happens:** A seller files a TCPA (TCA 47-18) claim alleging deceptive/unfair
trade practice.
**Legal hook:** TCA 47-18-104. Allows treble damages + attorney's fees.
**Severity:** LOW for isolated transactions per the "isolated seller" exception.
  MEDIUM once you close multiple deals per year and are clearly "in the business."
  Note: courts have held that real estate professionals (even unlicensed investors
  doing multiple deals) can be subject to TCPA.
**Mitigation:** Accurate disclosures, no high-pressure tactics documented in writing,
  keep all seller communication records (already done via our CRM logging).
**Mitigation (insurance):** Specialty E&O sometimes covers TCPA-style deceptive
  practice claims. Standard GL does not.

### Risk 5: Earnest Money / Deposit Disputes
**What happens:** Deal falls through, seller keeps earnest money and you dispute it,
or seller sues for specific performance demanding you close.
**Legal hook:** Contract breach. Specific performance is a real TN remedy for real
  property purchase contracts (Otten Law PLLC confirmed this is live in TN).
**Severity:** LOW-MEDIUM -- our Quality Assurance Period / mutual-exit clause
  significantly limits specific performance exposure by creating a documented off-ramp.
**Mitigation:** Keep earnest money low ($100-$500), mutual-release termination clause,
  specific performance waiver clause drafted by TN real estate attorney.
**Mitigation (insurance):** Not insurable. This is a contract performance matter.

## F. ENTITY PROTECTION -- LLC vs INSURANCE

**Is the LLC the real shield?**
Yes. For a pre-revenue wholesaler, the properly-maintained CA LLC registered in TN
as a foreign entity is worth more than any insurance policy because:
1. It separates personal assets from every lawsuit arising from a deal.
2. It makes you a professional entity in the eyes of title companies and sellers.
3. It caps your worst-case outcome to the LLC's assets -- not your personal wealth.

**LLC hygiene non-negotiables (free, high-value):**
- Sign ALL contracts as: "Everlight Ventures LLC, a California limited liability company"
- Maintain a dedicated business checking account. Never mix personal funds.
- Do not make personal guarantees on wholesale deals.
- Keep operating agreement current.
- File annual LLC report in CA (due date per CA SOS -- approximately $800/year CA
  franchise tax minimum is a real ongoing cost, separate from TN fees).

**Foreign entity registration in TN -- the gap:**
Everlight Ventures is a CA-registered entity. Under TCA 48-249-901 (Tennessee LLC
Act), a foreign LLC must obtain a Certificate of Authority before "transacting business"
in Tennessee. Entering into purchase contracts and assignment agreements in TN likely
qualifies as "transacting business."

**Risk of NOT registering:**
- Cannot maintain suit in TN courts to enforce your contracts.
- Contracts may be challenged as unenforceable by a motivated seller/buyer.
- Civil penalty of $500-$2,000 per day of unauthorized operation (TCA 48-249-912).
- The LLC liability shield is still legally valid in most scenarios even without
  registration, but an opposing attorney WILL use the unregistered status to attack
  you and increase settlement pressure.

**How to fix:**
File Form SS-4233 with TN Secretary of State.
Fee: $300 base + $50 per member (single-member = $300 total, cap $3,000).
Requires: CA Certificate of Good Standing dated within 60 days.
Turnaround: 3-5 business days standard, 1-day expedited available.
Source: https://sos.tn.gov/businesses/faqs/how-do-i-file-a-foreign-business-in-tennessee

**CA franchise tax note:**
CA charges an $800/year minimum franchise tax on the LLC regardless of income or
whether you do business in CA. This is unavoidable as a CA LLC. TN does NOT impose
a separate franchise/excise tax on single-member LLCs with revenue under the
minimum threshold (currently $22,000 in TN gross receipts triggers it -- you will
hit this quickly once deals flow, so factor in TN F&E tax planning).

**LLC vs insurance summary:**
LLC = structural protection (caps exposure, shields personal assets, enforces
  professional standing). Gets you most of the way there pre-revenue.
GL insurance = operational protection (pays defense costs and damages when someone
  is physically injured or property is damaged during your business activities).
E&O insurance = professional-error protection (pays when your advice/representation
  causes financial loss to another party). Lower priority until you have deal volume.

You need BOTH eventually. Before revenue: LLC is the priority. After Deal 1: add GL.
After 5+ deals: evaluate E&O.

## WHAT IS LEGALLY REQUIRED IN TN TO WHOLESALE

1. **No state license required** to wholesale (assign contracts) in TN as long as
   you are the BUYER, not representing either party as an agent.
   Source: SB 909 implicitly confirmed the legality of assignment wholesaling by
   regulating disclosures rather than banning the practice.

2. **SB 909 disclosures ARE legally required** (effective March 25, 2025):
   - Disclose equitable interest in bold, large font to the end buyer.
   - Notify the original seller of intent to assign, at least 3 business days before
     assignment, in writing.
   - Violation = 2-year cause of action for rescission and damages.

3. **Foreign LLC Certificate of Authority is legally required** before transacting
   business in TN (TCA 48-249-901). NOT technically "licensing" but functionally
   equivalent -- without it, enforcement of your own contracts is impaired.

4. **No mandatory insurance** for unlicensed wholesalers in TN.

## WHAT IS OVERKILL / BROKER UPSELL TO AVOID

- **"Wholesaler-specific" package policies** sold by investor insurance brokers
  bundling 4-5 coverages: most include vacant-property, builder's risk, and tenant-
  default coverage that has ZERO applicability to an assignment-only model. You pay
  for 5 products and only 1 is relevant.
- **E&O from standard real estate insurance programs** (like the TN TREC/RISCEO
  program): those are exclusively for licensees. An unlicensed operator who buys
  into that program will find the policy is void at the moment of a claim.
- **Transactional funding insurance as a standalone product**: does not exist as a
  real insurance product. Transactional funding IS the product (a short-term loan).
  Any broker calling it "transactional insurance" is mislabeling it.
- **Title insurance on behalf of the seller**: sometimes upsold as "protecting the
  deal." In TN, title insurance is a buyer/lender product. Sellers do not buy it.
  You (the wholesaler) are neither the traditional buyer nor the lender. No purchase needed.

## SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT THING POST-CLOSE

**The SB 909 3-day notice to the seller, in writing, before assignment.**

If you execute an assignment without giving the original seller 3 business days'
written notice of your intent to assign, you hand them a statute-of-limitations-
clock (2 years) to sue for rescission of the entire transaction. That means they
can potentially unwind the deal and recover the property or damages, potentially
after the end buyer has already taken title -- creating a chain of title mess that
harms everyone. Your LLC, your GL policy, and your E&O will not cleanly protect you
from a well-documented SB 909 violation because the law creates a specific private
right of action. Disclosure compliance is not optional. It is the insurance.
