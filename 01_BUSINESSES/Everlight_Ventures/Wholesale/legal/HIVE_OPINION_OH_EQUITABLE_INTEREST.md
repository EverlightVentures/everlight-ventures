## b. Short Answer

**GO with conditions, plus mandatory CA-license-status disclosure.** Ohio law permits a non-licensed natural person to enter a purchase contract as principal buyer, hold equitable interest, and assign that contract for a fee. ORC 4735.02 (brokerage license) is NOT triggered by a true principal-buyer transaction. The sole-prop DBA form does not change this conclusion. The lapsed CA license, by itself, does not impose Ohio licensure. However, three live risks force five operational guardrails: (1) Ohio HB 226 (eff. 2024) raises the disclosure-and-marketing scrutiny ceiling for wholesalers, (2) ORC 1349.61 forecloses any default-stage seller without attorney supervision, (3) the lapsed CA license creates a holding-out risk if outbound copy implies licensed status. With the five guardrails below, the Cleveland principal-buyer pipeline is operationally compliant pending external-counsel countersignature.

## c. Statutory Analysis

### ORC 4735.02 -- Real Estate Brokers Act
Defines a "real estate broker" as a person who, for compensation, sells, exchanges, lists, negotiates, or holds out as offering to sell real estate **owned by another**. The statute exempts a person acting on his or her own behalf as principal buyer or seller. The Hive position: a buyer who signs a purchase contract in his own name, places earnest money, and acquires equitable title under Ohio's doctrine of equitable conversion is acting on his own behalf. Assigning that contract for value is the assignment of a chose in action that belongs to the assignor, not brokerage of someone else's property. **Sole prop DBA does not change this.** The DBA is the operator's trade name; the natural person is still the principal. No LLC is required to claim the principal-buyer position.

### ORC 1349.61 -- Foreclosure Rescue Statute
Mirror of CA Civil Code 2945. Triggers on ANY OH homeowner with a recorded notice of default. Imposes 5-business-day rescission, mandatory written contract format, criminal penalties for non-compliance. **Hive posture: NOD-recorded leads are attorney-only and remain blocked in OH per state_gates.json.** This memo does not change that. If Marquise touches a default-stage OH seller without OH counsel wrapping the transaction, the principal-buyer defense collapses and 1349.61 criminal exposure attaches.

### Ohio HB 226 (2024 Wholesaler-Scrutiny Update)
HB 226 tightened ORC 4735 enforcement against bad-faith wholesalers who market property without equitable interest, who use deceptive ARV claims to consumers, or who fail to deliver ORC 5302.30 Residential Property Disclosure Forms on 1-to-4 unit residential. **It does not impose a wholesaler license.** It DOES expand Ohio Division of Real Estate authority to issue cease-and-desist letters and refer matters to the Attorney General for ORC 1345 (CSPA) action. The practical effect: the equitable-interest defense must be DOCUMENTED, not just claimed. A bare "I'm the buyer" assertion without a signed purchase contract on file, EMD trail, and ORC 5302.30 passthrough is now substantively weaker post-HB 226.

### ORC 1345 -- Consumer Sales Practices Act
Fair-dealing standard governs seller-facing communications. Deceptive ARVs, fictitious urgency, and misleading "we buy houses" copy when Marquise is in fact assigning rather than closing all create CSPA exposure. The Attorney General can act on behalf of consumers (sellers) regardless of whether the wholesale transaction itself is licensed activity.

### Interaction with CA License Lapse
California Bus. & Prof. Code 10130 prohibits unlicensed brokerage IN CA. A lapsed CA license is a CA matter, not directly an OH matter. **However**, two cross-jurisdictional risks attach:

1. **Holding-out risk in OH outbound.** If Marquise's email signature, footer, website, or marketing copy uses the phrase "real estate professional," "licensed broker," "REALTOR," or any equivalent that an OH seller could reasonably read as a claim of CURRENT licensed status, that misrepresentation is independently actionable under ORC 1345 (CSPA) and federal wire-fraud statutes if email is the channel. The CA license being LAPSED rather than ACTIVE makes this worse, not better.
2. **CA reciprocal-discipline risk on reinstatement.** If Marquise commits an ORC 4735 violation in OH while the CA license is lapsed, that finding will surface on CA reinstatement review under DRE character and fitness. The CA license must therefore be treated as if it were live for character-and-fitness purposes, which means a clean OH compliance posture today.

**Net analytic conclusion:** Ohio law permits the principal-buyer assignment model for a sole-prop DBA with no LLC. The lapsed CA license does NOT impose Ohio licensure but DOES require an affirmative disclosure ("not currently a licensed real estate broker or agent in Ohio or California") in every OH outbound where a reasonable seller might infer otherwise. The five guardrails below operationalize this conclusion.

## d. Five Operational Guardrails (Mandatory on Every OH Transaction)

1. **Sign the purchase contract FIRST. Market the contract rights only AFTER signature.** No Zillow-style listings, no buyer-list blasts, no "for sale" language on any property until Marquise has a fully executed purchase contract in hand and earnest money deposited into a third-party trust account. HB 132 + HB 226 both attach here. Marketing-before-contract is the single most common path from "wholesaler" to "unlicensed broker" in Ohio enforcement actions.

2. **Earnest money MUST be non-trivial and held by a third-party Ohio title agency under ORC 3953.** $10 EMD is the textbook prosecutor exhibit. Hive minimum: $1,000 or 1% of contract price, whichever is greater, wired to a licensed Ohio title agency trust account named in the purchase contract. The EMD is the documentary evidence of equitable interest. Without it, the principal-buyer defense degrades.

3. **Disclose the assignment intent in writing to the seller in the purchase contract itself, not after.** The contract must contain a "Buyer's Right to Assign" clause AND a plain-English principal-buyer disclosure. Post-signing surprise assignment is the HB 226 fact pattern that triggers Ohio Division of Real Estate and AG attention. Disclose in the four corners of the document the seller signs.

4. **Affirmative disclosure of license status in every OH outbound where licensed status could be inferred.** Marquise's CA license is lapsed. OH sellers may reasonably assume, given the formal-sounding "Everlight Ventures" brand, that they are dealing with a licensed party. The Hive standard is: include the disclosure in (a) every outbound email footer to OH sellers, (b) the purchase contract itself, (c) every direct-mail piece. See Section (e) for verbatim language.

5. **Pass the ORC 5302.30 Residential Property Disclosure Form through to the assignee unaltered.** On any 1-to-4 unit residential transaction, the seller-completed RPDF goes through the assignment unchanged. Failure to deliver gives the end buyer a 3-business-day rescission, which collapses the deal AND creates a record of statutory non-compliance that follows Marquise on the next OH transaction.

## f. Required Language EVERY OH Outbound MUST NOT Contain

The following phrases are the unauthorized-brokerage trap. Strike on sight. Justine's pre-send filter MUST block any of these in OH outbound:

- "I will list your property" / "list with me" / "list your home"
- "I represent buyers / sellers / both" / "I am your representative"
- "Let me negotiate on your behalf" / "I'll negotiate the best price for you"
- "As your real estate professional" / "as your agent" / "as your broker"
- "Licensed real estate broker" / "licensed agent" / "REALTOR" (without the lapsed-status disclosure on the same page)
- "I'll find you a buyer" (this is brokerage; you're not finding a buyer FOR the seller, you are buying the contract YOURSELF)
- "Commission" / "my commission" / "standard commission" (commissions are paid to licensees; assignment fees are paid to assignors)
- "Fiduciary duty" / "duty of loyalty" / "duty of care" (these are agency words, not principal-buyer words)
- "I'll get you the highest price" (representation language)
- "Everlight Ventures, real estate brokerage" / "real estate firm" / "real estate company" (style as "real estate investment" or "real estate buyer" only)
- Specific named title company recommendations in the FIRST seller-facing email. Offer 2-3 options, let seller choose. Naming one carries RESPA Section 8 + Lanham Act risk if there is any referral relationship. (This was the trigger for Justine's 2026-04-25 Variant B halt.)
- ARV claims to the seller in writing without "estimate, not appraisal, no warranty" qualifier and without a specific data source.
