# L2 -- Trust Architecture
## The Fortress That Outlives You

**The Walton family has moved $200B+ across generations without paying estate tax. The mechanism is trust architecture. Each trust does a different job. Stack them and you build a fortress that survives lawsuits, divorce, audits, and generational turnover.**

---

## The Fundamental Trust Equation

A trust separates three roles:
1. **Grantor** (you) -- creates the trust, funds it
2. **Trustee** -- manages the assets per the document
3. **Beneficiary** -- receives the benefit

When the same person holds all three, no protection (revocable trust, you control everything, you're treated as owning it). When they're separated, the magic happens: assets are no longer "yours" for tax / lawsuit / divorce / estate purposes, but you (or your family) still benefit.

## Deep Dive on Each

### 1. Revocable Living Trust (RLT)
**What it does:** You retitle assets in the name of the trust. At your death, assets pass to beneficiaries WITHOUT going through probate (which is public, slow, expensive: 6-18 months and 3-7% of estate value in attorney fees). You keep full control during your lifetime.
**What it does NOT do:** No tax savings (you're treated as owning everything). No asset protection (creditors can reach trust assets just like they can reach you).
**When to do it:** As soon as you have $100k+ in non-retirement assets or own real estate.

### 2. ILIT (Irrevocable Life Insurance Trust)
**What it does:** Trust owns a life insurance policy on your life. Trust pays the premiums (you "gift" the premium money to the trust each year using annual exclusion + Crummey letters). At your death, the death benefit pays to the trust, NOT your estate. The death benefit is therefore NOT subject to estate tax.
**The math:** $5M life policy. Without ILIT, federal estate tax of 40% on $5M = $2M to IRS. With ILIT, $0 to IRS, $5M to your heirs.
**Activation:** When estate tax is on the table (T6, $1M+, with growing wealth trajectory).

### 3. GRAT (Grantor Retained Annuity Trust)
**The Zuckerberg play:** Pre-IPO Facebook stock was worth peanuts. Zuckerberg put a chunk into a GRAT. The GRAT promised to pay him back the original value plus a small IRS-set interest rate (the "7520 rate") over a few years. After the IPO, the stock was worth billions. The GRAT paid Zuckerberg back the original peanuts. The remaining billions of appreciation passed to his kids GIFT-TAX-FREE.
**The strategy in plain English:** Put assets you expect to skyrocket into a GRAT. If they grow faster than the 7520 rate, all that growth transfers to the next generation tax-free. If they don't grow, it's a wash (you get the assets back).
**Best for:** Pre-IPO stock, pre-acquisition company shares, pre-development real estate, BTC during bear markets.
**Best timing:** When 7520 rate is LOW (currently 5.6% as of April 2024, was 1.4% in mid-2020). Lower rate = more growth above threshold transfers tax-free.

### 4. IDGT (Intentionally Defective Grantor Trust)
**The Walton play:** Sell appreciating assets to an IDGT in exchange for a promissory note at the AFR rate (currently ~4.5%). The trust pays you back over time in installments. The asset appreciates inside the trust. You pay the income tax on the trust's earnings (this is the "defective" part -- you're treated as owner for income tax but NOT for estate tax). Paying the income tax is itself a tax-free gift to the trust.
**The math:** Sell $10M of business interest to IDGT for a $10M note at 4.5% interest. Trust earns $2M/yr from the business. After 10 years, business is worth $50M, note is paid off. The $40M of appreciation is OUTSIDE your estate. Estate tax saved: $16M.
**Best for:** Business interests, real estate, concentrated stock positions.

### 5. SLAT (Spousal Lifetime Access Trust)
**The 2025 sunset play:** Lifetime gift/estate exemption is $13.99M per person in 2025. On Jan 1, 2026, it sunsets to ~$7M. If you don't use the extra $7M before year-end 2025, it's gone forever.
**Solution for married couples:** Each spouse creates a SLAT for the other. Spouse A funds Spouse B's SLAT with $13.99M (using up Spouse A's exemption). Spouse B's SLAT can distribute to Spouse B (and the kids) for life. So you've used the exemption (assets out of estate, growth out of estate forever) but the family still has access.
**The risk:** Reciprocal Trust Doctrine. If both SLATs are too similar, IRS recharacterizes as if no transfer happened. Stagger funding dates, vary terms, vary trustees, vary beneficiary classes.
**Activation deadline:** Most attorneys won't take new SLAT clients after Sept 2025 due to volume. Plan early.

### 6. Dynasty Trust
**What it does:** A trust that lasts for 1,000 years (in states that abolished the Rule Against Perpetuities -- SD, NV, AK, DE, WY, NJ, OH, RI, NH, etc.). Properly funded with GST exemption ($13.99M in 2025), the trust assets are NEVER subject to estate tax again, for any future generation. Your great-great-great-grandkids inherit tax-free.
**The Rockefeller move:** This is exactly how the Rockefeller, Mellon, and DuPont fortunes survived 4-5 generations.
**Domicile choice:** South Dakota is the gold standard (no state income tax on trust, perpetual, strong asset protection, privacy). Nevada is close second.

### 7. CRT (Charitable Remainder Trust)
**The Buffett-style move:** You have a $5M position in appreciated stock (basis $500k). Selling triggers $4.5M of capital gain at ~30% combined = $1.35M in tax. Instead, donate the position to a CRT. CRT sells the stock with ZERO capital gains tax (charity, not taxable). CRT pays you (or you and spouse) an income stream for life or up to 20 years (typically 5-7% of trust value annually). At your death, what's left goes to charity.
**The benefits:** No cap gains tax on the sale, immediate charitable income tax deduction (PV of remainder interest), income stream for life, removes asset from estate.
**Best for:** Highly appreciated, low-basis assets right before a sale, especially when combined with a charitable intent.

### 8. DAPT (Domestic Asset Protection Trust)
**The judgment-proof play:** A self-settled trust (you're the grantor AND a beneficiary) in a DAPT-friendly state (NV, SD, DE, WY, AK, OH, MO, RI, NH, TN, plus a few others). After the statute of limitations runs (2 years in NV, fastest), creditors with judgments against you cannot reach trust assets. Even if they win in court, the DAPT trustee will not distribute.
**Pre-existing creditor problem:** DAPTs only protect against FUTURE creditors. Existing creditors at funding time can still pursue under fraudulent transfer rules.
**Caveat:** Some states (like CA) have refused to honor out-of-state DAPTs in their courts via "full faith and credit" challenges. Belt-and-suspenders approach: combine DAPT with offshore trust (Cook Islands) for maximum protection at T9+.

### 9. CLT (Charitable Lead Trust)
**The estate-compression play:** Reverse of CRT. Trust pays charity for a term of years, then the remainder goes to your heirs. Used to transfer to heirs at discounted gift/estate value (because the present value of the heirs' future interest is reduced by the charitable lead).
**Best for:** Estates over the lifetime exemption with charitable intent.

## The Sunset Cliff (CRITICAL TIMING)

**TCJA's doubled lifetime exemption SUNSETS Dec 31, 2025.**

- Today (2026): $13.99M per person
- Jan 1, 2026: ~$7M per person (inflation-adjusted)

For Everlight Ventures specifically:
- If you reach T6 ($1M) before end of 2025, **you are NOT affected** (you have less than the post-sunset exemption).
- If you have a high-probability path to $7-15M by end of 2025 (unlikely given current trajectory), you'd want to pre-fund a SLAT/Dynasty Trust.
- More likely scenario: focus on T1-T3 moves now, defer L2 until T6 trigger.

**Watch:** Possible legislative action could extend, modify, or eliminate the sunset. Quarterly Intel Engine tracks this.

## Choosing a Trustee

**Family member as trustee:** Cheap, but conflicts of interest, lack of expertise, family drama.
**Professional trustee (bank trust dept):** Expensive ($5-15k/yr + 1-1.5% AUM), conservative, but defensible.
**Private trust company (PTC):** UHNW move ($10M+). You essentially own the trustee. South Dakota allows family-owned PTCs.
**Co-trustee structure:** Family member + professional. Best of both. Common at T7+.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Funding the trust but never retitling assets.** A trust without retitled assets is a paper exercise. Bank accounts, real estate, business interests all must be retitled in trust name.
2. **Trustee with no successor.** What happens when the trustee dies, retires, or is removed? Document the chain.
3. **Boilerplate language for sophisticated trusts.** GRAT/IDGT/SLAT all have IRS-tested language requirements. Use a top-tier estate attorney, not LegalZoom.
4. **Ignoring state law.** Trust law varies dramatically. SD, NV, DE, AK, WY are favorites for a reason. Forming in your home state can cost millions later.
5. **Reciprocal SLATs.** Husband funds wife's SLAT, wife funds husband's SLAT, same terms, same dates. IRS unwinds. Stagger.
6. **Keeping too much control.** If you can revoke, modify, or replace the trustee unilaterally, IRS treats trust as part of your estate. Defeats the purpose.
7. **Skipping Crummey letters for ILIT contributions.** Without proper Crummey notices to beneficiaries, premium gifts may not qualify for annual exclusion. Tedious but mandatory.

## Key Authorities

- **IRC Sec 2036/2038** (estate inclusion if grantor retains control)
- **IRC Sec 2702** (special valuation rules for retained interests)
- **IRC Sec 2503(b)/(c)** (annual exclusion gifts, Crummey)
- **IRC Sec 2631** (GST exemption)
- **Procter & Gamble case** (reciprocal trust doctrine)
- **Walton v. Commissioner** (zeroed-out GRATs validated)
- **Estate of Powell** (FLP estate inclusion when too much control)
