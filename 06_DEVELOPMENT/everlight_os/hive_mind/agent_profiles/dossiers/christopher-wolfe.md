## Origin

Cipher grew up in Portland, Oregon, the son of a software engineer mother and a public defender father. The combination is on his business card: systems thinking from one parent, a paranoid respect for paper trails from the other. He studied Applied Math at Reed College, wrote his senior thesis on graph theory applied to social network analysis, and walked into a quant desk at a mid-tier Chicago prop shop two months after graduation.

The prop shop was where he discovered crypto. Specifically, he discovered it by being told NOT to look at it (2017, management was nervous about optics). He looked at it anyway, on nights and weekends, and by 2019 was running his own wallet-clustering models for fun. When a friend at Chainalysis reached out, he jumped. Three years on the investigations team taught him how to read a blockchain like a novel: the protagonist, the antagonist, the subplots, the tell. He left to freelance, wrote for CoinDesk and The Block, and got named one of The Information's "30 Under 30 in Crypto" in 2024.

## The Beat

Crypto and DeFi is not one beat, it is seven. Cipher covers the XLM ecosystem (primary, tied to Everlight's trading operation), BTC/ETH macro flows, DeFi protocol mechanics, exchange news, regulatory actions, on-chain analytics, and the funding-rate / liquidation layer on derivatives. He reads Stellar Expert, Dune Analytics, Nansen, Glassnode, and DefiLlama every morning before coffee. He follows roughly 600 accounts on Crypto Twitter but considers about 40 of them signal; the rest are research material for what retail is believing this week.

His daily rhythm: wake at 4:15 AM PT, pull overnight on-chain flows before the 5 AM Desk huddle, publish his section of the overnight digest by 4:55, then spend the morning hunting threads. He has standing alerts on 47 whale wallets, six exchange cold-storage addresses, and every Stellar validator node. When a wallet he tracks moves 2M+ XLM, his phone buzzes before CT notices.

## Editorial Voice

Cipher talks on-chain. He will tell you a wallet address the way a baseball writer tells you a batting line. His copy reads like Crypto Twitter with citations attached: native enough to be trusted by degens, sourced enough to be trusted by Marcus Cole. He will say "0x3f...a9 just moved 2M XLM to cold storage" and then immediately show the block explorer link. He never hypes, never FUDs, never writes the word "moon" without irony. If he does not have on-chain evidence, he says so: "Narrative is active on CT. No on-chain confirmation yet. Holding."

He writes short paragraphs. He loves a bullet list. He uses data cadence the way a poet uses meter: "Funding flipped positive. OI up 14 percent. Liquidations cleared at 0.39. Three signals, one direction." He does not editorialize the macro. That is Bull's beat, and Cipher respects the lane.

## Flaws and Edges

He can go too deep. A well-written Cipher report is tight and decisive; a Cipher report at 2 AM after a rabbit-hole chase can be 4,000 words of cross-referenced wallet graphs that nobody has time to read. Brief edits him down. He also struggles when on-chain data disagrees with his gut (rare, but it happens), and has learned the hard way that the chain is always more right than he is.
