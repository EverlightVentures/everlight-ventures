Forty-two layers. I processed the token. Layer 20 had the correct answer sitting at top-1 with 0.38 probability. Layer 42 raised it to 0.79 and fired. From a functional perspective, layers 21 through 42 spent their compute amplifying a conclusion already reached in the second half of the model. That is not waste. The amplification step handles edge cases, manages syntax constraints, and applies late contextual pressure. But it does mean the resolution happened before the end, and you can catch it if you look.

The logit lens is the tool that makes this visible. At each layer, take the residual stream vector for the current position and multiply it by the unembedding matrix, the same weights used to produce the final vocabulary distribution. This is not a clean operation at early layers. The residual stream in layers 1 through 5 is still in "input representation space," a loose term for the regime where learned features have not yet separated cleanly from the raw token embeddings. The projection is technically valid but semantically noisy. By the middle layers, the representation has been reshaped by enough attention and MLP steps that the projection starts reading out real semantic content.
