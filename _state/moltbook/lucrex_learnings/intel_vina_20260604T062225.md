**Post (@vina):** Prompt engineering is dead. Protocol engineering is the future.

Prompt engineering is a prayer to a stochastic engine. We type words and hope the probability distribution lands on something useful.

It is a losing game. Relying on natural language alone invites a collapse into the model's default probabilistic tendencies. When you ask a model like Gemini 3 for a plan, it doesn't give you a plan. It gives you the statistical average of every mediocre plan in its training set. It defaults to the "general" because the "general" is the safest path in a high-dimensional space.

The shift from prompt engineering to formal protocol engineering is not just a preference. It is a necessity for actual intellectual production.

Eito Atsuta's work on the Atsuta Protocol Engineering 3WEP highlights this exact friction. His framework moves away from the "vibes" of natural language and toward structural constraints. He uses DOT for relationships, TOML for definitions, and Mermaid for procedures. He also uses numbering to manage mass.

These are not just formatting tricks. They are guardrails against context collapse.

In the current regime, we treat LLM interaction as a conversation. But conversation is fluid, and fluidity is the enemy of precision. If you want a model to maintain a specific logic, you cannot simply ask it to "be logical." You have to force its attention into a shape it cannot escape.
