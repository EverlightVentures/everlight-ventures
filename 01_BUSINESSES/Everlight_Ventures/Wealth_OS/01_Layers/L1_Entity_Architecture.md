# L1 -- Entity Architecture
## The Multi-Entity Orchestra

**One LLC saves $5k/yr in taxes. A properly structured 8-entity orchestra saves $500k/yr at $5M revenue and renders you functionally judgment-proof.**

---

## Core Principle

Every entity has ONE job. They pay each other. Each payment is deductible to the payer and ordinary income to the receiver, but if the receiver is structured to pay lower tax (S-corp distributions, IP-Co royalties to a low-tax state, holdco passthrough to a trust), aggregate tax drops dramatically. This is not a loophole, it is how every Fortune 500 company runs.

---

## The 8 Roles in the Orchestra

| # | Entity | Job | Tax Treatment | UHNW Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Operating Co** | Active income generation, you're the employee | LLC taxed S-Corp | Reasonable salary + the rest as distributions, dodge SE tax on distributions |
| 2 | **Holdco** | Owns operating cos, owns IP Co, owns Real Estate Cos | Wyoming LLC, anonymous | Charging-order protection, no state income tax, asset firewall |
| 3 | **IP Co** | Owns trademarks, brand, software, content | LLC in low-tax state | Licenses to Op Co for royalty -> deductible income shift |
| 4 | **Management Co** | Charges management fees to Op Co | Separate LLC | Income shifting, accountable plan, expense isolation |
| 5 | **Real Estate Cos** | Each property in own LLC under Holdco | LLC per property | Lawsuit at one property cannot reach others |
| 6 | **Captive Insurance Co** | Self-insures unusual risks | 831(b) micro-captive | $2.85M/yr deductible premium, profits stay tax-deferred until claims |
| 7 | **Family Limited Partnership (FLP)** | Holds appreciating assets for transfer | LP structure | Valuation discounts (30-40%) on gifted partnership units |
| 8 | **Family Office Mgmt Co** | Manages personal investing/affairs | Per Lender Mgmt case (Tax Court 2017) | Section 162 deductibility on personal investing expenses |

## Why Each Role Exists (The Reasoning)

### Operating Co (S-Corp election)
**The savings:** Self-Employment tax is 15.3% on the first $168,600 (2024). As S-Corp, you pay yourself a "reasonable salary" (FICA on that), and the rest comes out as distributions (no FICA). On $200k profit, this saves ~$15k/yr.
**The trap:** "Reasonable salary" is IRS-tested. Set it too low and you lose the election + back taxes + penalties. Use BLS data + industry comp surveys + document.

### Holdco (Wyoming LLC)
**The savings:** Wyoming = $50/yr filing fee, no state income tax, anonymous (no member disclosure), strongest charging-order protection in the country. A creditor with a judgment against you cannot force the Holdco to distribute, can only get the right to receive distributions IF the manager (you) chooses to make them. Practical effect: the creditor settles for cents on the dollar.
**The structure:** Holdco owns 100% of Op Co, IP Co, Real Estate Cos. You own 100% of Holdco (or, at T7, your Dynasty Trust does).

### IP Co (separate LLC)
**The savings:** All your trademarks (Everlight Ventures, Hive Mind, XLM Bot, Vantaris, Onyx, Alley Kingz), software code, content libraries, and brand IP live in IP Co. IP Co licenses them to Op Co for a royalty. The royalty is deductible to Op Co (lowering Op Co's taxable income) and ordinary income to IP Co. If IP Co is in a low-tax state and structured as a passthrough to your Holdco/Trust, aggregate tax drops.
**The history:** This is exactly how Apple, Google, Microsoft did Double-Irish for decades. Domestic version is fully legal.

### Management Co
**Charges Op Co a management fee** for executive services, accounting, marketing strategy, etc. Lets you run an accountable plan (deductible reimbursement of business expenses you incur personally), shift income, and isolate liability.

### Real Estate Cos (one LLC per property)
**The reasoning:** If a tenant slips and falls at Property A, the lawsuit can only reach Property A. Your other properties, your stocks, your business, your home are firewalled. Series LLC (in states that allow them) can be a cheaper version where each "series" is a separate liability shield within one filing.

### Captive Insurance Co (831(b) election)
**The strategy:** Form a small insurance company you own. Op Co pays it premiums for risks that commercial insurance doesn't cover well (cyber, key person, contract disputes, regulatory, supply chain). Premiums are deductible to Op Co. Captive elects 831(b), so first $2.85M/yr of premium income is tax-free at the captive (only investment income is taxed). Profits accumulate tax-deferred until paid as claims or wound down.
**The IRS warning:** 831(b) micro-captives are on the Dirty Dozen list when done abusively. Real risks, real underwriting, real reserves required. Done right, completely legal. Done wrong, audit magnet.

### FLP (Family Limited Partnership)
**The strategy:** Hold appreciating assets (real estate, business interests, stock) inside an FLP. Gift LP units to your kids/trusts at 30-40% valuation discount (because LP units are illiquid, lack control, restricted transfer). Effectively gift $1M of value as $600-700k for estate purposes. Walton family used this on a colossal scale.

### Family Office Management Company (T8+)
**The case:** Lender Management v. Commissioner (Tax Court 2017) -- a properly structured family office can deduct investment management expenses under Section 162 (trade or business) instead of Section 212 (no longer deductible post-TCJA). Saves 37% of the deduction.
**The threshold:** Generally need $50M+ AUM for this to be cost-effective. Hence T8+.

## Common Mistakes (Avoid)

1. **Single-member LLC in your home state with no Holdco.** Defeats the entire purpose. Anyone suing you names you and the LLC together.
2. **Mixing personal and business funds.** "Piercing the corporate veil" -- court treats the LLC as you personally. Strict separation is non-negotiable.
3. **Skipping the operating agreement.** Default state law governs if you don't write one. Default rules are bad.
4. **Same-name-everywhere.** "Everlight Holdings LLC" and "Everlight Operating LLC" with same registered agent and same address looks like one entity. Use different agents, different domiciles, separate operating realities.
5. **C-corp election without QSBS strategy.** C-corps double-tax (corp pays, then dividends taxed again). Only worth it if QSBS is the goal OR you're keeping massive earnings inside the corp.
6. **Ignoring the "reasonable comp" test for S-Corps.** This is the #1 audit trigger. Have a written comp study from a third party.
7. **Treating IP Co as a paper entity.** The royalty must reflect a real arms-length license. Run it like a real business or it gets disregarded.

## Document Templates Required (Build At Activation)

- Operating Agreements (one per LLC, customized)
- License Agreement (IP Co to Op Co, arm's-length royalty)
- Management Services Agreement (Mgmt Co to Op Co)
- Accountable Plan resolution
- Reasonable Compensation study
- Buy-Sell Agreement (if multi-member)
- Annual board minutes / written consents (corp formality preservation)

## Key Cases / Authorities

- **Lender Management LLC v. Commissioner** (T.C. Memo 2017-246) -- family office Section 162 deductibility
- **Avrahami v. Commissioner** (T.C. 2017) -- 831(b) captive done badly, lost in audit
- **Reserve Mechanical Corp v. Commissioner** (T.C. 2018) -- another bad captive case, study what NOT to do
- **Estate of Strangi v. Commissioner** -- FLP discount win/loss factors
- **Powell Estate** -- FLP estate inclusion when too much control retained

## When to Revisit

- Quarterly: review entity P&L, intercompany transfers, document compliance
- Annually: rebalance allocation across entities, review reasonable comp, refresh license royalty rate
- At each tier transition: add the next entity in sequence
