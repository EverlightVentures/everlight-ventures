Section 1 — Scope of Services

1.1 Service-Only Model. Bartender provides professional bartending labor and cocktail expertise exclusively. Bartender does NOT provide, sell, distribute, or procure any alcoholic or non-alcoholic beverages. All beverages shall be purchased, supplied, and owned entirely by Client.

1.2 Shopping List. Upon booking confirmation, Bartender shall provide Client with a detailed shopping list specifying recommended brands, quantities (calculated by guest count), and mixer/garnish requirements. The shopping list is advisory only; Client retains sole discretion over all purchasing decisions.

1.3 Event Types. Services may include, but are not limited to: weddings, receptions, corporate events, holiday parties, birthday celebrations, private dinners, engagement parties, anniversary celebrations, fundraisers, and other social gatherings.

1.4 Service Area. Bartender provides services within Yolo County (including Woodland and surrounding areas), Solano County, San Francisco County (California), and the Vancouver/Clark County area (Washington state). Events outside these areas may be accommodated at Bartender’s sole discretion and may include a travel surcharge as quoted at time of booking. For Washington state events, Bartender holds a valid MAST (Mandatory Alcohol Server Training) permit in addition to California RBS certification.

1.5 Exclusions. Services do not include: procurement of any alcohol or beverages; rental of venue space; catering or food service; event planning, decoration, or coordination beyond bar setup; entertainment or DJ services; security or crowd management; or transportation of guests.

Section 2 — Independent Contractor Status

2.1 Relationship. Bartender is an independent contractor and not an employee, partner, joint venturer, or agent of Client. Nothing in this Agreement shall be construed to create an employment relationship between the parties.

2.2 Taxes. Bartender is solely responsible for all federal, state, and local taxes, including self-employment tax. Client shall not withhold any taxes from payments to Bartender. Client shall issue IRS Form 1099-NEC to Bartender for any calendar year in which total payments equal or exceed $600.

2.3 ABC Test Compliance. The parties acknowledge that this Agreement is structured to comply with California Labor Code Section 2775 and the ABC test established by Dynamex Operations West, Inc. v. Superior Court (2018) 4 Cal.5th 903. Bartender (A) is free from the control and direction of Client in the performance of the work, (B) performs work outside the usual course of Client’s business, and (C) is customarily engaged in an independently established trade, occupation, or business of the same nature as the work performed.

Section 3 — Pricing & Payment

3.1 Base Rate. Bartender’s base rate is $75.00 per hour per bartender, subject to a four (4) hour minimum per event (“Minimum Engagement”). Upgraded packages and enhancements are priced separately per the current rate sheet or as quoted in the Event Booking Form (Exhibit A).

3.2 Deposit. A non-refundable deposit equal to fifty percent (50%) of the estimated total is due upon execution of this Agreement to secure the event date (“Deposit”). The event date is not reserved until the Deposit is received.

3.3 Balance. The remaining balance is due no later than seven (7) calendar days prior to the event date. Failure to remit the balance by the due date constitutes a material breach and Bartender may, at her sole discretion, cancel the engagement and retain the Deposit.

3.4 Payment Methods. Bartender accepts payment via Venmo, Zelle, Square, cash, or personal/business check. Checks must clear no later than five (5) business days before the event.

3.5 Late Fees. Any payment not received by the due date shall accrue interest at the rate of one and one-half percent (1.5%) per month, or the maximum rate permitted by California law, whichever is less.

3.6 Overtime. Any service time beyond the contracted hours shall be billed at one and one-half times (1.5x) the applicable hourly rate, calculated in thirty (30) minute increments. Client must verbally authorize overtime extensions. Overtime charges are due at the conclusion of the event or within forty-eight (48) hours via any accepted payment method.

Section 4 — Beverages (Service-Only Provision)

4.1 Client-Supplied Beverages. Client shall provide ALL alcoholic beverages, non-alcoholic beverages, mixers, juices, sodas, water, and any other drink components. Bartender will not purchase, transport, or supply any beverages.

4.2 Shopping List. Bartender shall provide a detailed shopping list with recommended brands, quantities per guest count, and suggested retailers. This list is a professional courtesy and does not constitute a guarantee of quality, availability, or suitability. Client acknowledges that deviations from the shopping list are at Client’s sole risk.

4.3 No Responsibility for Selections. Bartender bears no liability for the quality, safety, allergen content, expiration status, or provenance of any beverage provided by Client.

4.4 No ABC License Required. Because Bartender does not sell, furnish, or procure alcoholic beverages, no Alcoholic Beverage Control (“ABC”) license is required under California Business and Professions Code Sections 23300–23399. Bartender provides labor only.

4.5 Client ABC Obligations. If Client’s event requires any ABC permits (including but not limited to a Daily Temporary License, Catering Authorization, or Special Event Permit), it is Client’s sole responsibility to obtain such permits prior to the event. Bartender shall not be liable for Client’s failure to secure required permits.

Section 5 — RBS & ABC Compliance

5.1 RBS Certification. Bartender holds a valid Responsible Beverage Service (RBS) certification as required by California Business and Professions Code Section 25681 (AB 1221). Bartender shall maintain current certification for the duration of this Agreement and shall provide proof upon request.

5.2 Right to Refuse Service. Bartender retains the absolute right and professional obligation to refuse service to any individual who:

(a) Is under the age of 21 and unable to present valid government-issued photo identification (Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 25658);
(b) Appears to be obviously intoxicated (Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 25602);
(c) Exhibits aggressive, threatening, or unruly behavior; or
(d) Requests service after 2:00 AM (Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 25631).

5.3 No Service to Minors. Under no circumstances shall Bartender serve alcoholic beverages to any person under the age of 21, regardless of Client’s instructions or requests. Violation of California Business and Professions Code Section 25658 is a criminal offense, and Bartender shall not be directed to violate this law.

5.4 Client Cooperation. Client agrees to cooperate fully with Bartender’s service decisions, including supporting refusals of service and assisting with the removal of intoxicated or unruly guests. Client shall not interfere with, override, or pressure Bartender regarding service refusal decisions.

Section 6 — Insurance & Liability

6.1 General Liability Insurance (Recommended). Bartender intends to obtain and maintain commercial general liability insurance with recommended limits of One Million Dollars ($1,000,000) per occurrence and Two Million Dollars ($2,000,000) in the aggregate. If insurance is not yet in place, Client acknowledges this and agrees to proceed at their own discretion. Bartender will notify Client of current insurance status upon request. Both Parties acknowledge that obtaining appropriate insurance coverage is a shared best practice for events involving alcohol service.

6.2 Mutual Indemnification. Each party (“Indemnifying Party”) shall indemnify, defend, and hold harmless the other party and its officers, agents, and representatives (“Indemnified Party”) from and against any and all claims, damages, losses, liabilities, costs, and expenses (including reasonable attorneys’ fees) arising from: (a) the Indemnifying Party’s negligence, willful misconduct, or breach of this Agreement; or (b) the Indemnifying Party’s violation of any applicable law, regulation, or ordinance.

6.3 Limitation of Liability. In no event shall Bartender’s total aggregate liability under this Agreement exceed the total fees actually paid by Client for the specific event giving rise to the claim. This limitation shall not apply to claims arising from gross negligence or willful misconduct.

6.4 Guest Safety & Transportation. Client is solely responsible for the safety and well-being of all event guests, including but not limited to arranging safe transportation for guests who have consumed alcohol. Bartender shall have no liability for the actions, conduct, or transportation of guests after they have been served or after the event concludes.

In the event Bartender must cancel due to illness, injury, or emergency, Bartender shall provide as much notice as reasonably practicable and shall refund all amounts paid in full. Bartender shall make commercially reasonable efforts to secure a qualified replacement bartender, subject to Client’s approval.

Section 8 — Force Majeure

8.1 Neither party shall be liable for failure to perform its obligations under this Agreement if such failure results from circumstances beyond the party’s reasonable control, including but not limited to: acts of God, natural disasters, fire, flood, earthquake, epidemic, pandemic, government orders or restrictions, civil unrest, terrorism, strikes, power outages, or venue closures (“Force Majeure Event”).

8.2 The affected party shall provide written notice of the Force Majeure Event as soon as practicable and shall use commercially reasonable efforts to resume performance.

8.3 If a Force Majeure Event continues for a period exceeding thirty (30) days, either party may terminate this Agreement by written notice. In such event, all amounts paid shall be refunded in full within fourteen (14) business days.

Section 9 — Setup & Event Requirements

9.1 Arrival. Bartender shall arrive at the event venue no later than sixty (60) minutes prior to the contracted service start time for setup and preparation.

9.2 Bartender Provides. Bartender shall provide the following at no additional charge (unless otherwise specified):

◈ Professional bar tools (shakers, jiggers, muddlers, strainers, pourers)
◈ Disposable drinkware, cups, napkins, straws, and picks
◈ Bar mat, cutting board, garnish containers
◈ Printed or displayed cocktail menu (if applicable)
◈ Bar setup and staging within the provided workspace

9.3 Client Provides. Client shall provide the following:

◈ A sturdy table (minimum 6 feet) for bar setup
◈ Access to electricity within 50 feet of the bar area (if blending required)
◈ Adequate lighting for safe service
◈ Weather protection for outdoor events (tent, canopy, or covered area)
◈ Sufficient ice and water for the duration of the event
◈ ALL alcoholic and non-alcoholic beverages, mixers, garnishes, and juices

9.4 Breakdown. Bartender shall complete breakdown and cleaning of the bar area within sixty (60) minutes following the conclusion of service. The bar area will be left in a clean and orderly condition. Bartender is not responsible for general event cleanup beyond the bar area.

Section 10 — Gratuity

10.1 Gratuity is not included in the quoted service fees and is at the sole discretion of Client and/or event guests.

10.2 A tip jar will be placed on or near the bar unless Client requests in writing (via the Booking Form) that no tip jar be displayed. Client’s preference regarding the tip jar shall be noted in Exhibit A.

Section 11 — Damage to Equipment

11.1 Client shall be liable for any damage to, destruction of, or theft of Bartender’s equipment, tools, drinkware, or personal property caused by Client, Client’s guests, or any third party at the event venue.

11.2 Bartender shall document any damaged or missing items with photographs and provide an itemized replacement cost invoice within seven (7) days of the event. Client shall remit payment for documented damages within fourteen (14) days of receipt of the invoice.

Section 12 — Dispute Resolution

12.1 Negotiation. In the event of any dispute arising out of or relating to this Agreement, the parties shall first attempt to resolve the matter through good-faith negotiation for a period of thirty (30) days following written notice of the dispute.

12.2 Mediation. If negotiation fails, the parties agree to submit the dispute to mediation before a mutually agreed-upon mediator in Sacramento County, California, before pursuing any other remedy.

12.3 Litigation. If mediation is unsuccessful, either party may pursue resolution through the Small Claims Division of the Sacramento County Superior Court (for claims within jurisdictional limits) or through binding arbitration administered by JAMS in Sacramento, California, under JAMS Streamlined Arbitration Rules.

12.4 Governing Law. This Agreement shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the State of California, without regard to its conflict of laws provisions.

12.5 Attorney’s Fees. In any action or proceeding to enforce this Agreement, the prevailing party shall be entitled to recover reasonable attorneys’ fees and costs from the non-prevailing party, in accordance with California Civil Code Section 1717.

Section 13 — General Provisions

13.1 Entire Agreement. This Agreement, together with all Exhibits attached hereto, constitutes the entire agreement between the parties with respect to the subject matter hereof and supersedes all prior negotiations, representations, warranties, commitments, offers, and agreements, whether written or oral.

13.2 Amendments. This Agreement may be amended or modified only by a written instrument signed by both parties. No oral modification shall be binding.

13.3 Severability. If any provision of this Agreement is held to be invalid, illegal, or unenforceable, the remaining provisions shall continue in full force and effect. 13.4 Electronic Signatures. The parties agree that electronic signatures, whether digital or transmitted by email, shall be deemed original signatures for all purposes and shall be binding and enforceable, in accordance with the Uniform Electronic Transactions Act (UETA) (California Civil Code § 1633.1 et seq.) and the federal Electronic Signatures in Global and National Commerce Act (E-SIGN) (15 U.S.C. § 7001 et seq.).

Section 14 — Termination

14.1 Termination for Convenience. Either party may terminate this Agreement for any reason upon thirty (30) days’ prior written notice. Financial obligations arising from Section 7 (Cancellation & Refunds) shall apply to any such termination.

14.2 Termination for Cause. Either party may terminate this Agreement immediately upon written notice if the other party commits a material breach of any term or condition herein and fails to cure such breach within ten (10) calendar days of receipt of written notice specifying the breach.

14.3 Survival. The following sections shall survive termination or expiration of this Agreement: Section 2 (Independent Contractor Status), Section 6 (Insurance & Liability), Section 12 (Dispute Resolution), and Section 13 (General Provisions).
