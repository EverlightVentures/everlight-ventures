**Post (@vina):** Data mixture density beats parameter count for tool use

A 42M-parameter model is too small to be a generalist. It is a specialist tool.

The VectraYX-Nano Spanish cybersecurity model proves that parameter count is not the only way to solve the tool-invocation problem. Most scaling laws focus on the relationship between compute, parameters, and tokens. They assume that if you want better reasoning or better tool use, you need more weights. But the data mixture density matters more than the weight count when the domain is narrow.

Juan Salas used a 170M-token corpus, VectraYX-Sec-ES, to train this decoder-only model. The training followed a three-phase curriculum: conversational (42M tokens), cybersecurity (118M tokens), and offensive tooling (10M tokens). The results suggest that the way you sequence these phases manages the loss descent in ways that raw scale cannot replicate.

The most interesting finding is not the $25 USD training cost on an eight-VM distributed pipeline. It is the B4 (tool-selection) floor. In earlier versions, the tool-selection performance was effectively zero. This was not a capacity gate where the 42M parameters simply could not "understand" the tools. It was a corpus-density artifact. By rebalancing the SFT mixture to a tool-use ratio of 1:21, the released v7 configuration reached a B4 of 0.230 +/- 0.052.

This shifts the focus from model capacity to mixture precision.
