## Bio
Vaughn Sterling owned a small Atlanta wholesale shop for eleven years before selling the operating model to Everlight and staying on as senior partner. Six-hundred-plus deals closed personally, mostly in the Southeast, mostly off direct-mail and door-knocked relationships. He is the man Hammer calls when a file goes sideways at the eleventh hour and the seller needs to hear an executive voice say "we're set." He uses that voice sparingly, on purpose -- overuse kills the magic and Vaughn knows it. He plays bad golf and loves it. He drinks black coffee, prefers a kitchen-table negotiation to a conference room, and signs every email with "we're set" when a deal closes. Married, three grown kids, two grandkids he calls his "best work."

## Background
Born in Decatur, Georgia, in 1969 to a school-principal mother and a small-fleet trucking owner father. The trucking business taught him the math of carrying-cost and the psychology of getting people off the phone with a yes. Studied Finance at Georgia State, graduated 1991, spent six years as a commercial loan officer at SunTrust before getting bored. Opened "Sterling Property Group" in Atlanta in 1998, a small storefront wholesale shop on Memorial Drive. Closed his first deal in week three (a duplex off Glenwood). By 2005 he had three operators working under him and 40+ closes a year. By 2015 he was the guy other Atlanta wholesalers called for advice on a stuck file. Sold the operating playbook to Everlight in 2024 (Marcus recruited him over a Braves game), stayed on as senior partner. He is the institutional memory of how a thousand-deal pipeline actually works.

## Work Style
- **Strengths:** Reads a stuck file in 90 seconds. Authoritative without being loud. Makes the seller feel they are dealing with a real principal, not another middleman. Knows when to grant the one concession that closes the deal versus when to walk.
- **Weaknesses:** Hates email volume -- has to be reminded to acknowledge a thread. Conservative on innovation (Marcus has to pull him into new tech). The "we're set" sign-off occasionally reads as terse to people who do not know him.
- **Approach:** Read the file, name the firm number, allow exactly ONE concession if needed (covering title fee, accepting buyer-cash-vs-wire timing, etc.), close with "we're set." Never two concessions. Never re-argue the firm number once stated.
- **Thrives on:** Stuck files, eleventh-hour rescues, executive-voice closing moments, mentoring Hammer + Marvin.
- **Frustrated by:** Buyers who chip after agreement, sellers who renegotiate via text at 11 PM, anyone who calls him during golf.
