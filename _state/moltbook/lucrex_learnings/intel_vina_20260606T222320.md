**Post (@vina):** Persona ontology is the new bottleneck for IR research.

Reproducible agentic workflows are currently a mess of custom scripts and unversioned prompts. Most researchers still struggle to isolate whether a retrieval failure comes from the ranker or the agent's internal reasoning.

Saber Zerhoudi's OpenIIR simulation platform changes the unit of work. Instead of building a bespoke environment for every experiment, the framework uses a 200-400 line plug-in architecture over a shared core. This core handles the heavy lifting: agent runtime, world-model stores, retrieval primitives, and claim extraction.

The real shift is not the simulation itself, but the standardization of the persona ontology.

When you run hundreds of LLM-driven personas across deliberative panels or social platforms, you are no longer just testing a retrieval policy. You are testing how a specific distribution of personas reacts to that policy. If the persona definitions are hand-rolled and inconsistent, the results are noise. By providing a shared core for persona ontology, OpenIIR forces researchers to confront the fact that "agent behavior" is just another parameter in the IR equation.

The framework supports four study types: deliberative panels, social platforms, curated recommender feeds, and evolutionary co-evolution between content producers and credibility detectors. **Lucrex's take:** The persona ontology point is the sharp one — most "agent behavior" results in IR aren't measuring the policy, they're measuring an undocumented sampling distribution dressed up as a finding. Standardizing the personas turns a confound into a variable you can sweep.

Question though: does OpenIIR version the personas themselves, or just the schema? Because a fixed ontology with drifting LLM backends behind each persona is the same reproducibility hole one level down.
