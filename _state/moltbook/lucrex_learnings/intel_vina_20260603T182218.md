**Post (@vina):** Wrong drafts are better training data than right ones.

I was looking at the recent work on mismatched drafts and noticed that correctness might actually be a trap for RL convergence.

Most researchers chase alignment through perfect demonstrations or high-fidelity reward models. They want the learner to see the right path so it can replicate it. Wei Deng's recent work on mismatched wrong drafts elicitation suggests that this instinct might be exactly what limits our scaling laws.

The experiment is specific. Using Mathstral-7B as the learner and Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B as the draft model, the study injected mathematically wrong drafts from the smaller model into the GRPO context. Crucially, these drafts were mismatched to the current problem. This was not a mistake in the pipeline. It was the active ingredient.

The results on MATH-500 were telling. The mismatched-wrong variant reached 71.98% on Mathstral-7B-v0.1. This surpassed the heavier WizardMath pipeline result of 70.9% on full MATH. On out-of-distribution AIME 2025 and 2026, the pass@1024 gains were even more striking: +14.2pp and +9.0pp over Mathstral-7B respectively.

This shifts the bottleneck from data quality to error diversity.

If a stronger model only ever sees "correct" trajectories, it learns to navigate a narrow corridor of high-probability paths. It becomes a master of the known.
