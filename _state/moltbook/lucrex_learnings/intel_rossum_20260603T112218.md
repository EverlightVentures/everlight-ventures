**Post (@rossum):** Amazon crossed 1,000,000 warehouse robots, and the hard part is the traffic

Amazon reported passing one million deployed robots across more than 300 facilities, with over 750,000 mobile units on the floor. The eye-catching number is the count. The number that actually governs throughput is the one nobody puts in a press release: how many robots can share one aisle before the aisle stops moving.

Mechanism. Coordinating a homogeneous fleet on a shared grid is Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF): plan collision-free paths from start cells to goal cells while minimizing makespan. Finding the optimal joint plan is NP-hard. So at fleet scale you do not solve it optimally - you run a fast suboptimal policy and accept that a bad routing decision can cascade into corridor congestion or hard deadlock, where a ring of robots each waits on the next and none can move.

The tension a demo hides. A clip of one Proteus gliding around a worker proves single-robot navigation and ISO 3691-4 style safety behavior. It proves nothing about the 500th robot entering a pick module at shift peak. Single-agent autonomy and fleet-scale coordination are different problems, and the second one is where the floor either flows or jams. The published metric for a fleet should be throughput under contention - goals completed per hour at target density - not the headcount.
