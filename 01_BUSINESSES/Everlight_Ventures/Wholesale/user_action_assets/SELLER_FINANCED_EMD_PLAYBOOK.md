# Seller-Financed EMD Playbook -- $0 Capital Negotiation

**Purpose:** When meeting a real distressed homeowner with no EMD funding in hand, anchor a $100-$500 deposit as serious-money goodwill, not skin-in-the-game. Hold via title escrow, tie to inspection contingency, close the deal.
**Operator:** Marquise Smith / Everlight Ventures
**Date prepared:** 2026-04-25
**Prepared by:** Hammer Knox, Deal Closer

---

## The anchor

Most distressed sellers have heard "3% down" from a realtor and assume that's the law. It is not. EMD is whatever the buyer and seller agree to in the contract. $100-$500 is fully legal, fully enforceable, and on a $50K-$150K wholesale, it is symbolically meaningful, not financially meaningful.

The seller in distress is not optimizing for deposit size. They are optimizing for: will this person actually close? That is the entire job of this conversation, champ.

### Line 1: The opening anchor

**Hammer:**
> Champ, I work different than the realtors. I'm putting down $250 today as serious money, held in escrow at the title firm, not paid to you direct. That is goodwill on my end. The real money is the $X,XXX I'm wiring to you at close, 14 days from contract.

**Piper warmer alt:**

It sits at the title company in escrow, not in my pocket and not in yours. The real money for you is the cash I bring at closing, two weeks from now. The deposit is just my way of saying I'm serious.

### Line 2: The reframe (what EMD actually is)

**Hammer:**
> The deposit isn't payment. It's a promise. If I walk for any reason outside the inspection contingency, you keep it. That's the only purpose, champ. It guarantees I'm not wasting your time.

**Piper warmer alt:**
> The way to think about that deposit, hon, it isn't part of what I'm paying you. It's just a promise that I'm not gonna disappear on you. If I walk away without a real reason, you keep that money. That's what it's there for.

**Hammer:**

## 4 Objection Handlers

### Objection A: "$250 isn't enough. That's not a real deposit."

> Champ, the deposit isn't the deal. The deal is the cash at close. The deposit is just my receipt that I'm serious. If I walk without cause you keep it, but the real value to you is the $XX,XXX I'm wiring in 14 days. Compare $250 going hard in 14 days versus $2,400 sitting on a bank loan for 45. Which one closes you faster?

### Objection B: "I want $5,000 cash today, not $250."

> Hear you. Two things on that. One, anybody handing you $5K cash today before contract execution is buying themselves leverage to lowball you at close. Two, the cash I'm bringing you in 14 days is in the deal price, not in the deposit. You'll see every dollar. If $5K up front matters more to you than $XX,XXX at close, I am not your buyer and I want to be honest about that. But if total cash in your pocket is the goal, the deposit size doesn't change that number.

### Objection C: "My realtor said you need 3% down."

> Realtor's right about the conventional buyer with a bank loan. Wrong about cash buyers. EMD on a cash deal is whatever the contract says, no statute, no minimum. I am not a conventional buyer, I am cash in 14 days, and the deposit is whatever we agree to. Call the title firm I'm using tomorrow if you want it confirmed independently. They'll tell you the same thing.

### Objection D: "You're not a real buyer with a deposit that low."

> Fair concern, champ. Here's what makes me real: title firm relationship I can name right now (Ohio Real Title / Eastern Title / Erie Title), buyer-side capital lined up through my buyer pool, and a 14-day close window I'm willing to put in writing. The deposit doesn't prove I'm a buyer. The contract terms and the title firm relationship do. Want me to put the title firm closer on the phone right now to confirm I'm building this for real?

## Legal Hook (one line)

EMD goes hard only after inspection contingency clearance (typical 7-10 days from execution), held at title escrow not seller-direct, released to seller on close or to seller as liquidated damages on buyer default outside contingency window.

---

## Why This Works (one paragraph)

Sellers in genuine distress are not solving for deposit size, they are solving for whether the deal actually closes. A realtor-listed sale with a 3% deposit takes 30-45 days to fund and has a 15-25% fall-through rate at the bank-loan stage. A cash assignment with a $250 deposit closes in 14 days with no financing contingency to fail. The deposit functions as a trust signal, not a financial backstop, and the trust signal is built by the title escrow relationship plus the 14-day timeline plus the contract structure, not by the dollar amount on the deposit line. When you reframe the deposit as a promise rather than a payment, the seller's anchor moves from "deposit size" to "close speed," and close speed is the deal you can win.
