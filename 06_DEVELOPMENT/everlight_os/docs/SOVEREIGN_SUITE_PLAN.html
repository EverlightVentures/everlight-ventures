Sovereign Suite Plan

Replacing the Proton stack with encrypted, API-automatable, zero-cost infrastructure · 22 July 2026 · Prepared for Rich Gee
The reframe. "End-to-end encryption and open APIs are fundamentally in tension" is not true. The real variable is who holds the key. Proton cannot ship you an API because Proton does not hold your key, so their servers physically cannot decrypt anything to serve a request. Move the key onto hardware you control and encryption stops fighting automation. Everything below follows from that one move.

1. Kill shot: the Oracle mail plan does not work

Blocker. Oracle Cloud blocks outbound TCP port 25 on every tenancy created after 23 June 2021. It cannot be opened with a security-list egress rule. The sanctioned unblock path is Oracle Email Delivery relay, which is paid-account only. Any plan that says "install Mailcow on your Oracle free tier and send mail" fails in week three.

The fix is architectural, not a workaround. Split the mail path:

  • Inbound lands on the Oracle box directly. Ports 25-inbound, 143, 993, 587 are all fine. Nothing is blocked on the receive side.
  • Outbound never touches port 25. The mail server is configured with a smarthost and relays every message over port 587 to an authenticated provider.

You already own the smarthost. It is Resend, and it is already wrapped in content_tools/branded_mailer.py behind resend_guard and resend_budget. The self-hosted server becomes just another caller. Free-tier relay options if Resend budget gets tight: Brevo at 300/day with SMTP relay included, Mailjet at 6,000/month capped 200/day. SendGrid retired its free plan in 2025, do not plan around it.

2. Your Oracle box is already free, and it is grandfathered

Verified in 03_AUTOMATION_CORE/01_Scripts/e5_mother/README.md:27: e5-mother is VM.Standard.A1.Flex, 4 OCPU, 18 GB. That is the Always Free Ampere allocation, not a paid instance.

Do not tear this down. Oracle halved the Always Free ARM allowance in June 2026, from 4 OCPU / 24 GB to 2 OCPU / 12 GB. You are holding a legacy allocation that a new signup cannot get. Oracle is also the only free tier with 10 TB monthly egress. There is no better free cloud to "move to" -- you are already standing on it.

On opening a second Oracle account under a private email: do not. Oracle fingerprints the payment card, phone, and billing address, not the email. A duplicate signature gets both tenancies terminated with no export window, and e5-mother is Blinko plus Open WebUI plus the MCP fleet plus the crons. Three cleaner paths, in order:

  • Change the admin user's email in IAM on the tenancy you already have. Zero risk, keeps the capacity.
  • Use compartments for work-vs-personal isolation. Separate IAM policies, quotas, and billing views inside one tenancy. This is Oracle's native answer and a second account is usually just a workaround for not knowing it exists.
  • If you genuinely need a second box, take it from a different vendor. Google Cloud e2-micro is always-free but shared-core and only 200 GB egress. No ToS collision.

Practical wrinkle: a new Oracle account picks a new permanent home region and Ampere A1 free capacity is usually out of stock. You would be capacity hunting for weeks for a box you might lose anyway.

2B. Getting a second box: work vs personal separation

The goal is a personal machine, separate from the work Hive, to hold the Proton replacement. That is a good instinct and it is achievable at $0. A second Oracle account is just the wrong instrument. Five options, ranked by what to actually do:

Option 1 — Second VM in the same tenancy, personal compartment do this

Free, available today, zero ToS risk. Separate from the Ampere allocation, every Oracle tenancy also gets 2 x VM.Standard.E2.1.Micro (AMD, 1/8 OCPU, 1 GB RAM each) always free, plus 200 GB block storage. These were not touched by the June 2026 ARM cut. You have almost certainly never spun them up.

Put one in a compartment named personal with its own IAM policy, its own VCN, its own security list, and its own SSH key. Work automation then cannot reach it, because the policy boundary is enforced by Oracle rather than by discipline. That is stronger separation than two accounts would give you, since two accounts still share your credentials in practice.

Honest sizing. 1 GB of RAM is small. What fits: Vaultwarden (about 50 MB idle), Tailscale, rclone crypt jobs, a lightweight mail relay, a cron host. What does not fit: Nextcloud, Open WebUI, Stalwart with full-text search, anything running a model. So this box is Tier 0 and Tier 3 of the plan, and it is genuinely enough for the password manager plus encrypted file sync, which is the majority of what Proton was doing for you.

Option 2 — The AceMagician PC, which you already own best for personal data

Arch Linux, on the tailnet at 100.93.253.49, already running bidirectional sync via claude_sync_acemagician.sh, and already designated in TRI_DEVICE_VAULT_DESIGN.md as the sole full holder of the vault.

Personal data belongs on hardware you own. No ToS, no vendor, no termination email, no RAM ceiling, no capacity hunt. It runs the heavy half of the personal suite: Nextcloud or plain rclone storage, a local model, full-size mail if you go that route.

Its one weakness is uptime. A desktop is not always on, so it cannot be the only home for a password vault you need at 2am on your phone. That is precisely why Options 1 and 2 pair: Oracle micro holds the always-on services (Vaultwarden, Tailscale, relay), the PC holds the bulk (files, backups, local AI). Two boxes, both free, cleanly separated from the work Hive.

Option 3 — Google Cloud e2-micro clean second vendor

One non-preemptible e2-micro per month in us-west1, us-central1, or us-east1. Permanently free, 1 GB RAM, 30 GB standard persistent disk, 200 GB egress. Different vendor means no ToS collision with your Oracle tenancy whatsoever, and it is a genuinely separate blast radius: an Oracle sweep cannot take it with them. Comparable size to the Oracle micro, so pick it over Option 1 if what you want most is independence from Oracle rather than convenience.

Option 4 — A second Oracle account do not

Explicitly prohibited. Oracle's FAQ states one Free Trial or Always Free account per person, and that "creating or attempting to create multiple free accounts is prohibited," with non-compliance resulting in suspension or termination. Oracle fingerprints the payment card, phone number, and billing address, not the email address. A new private email on the same card is the textbook duplicate signature.

The cost of being wrong is not "the new box gets closed." It is both tenancies terminated, and the work one is e5-mother: Blinko, Open WebUI, the MCP fleet, the crons, and a grandfathered 4 OCPU allocation that cannot be re-obtained. Terminations in this class come with no grace period and no export window. You would be risking the entire Hive to avoid spinning up a micro instance you already have for free.

The one legitimate version of this: once the NV parent / TN sub entity exists (already on the roadmap for after Deal 1), a business account opened on the entity's EIN, business card, and business address is a genuinely different customer, not the same person twice. That is the clean path to a real second tenancy. It just is not available today, and it is not needed for this.

Option 5 — AWS dead end

AWS replaced the old 12-month free tier in July 2025 with a credit model: $100 growing to $200, and the Free plan runs 6 months or until credits are exhausted, after which the account closes automatically. That is a trial, not a home for a personal suite. Rule it out.

Live risk, verify this week. Oracle cut Always Free Ampere from 4 OCPU / 24 GB to 2 OCPU / 12 GB on 15 June 2026 with no blog post, no email, and no customer notification. Per InfoQ, users "discovered the new limits when their instances were shut down." 4. Three corrections that change decisions

Kimi is a privacy downgrade from Lumo, not a sidegrade

Moonshot processes hosted-API prompts on China-based servers. The policy never names the storage country, claims the right to train on user prompts, and documents no in-product training opt-out as of July 2026. Lumo has zero-access encryption, runs on Proton-controlled hardware in Europe under Swiss law, and does not train on chats. Leaving Proton over cost and landing on Kimi trades money for the exact thing you said you were protecting.

Correct move: Open WebUI on e5-mother is already live and beats both for anything touching deals, contracts, seeds, or personal records. Kimi stays for disposable public queries. If you want Kimi's actual model quality without Moonshot's servers, K2 is open-weight and self-hostable, though 4 OCPU / 18 GB will not run it -- that is an AceMagician job, not an e5 job.

Bitwarden Free was cut in 2026, so Vaultwarden is not a coequal option

Bitwarden's free plan lost integrated TOTP, file attachments, and emergency access. Premium went from $9.99/yr to $19.80/yr in January 2026, roughly a 98% jump. Vaultwarden reimplements the same server API in Rust, so every official Bitwarden client (browser, Android, desktop, CLI) connects to it and gets all the stripped features back at $0. It idles around 50 MB of RAM on SQLite.

Correct move: Vaultwarden on e5, bound tailnet-only per the Network Binding Doctrine. The Bitwarden CLI then becomes a scriptable secret source that secrets_vault.py can read from. That is a password manager with an API, which Proton Pass is not.

rclone crypt beats Cryptomator for this use case

Cryptomator charges for Android write access. rclone crypt is free on every platform, does client-side AES before upload, works against any backend (Nextcloud, Drive, S3, Backblaze, a local disk), and is a command-line tool, which means the encryption layer itself is automatable. For a workspace whose whole thesis is scripted operations, that difference is the entire decision.

5. The architecture: tier by whether the counterparty is encrypted

The insight that collapses the problem. Encrypting an outbound email to a seller in Memphis buys close to nothing, because it lands in their plaintext Gmail regardless. E2EE pays off in exactly two places: data at rest that you alone hold, and messages where both ends hold keys. Stop encrypting everything and tier it instead.

The one real fork: Tier 2

Everything else has an obvious answer. This one does not, and the answer changes what gets built.

  • Option A, Stalwart on e5. AGPL-3.0, single Rust binary, speaks IMAP4rev2, JMAP, SMTP, CalDAV, CardDAV, WebDAV. Community Edition has no mailbox or domain limits and no feature gating on core mail. JMAP (RFC 8621) is the modern JSON email API, which is the actual "encrypted mailbox with a real API" you asked for and the thing Proton has refused to ship for eleven years. Cost: you become a mail sysadmin. DNS discipline (SPF, DKIM, DMARC, PTR) is non-optional and deliverability is your problem forever.
  • Option B, free managed E2EE mailbox, no automation. Tuta free (1 GB, max encryption, no IMAP) or Disroot (2 GB, IMAP, GPG-compatible but no E2EE by default). Zero maintenance, zero cost, and you accept that personal mail simply is not scriptable. Given that Tier 1 already carries every automated flow, this may cost you nothing real.

Recommendation: Option B first, Option A later if you actually miss something. Nothing in the Deal-1 pipeline or the Hive depends on personal mail being an API surface, and standing up a mail server is the single highest-maintenance item on this entire page. Do the two-hour wins before the two-week one.

6. Verify before you build: ImprovMX headroom

Unverified assumption. ImprovMX free tier is 1 domain, 25 aliases, 500 forwards per day, 7-day logs, and is receive-only with no SMTP sending. CLAUDE.md claims 42 aliases at @everlightventures.io. That is 17 over the free ceiling. Either this is a paid plan (an uncounted recurring cost) or some persona aliases are silently not forwarding. Pull the ImprovMX dashboard and get a receipt before any persona routing gets built on top of it.

If it turns out to be over the line, Stalwart on e5 absorbs all 42 aliases at zero cost and removes the vendor entirely. That, not personal mail, is the strongest argument for Option A.

7. The DeX / VITURE workstation layer

Correcting the hardware: the glasses are VITURE Luma Pro / Luma Ultra, not "Lumina". Confirmed facts:

  • VITURE glasses require DisplayPort Alt Mode over USB-C. A Galaxy flagship supplies this, which is also exactly what DeX needs.
  • Samsung DeX drives the glasses as an external display and is reported working on Galaxy S25 Ultra. Luma Ultra is a 1200p, 152-inch virtual screen at 1500 nits.
  • SpaceWalker adds multiple virtual screens. Note the limit: full 6DoF (screen locked in physical space) needs the Pro Neckband, an INAIR Pod, or a Mac/Windows host. On a bare phone in DeX you get head-locked or 3DoF, which is fine for reading and terminal work but not for a fixed multi-monitor illusion.
Design principle. Do not try to run the suite on the phone. DeX plus glasses is a thin client. e5-mother is the compute. Everything in this plan is reachable over Tailscale in a browser tab: Vaultwarden web vault, Open WebUI, Nextcloud or the mail webmail, the Hive dashboards on their port bands. The phone renders pixels and nothing else.

This also sidesteps two known phone constraints already in the record: proot cannot npm install (SIGSEGV) and cannot decode video or large images. A browser-only client layer never hits either wall.
