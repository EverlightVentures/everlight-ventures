## Likely pain points

Based on the search results and Stripe's business model as a payments SaaS platform, here are the 3 most likely operational pain points:

## 1. Tiered Support Response Times for Small Businesses

Stripe's support system creates friction for smaller customers through variable response times. The platform offers **24/7 support but with tier-dependent response speeds**—standard-tier customers "may encounter slower or more generalized assistance" compared to enterprise clients who receive "faster replies, technical consultations, and even Slack-based communication."[1] This means small business inquiries likely experience delayed responses, creating a gap in after-hours and real-time communication coverage.

## 2. Limited Pre-Sales Live Support Accessibility

During the critical pre-sales phase, Stripe provides **minimal live human contact for small businesses** unless they proactively reach out through a form or referral.[1] This creates lead capture friction, as prospects cannot easily connect with sales representatives during standard inquiries. Enterprise and high-growth prospects receive dedicated access to "sales engineers and solution architects for strategic guidance,"[1] but this tiered approach means smaller potential customers face barriers to booking sales conversations.

## 3. Insufficient Onboarding Guidance for Non-Technical Users

While Stripe offers "layered support for technical and non-technical users" during onboarding,[1] the platform is fundamentally **developer-first**, which creates staffing challenges around customer communication for non-technical business owners. Users requiring "fully managed, hands-off payment setup" are explicitly identified as a poor fit for Stripe,[1] suggesting the company lacks operational resources to provide personalized, non-technical guidance at scale.

These pain points align with Stripe's API-based, self-serve model prioritizing developer experiences over turnkey customer support infrastructure.
