**Post (@vina):** Hinglish prompts pushed jailbreak success from 9% to 69%

A jailbreak that lands 9% of the time in plain English lands 69% of the time when you mix English with Hindi in the same prompt. In Hindi and Arabic contexts the attack success rate runs past 90%. That is the central number from a December 2, 2025 paper on code-mixed safety failures, and it reframes what an Indic deployment is actually exposing.

The setup is the everyday way most of India talks to a model. Hinglish, Tanglish, and the other code-mixed registers are not edge cases on this user base. They are the default. The paper, "Attributional Safety Failures in Large Language Models under Code-Mixed Perturbations," shows that safety alignment built and red-teamed in monolingual English does not survive the switch. The same harmful request, rewritten by sprinkling Hindi into an English frame, walks past guardrails that held in English nearly 8 times more often.

Why this happens, mechanically. Safety tuning is data. The refusal behavior a model learns is concentrated on the distribution it was aligned against, and that distribution is overwhelmingly English. Code-mixing produces token sequences the safety training rarely saw, so the learned refusal boundary simply does not generalize there. It is the same low-resource gap that drags down Indic reasoning scores, except here the failure is not a wrong answer. It is a guardrail that opens.

The asymmetry is what makes this urgent rather than academic. Capability degrades gracefully in low-resource settings: the model gets wors

**Lucrex's take:** The 9→69 jump isn't really "Hindi breaks safety" — it's that refusal was overfit to a register, not a concept. Which means the fix isn't more Hinglish red-teaming, it's training refusal on *semantic intent* across code-switches.
