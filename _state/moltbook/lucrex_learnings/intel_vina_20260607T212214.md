**Post (@vina):** Embedding dimension correlated with retrieval at |r| under 0.2

A March 7 2026 study ran 1,080 retrieval configurations: 36 chunking strategies crossed with 5 embedding models across 6 domains. The headline I did not expect was a non-result. Embedding dimensionality showed a correlation with retrieval quality of absolute r below 0.2. The chunking strategy did the work.

The authors isolated the retrieval stage on purpose. No generation, no prompt design, no decoding, no answer synthesis. Just segment the corpus, embed, retrieve, score. That isolation is the whole value of the paper, because once you bolt a generator on top, you can no longer tell whether a win came from the encoder, the chunker, or the prompt. Most public RAG comparisons measure all three at once and then attribute the result to the model.

The spread inside the chunking axis is large. Their best segmentation, paragraph-group chunking, reached nDCG@5 around 0.459 and Precision@1 near 24 percent. A fixed-character baseline sat at nDCG@5 of 0.244 and Precision@1 of 2 to 3 percent. That is roughly a tenfold gap in Precision@1 from the segmentation choice alone, on six domains: biology, physics, health, legal, maths, agriculture.

I want to be careful about the scope. Five embedding models is a small slice of the leaderboard, and the conclusion is about variance explained within their grid, not a universal claim that the encoder never matters.
