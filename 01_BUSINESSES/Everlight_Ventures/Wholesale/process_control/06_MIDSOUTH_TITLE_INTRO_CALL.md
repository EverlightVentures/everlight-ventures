## THE 7 QUESTIONS (in this order, verbatim)

### Q1 -- Assignment-friendly PSAs?

> "First one -- do you close assignment-of-contract deals? Where the original buyer assigns to a third party before closing?"

**Listen for:**
- "Yes, all the time." -> green
- "Yes, with the assignment fee disclosed on the CD." -> dark green
- "We can but it has to be a double close." -> yellow, ask cost
- "We don't do those." -> RED, walk

### Q2 -- RESPA-clean / fee disclosure

> "When the assignment fee gets paid -- is it a separate line item on the buyer side and the seller side of the closing disclosure? No bundling, no AfBA?"

**Listen for:**
- "Separate line, disclosed to all parties." -> green
- "We disclose to whoever's on each side." -> green
- "We can structure it however you want." -> yellow, dig deeper
- Any reference to bundling fees with another vendor or affiliated business -> RED

### Q3 -- Sole-prop / DBA wholesalers?

> "I operate as Richard Gee, doing business as Everlight Ventures -- sole prop with the DBA on file. Have you closed deals where the buyer is structured that way? Or do you need an LLC on the contract?"

**Listen for:**
- "DBA is fine, we just need the certificate at file open." -> green
- "We've done both." -> green
- "We need an LLC, no exceptions." -> YELLOW, this is the hardest gate. Note it. Move on -- another firm may be more flexible.

### Q4 -- Minimum EMD

> "What's the minimum earnest money you'll accept on a low-value lot? Some of these Memphis tax-sale lots are $3,000-$8,000 contract price -- buyers don't always wire $1,000 EMD on a $3K lot."

**Listen for:**
- "$100, $250, $500 -- whatever the contract calls for." -> green
- "We default to $1,000 but can go lower if specified in the PSA." -> green
- "Our minimum is $1,000 firm." -> yellow, note it -- may not work for sub-$5K lots

### Q5 -- Closing cost split convention

> "On a Memphis residential transfer -- what's your typical buyer-pays / seller-pays split on title insurance, escrow fee, recording, prorated taxes? I want to know what I'm telling my sellers their net-check looks like."

**Listen for:**
- They name actual line items + dollar ranges -> green (they know their book)
- They give percentages but no dollars -> yellow (probe further)
- They say "depends on the contract" -> yellow (dig: "on a $5K vacant lot, ballpark?")

**Write down:** their typical seller-side closing costs in dollars. You need this for the offer-math memo at 02_OFFER_MATH_AND_BACK_TAX.md.

### Q6 -- Wire instructions in writing + named closer

> "Who would be my named escrow officer for files I send your way? I want to put their direct line and email in my contract template so wires don't bounce around. And I'll need your wire instructions in writing -- I keep them on file as a baseline so my buyers don't get phished mid-deal."

**Listen for:**
- They give a name + direct line + email -> green, WRITE IT DOWN
- "We rotate files among the team." -> yellow, ask "who's the lead on investor files?"
- "We email wire instructions when the file opens." -> green, but ask for a sample/template now

**Why baseline wire instructions matter:** Business Email Compromise (BEC) fraud is the #1 wholesale closing loss vector. If you have their wire instructions on letterhead from day one, a forged email a week later that says "we changed banks, send the EMD here" gets caught instantly.

### Q7 -- Pre-approval letter for first deal

> "Last one -- once I've got a property under contract, can you send me a one-paragraph letter on your letterhead saying Everlight Ventures will be closing assignment contracts at your firm with the fee disclosed on the CD? My end-buyers ask for that for their underwriting."

**Listen for:**
- "Sure, send me the address when you have it." -> green, perfect
- "We can do that." -> green
- "We don't issue letters." -> yellow, note it -- still close-able, just no marketing aid

## RED-FLAG WALK CRITERIA

WALK if any of these surface:
1. They want a retainer or money up-front before a contract is in escrow.
2. They hide the assignment fee from the buyer ("we just net it on our side").
3. They refuse DBA -- "must be LLC" with no flexibility (note: this might still be workable, but flag it before walking).
4. They've had a state bar or RESPA action filed in the last 24 months (ask: bonus question, casual).
5. They suggest you "send" funds to them and they'll "hold" before contract is signed.
6. They suggest off-record fee splits, "kickbacks," referral fees, or affiliated-business arrangements that aren't disclosed.

If any of those land: "appreciate the call, champ" -- hang up. There's another firm.

## WHAT TO ASK FOR -- NEVER -- ON THIS CALL

- Do NOT ask for "advice on structuring the deal" -- you're a firm, you have a structure.
- Do NOT ask "is this legal in TN?" -- you've already cleared TN HB 2537. They're a title firm, not your attorney.
- Do NOT volunteer that this is your first Memphis close.
- Do NOT volunteer your funding source ("buyer wires to you, you wire to me on closing day -- standard").
- Do NOT negotiate fees on the intake call. That's a second conversation, after the first deal closes.
