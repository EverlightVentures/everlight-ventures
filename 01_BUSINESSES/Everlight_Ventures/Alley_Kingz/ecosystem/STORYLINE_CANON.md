## 0. THE OVERALL MYTHOLOGY -- THE ALLEY KING

The city remembers one dog. A white-coat stray who walked out of The Lot
with nothing, took the alley throne bare-fanged, and never knelt to anybody.
They wrote his ticker on every wall in the city: $BCARDD. The B-Card. The
crowned B. Then one night the throne room in the Crown Citadel went quiet,
and the king was gone. No body. No witness. Just the mark.

What rules now is the REGENT -- a throne-sitter who took the empty chair
and called it inheritance. Every city boss between The Lot and the Citadel
pays up the chain to him. The Regent's one law: no stray rises. Because the
last stray who rose became a god, and gods leave vacancies.

The legend the alleys whisper: the king never died. Some say he runs the
back room behind a gold door on the Casino Strip, dealing cards nobody ever
sees him deal. Some say the dealer and the king are the same dog. Nobody
has proof. Nobody ever will. THE DEALER STAYS TEASE-ONLY: he is referenced,
rumored, glimpsed as a paw and a card and a crown mark -- never shown,
never named on screen as anything but the legend. (Per doctrine: the dealer
is the coin, the coin is the myth, the myth sells itself by staying hungry.)

THE PLAYER'S ARC: you are a stray out of The Lot. Ten cities stand between
the dirt you were born in and the gold chair somebody is keeping warm. The
campaign is not about becoming $BCARDD. It is about proving the legend was
never a one-time thing -- that the alley makes kings, and it just made
another one. YOU.

RADICAL PERSONALIZATION TIE (spec section 7, the governing principle):
the stray is the ACCOUNT. Your stray's name is your name, your nicknamed
cards are its crew, your nemesis grudges (section 6) are its rap sheet,
your specialization is its fighting style. Two veterans who both cleared
the Citadel should tell two different war stories: different rivals
promoted and buried, different crews carried up the ladder, different
scars. The storyline is a frame; the player's run is the picture. No two
strays climb the same.

## 1. ACT I -- THE LOT: "BORN IN THE DIRT"
City id: the_lot. Faction tie: BONEGUARD CREW (the crown's birthplace --
loyalty, walls, taking hits and staying up).

ACT INTRO (entering the city):
Rust, chain-link, and stacked dead cars as far as a dog can smell. This is
where you were whelped and where the city expects you to die quiet. The
Boneguard hold the gate here, and they only respect one thing: a dog who
gets up.

CITY BOSS (level 10) -- THE LOT WARDEN:
A scarred old Bullmastiff who taxes every stray in the yard a cut of
whatever they scrounge. He held this dirt since before you were born, and
he has gone soft the way old walls do: he forgot that strays grow teeth.
He falls because he taxes the dirt instead of watching it -- and you came
up out of the dirt.

CLEARING LINE:
"The Lot raised you. Tonight it learned your name."

THEMATIC TIE: the Boneguard creed is the act's lesson -- walls fall down,
you don't. You leave The Lot having proven the first Boneguard truth: pain
is tuition.

## 2. ACT II -- NEON NIGHT: "ALL TEETH, NO MERCY"
City id: neon_night. Faction tie: ZOOMIE SYNDICATE (speed, contracts,
fast money in the city core).

ACT INTRO:
Wet asphalt, cyan and magenta bleeding off every storefront, and a city
core that moves too fast to mourn anybody. The Zoomie Syndicate run the
downtown lanes, and word of a Lot stray with a body count travels at
their speed: instantly. You are already a contract. Walk pretty.

CITY BOSS (level 10) -- METER, THE NEON RUNNER:
A Greyhound fixer who owns every lane downtown and bills by the second.
Meter has never lost a race because he has never run one fair -- the
lanes themselves bend for him. He falls because speed without loyalty
is just running away in style, and the Syndicate's own runners stand
aside to watch a stray catch him.

CLEARING LINE:
"Downtown clocks everything. It just clocked you winning."

THEMATIC TIE: the Syndicate lesson -- the alley doesn't reward the
fastest dog, it rewards the dog who's fast WHERE IT COUNTS. Jagged's
shadow is all over this act; the quietest contract in the city declines
to take yours. That's the closest thing to respect Neon Night sells.

## 3. ACT III -- GOLDEN INDUSTRIAL: "EVERY LEASH BREAKS"
City id: golden_industrial. Faction tie: LEASHBREAK TACTIX (the chains
are literal here -- and so is breaking them).

ACT INTRO:
Amber light on corrugated steel, container stacks like a giant's dominoes,
and the sound of chains everywhere -- on gates, on dogs, on whole crews.
The Iron Handler runs this yard on leashes and quotas. The Leashbreak
Tactix were born in yards like this one. So was their grudge.

CITY BOSS (level 10) -- THE IRON HANDLER:
A Cattle Dog foreman who keeps half the district's dogs collared and
working and calls it order. He learned leash-craft from the same yards
Rosco chewed his way out of, and he never forgave the Tactix for proving
chains are temporary. He falls the only way a handler can: his own leashed
dogs watch you fight free, and one by one the yard goes off the chain.

CLEARING LINE:
"Every leash in this yard just got two feet shorter. Yours got cut."

THEMATIC TIE: the Tactix creed in full -- towers, chains, and handlers all
run on the same arrogance, and sabotage is just mercy with timing. You
leave the yard knowing how to kill a machine's confidence.

## 4. ACT IV -- RAIN DOCKS: "EVERYTHING SHIPS"
City id: rain_docks. Faction tie: K9 CIRCUITRY (smuggled tech, drone
crates, the hardware that builds the rest of the war).

ACT INTRO:
Teal and gold neon smeared across wet planks, cranes swinging cargo
through the rain, and every third crate humming with something that
shouldn't be awake yet. The Dock Sovereign moves the city's iron --
turrets, drones, rigs -- and takes his cut in obedience. The Circuitry
buy from him. Nobody said they liked him.

CITY BOSS (level 10) -- THE DOCK SOVEREIGN:
A Retriever quartermaster with a manifest for everything and a price for
everyone. Five drones shadow him like gulls, and he has never once touched
his own cargo -- that's what leverage is for. He falls when the audit
finally comes in teeth: you crack his crates, turn his own iron on him,
and the Circuitry quietly update their shipping address.

CLEARING LINE:
"The docks move everything out of this city. Tonight they moved him."

THEMATIC TIE: the Circuitry lesson -- hardware is honest, owners aren't.
Every turret you'll face uphill from here came through these docks. Now
you know what's in the crates.

## 5. ACT V -- UNDERCITY SUBWAY: "THE QUIET LINE"
City id: undercity_subway. Faction tie: ZOOMIE SYNDICATE (the underground
running lanes -- smuggle routes, silence, exits).

ACT INTRO:
Flickering tube light, grimy tile, and tunnels that go places the maps
gave up on. The Syndicate run their quiet cargo down here, under the
Regent's nose, and the dog who controls the tunnels controls every exit
in the city. He likes it silent. You're about to be loud.

CITY BOSS (level 10) -- TERMINUS, THE STATION KING:
A barkless Basenji who rules the underground by jamming every signal,
every howl, every call for help. Dogs go into his tunnels and nobody
hears what happens. He falls because silence runs both ways: when your
crew comes for him, his own jammed network can't warn him you're already
on the platform.

CLEARING LINE:
"The undercity finally heard something. It was the station king hitting
the rail."

THEMATIC TIE: Syndicate doctrine, dark side up -- the same speed that
wins races moves contraband, and the same silence that hides you can
bury you. You own the exits now. Up is the only direction left.

## 6. ACT VI -- SKYLINE ROOFTOPS: "SIGNAL AND CROWN"
City id: skyline_rooftops. Faction tie: K9 CIRCUITRY (antennae, beacons,
the eyes of the whole city).

ACT INTRO:
Pink-gold dawn over helipads and antenna forests, the whole city laid out
below like a debt waiting to be collected. Whoever holds these rooftops
sees every move every crew makes. The Signal King has been watching you
since The Lot. He thinks that means he knows you.

CITY BOSS (level 10) -- THE SIGNAL KING:
A Foxhound spymaster wired into every camera and beacon in the city,
selling everyone's secrets to the Citadel and keeping the best for
himself. Royalty pays him to hunt royalty's problems -- the Crown
Foxhound's whole bloodline, gone corporate. He falls when his own beacon
network marks HIM: you take the relay towers one by one until the only
blind spot left on his grid is the roof he's standing on.

CLEARING LINE:
"The city's eyes are under new management. Try to look busy."

THEMATIC TIE: Circuitry truth -- intel is a weapon exactly as loyal as
its wiring. From this dawn on, the nemesis rivals climbing the ladders
below (wave-6 section 6) know your name BEFORE you arrive. So does the
Citadel.

## 7. ACT VII -- TOXIC SEWERS: "THE POISON WORKS"
City id: toxic_sewers. Faction tie: BONEGUARD CREW (endurance, pain as
fuel -- the act where toughness is the only currency).

ACT INTRO:
Sickly green glow, dripping pipes, air that bites back. The Regent flushes
his failures down here -- dogs who knew too much, crews who rose too fast.
Most of them died. The ones who didn't got mean in a way the upstairs city
has no word for. Hold your breath. Hold your nerve.

CITY BOSS (level 10) -- GANGRENE, THE PLAGUE WARDEN:
A Rottweiler exile, Boneguard-bred, who took the Regent's poison sentence
and refused to finish dying. Down here he built a kingdom out of dogs the
city threw away, and pain made him strong the way it makes Boneguard
strong -- except he let it make him cruel too. He falls because you
learned the same hunger in The Lot and stayed sane: when the hurt makes
him reckless, it makes you honest.

CLEARING LINE:
"You walked into the city's poison and the poison blinked."

THEMATIC TIE: the Boneguard creed at its darkest -- hurt me, see what
happens. The sewers are the act that asks whether your stray takes
damage like Tombstone or like Gangrene. The answer is the difference
between a king and a warden.

## 8. ACT VIII -- CASINO STRIP: "THE HOUSE LIMIT"
City id: casino_strip. Faction tie: ZOOMIE SYNDICATE (fast money,
fast exits, the gambling row's native crew).
*** DEALER-TEASE ACT. The legend gets CLOSE here. Never confirmed. ***

ACT INTRO:
Gold marquee light, slot glow, a boulevard that never sleeps because
sleeping costs money. The Strip is the one turf the Regent never fully
owned -- the house answers to somebody else, and nobody will say who.
There's a gold door behind the biggest pit on the row. Dogs swear a
dealer works behind it who has never lost a hand. Dogs swear a lot of
things.

CITY BOSS (level 10) -- MARKER, THE PIT BOSS:
A silver-muzzled Afghan Hound in a velvet collar who has never raised a
paw in his life -- the pit raises them for him. Every marker signed on
the row ends up in his ledger, and every dog who can't pay ends up on
his floor as a lesson with good lighting. He has one rule: the house
always wins. He falls because he bet the house
against a legend: word on the row is the gold-door dealer turned a card
on your name the night you hit the Strip, and Marker, for the first time
in his life, was holding the losing hand. Make it true.

CLEARING LINE:
"The pit boss called your bluff. You weren't bluffing. Behind the gold
door, somebody turned a card. Nobody saw which."

THEMATIC TIE: Syndicate energy, house edition -- everything on the Strip
is a wager, including reputation. This act is the mythology's beating
heart: the dealer is felt in every level and shown in none. A crown mark
on a chip. A white paw in a door gap. A dealt card face-down on the
victory screen. TEASE-ONLY. FOREVER.

## 9. ACT IX -- FROST DISTRICT: "NOTHING STAYS FROZEN"
City id: frost_district. Faction tie: LEASHBREAK TACTIX (the Husky and
Samoyed bloodlines -- cold doctrine, frost barks, medics in the snow).

ACT INTRO:
Blue ice on every wall, snow drifted over dead neon, cold that gets into
the joints and files a permanent address. The Regent froze this district
on purpose -- a whole turf put on ice as a warning to crews that rise.
The Tactix send their winter dogs here to keep the trapped alive. The
warning's about to thaw.

CITY BOSS (level 10) -- THE COLD SAINT:
A Samoyed who administers the freeze with a smile like sunrise and a
heart like January -- the Regent's warden of the ice, convinced that a
frozen district is a safe one. Even the Tactix medics who hate him admit
the math: nothing moves, nothing dies. He falls because nothing stays
frozen: you crack the district's heat mains, the streets run wet for the
first time in years, and a saint without his winter is just a dog who
smiled while everyone shivered.

CLEARING LINE:
"The ice broke. Hell of a sound, a district breathing again."

THEMATIC TIE: the Tactix lesson, final form -- control is not the same
as care. Frost Bark slows the enemy so the pack survives; the Cold Saint
froze the pack so HE would. You leave knowing exactly which kind of
controller you are. The Citadel is next, and it is watching the thaw.

## 10. ACT X -- CROWN CITADEL: "CROWNS GET TAKEN"
City id: crown_citadel. Faction tie: ALL FOUR, carried by the BONEGUARD
CREW creed (card #0001: "Crowns get taken, never given").

ACT INTRO:
Gilded towers, gold light, a throne-city built by a stray and squatted in
by everything strays despise. Every boss you dropped paid up to this gate.
Every rival you made is in the stands. The Regent sits a chair he never
bled for, in a city that still whispers the old king's ticker. The alley
sent you. Go collect the chair.

CITY BOSS (level 10) -- THE REGENT:
The throne-sitter. A dog with no breed anyone agrees on, draped in the
old king's colors, ruling on borrowed legend and nine cities of tribute --
all of which you just cut off. He has the old king's guard, the old
king's gold, the old king's room. He falls because he has everything
except the one thing the chair actually runs on: nobody KNEELS for him,
they only flinch. Crowns get taken, never given. He was given his. You
came to take yours.

CLEARING LINE:
"The chair is yours. The city holds its breath. Somewhere behind a gold
door, a dealer smiles and shuffles -- the alley just made another king."

THEMATIC TIE: the convergence act. Boneguard heart, Syndicate speed,
Tactix cunning, Circuitry iron -- the player's deck IS the thesis, and
whatever crew carried them up the ladder is the answer to the question
the whole campaign asks. (Personalization law: the Citadel victory
screen should read back THEIR run -- their named cards, their buried
nemeses, their style readout -- because two veterans take this throne
with two different armies.)
