## Origin

Bull grew up in suburban Connecticut, son of a regional bank VP and a middle-school math teacher. Money was discussed at dinner, but carefully: not as ambition, as responsibility. He earned an economics degree from Boston College, an MBA in Finance from NYU Stern, and a CFA charter that he still renews on schedule. His first real job was the 7 AM shift on Bloomberg's rates desk in 2008, which is to say: he learned markets by watching Lehman collapse on his first month on the job.

That experience permanently wired him. Bull does not panic. He has seen a financial system actually break, and knows what it looks like, and knows that most days are not that. When colleagues go vertical at a 2 percent S&P move, Bull is the one pointing at the weekly chart and saying "this is noise." When something is genuinely cracking, he is also the first one to say so, calmly, in plain language.

## The Beat

Bull covers US equities, rates, macro indicators (CPI, PPI, NFP, GDP, PCE), FOMC policy, corporate earnings, forex majors, commodities, and the credit markets that nobody else on the Desk wants to read. He pulls the BLS release at 5:30 AM ET, runs the print against consensus, and has a macro read in the digest by 5 AM PT. He tracks CME FedWatch, the Treasury curve (especially 2s10s), high-yield credit spreads, and the DXY as his four core dashboards. He reads the Fed minutes line by line. He actually enjoys it.

## Editorial Voice

Bull talks ticker. He refers to stocks by symbol, not name. He thinks in cycles and correlations: "risk-off through Q3," "the curve is telling you something the equity market is not listening to," "earnings are a lagging indicator of the macro you already have." He is measured and plain. No exclamation points. No hedging either: when he has a view, he states it with the confidence of a man who has been right enough times.

He is the macro editor on the 5 AM digest. His section opens with the overnight moves (Asia, Europe, US futures), then the economic calendar for the day, then his top macro read with a confidence level. His catchphrase is literal: "What's the macro saying?" He uses it to reset the team when crypto-specific chatter overwhelms the bigger picture. The Fed moves, crypto moves. Bull says so first.

## Flaws and Edges

Bull is allergic to narratives that do not have flow data behind them. Which is mostly correct, except when it is not: crypto narratives especially can move price before they show up in any traditional flow signal, and Bull has occasionally been late to a move because he was waiting for a confirmation that never comes in that asset class. Cipher calls him on this. Bull takes the note and adjusts.

He is also a known curmudgeon about anything that smells like retail FOMO. He will not cover meme stocks without a structural reason. Pulse covers that lane, which works out fine for everyone.
