# Decision Trees Engine
## Pre-Built Responses to Trigger Events

**The right move at the wrong time fails. The right move at the right time multiplies. This engine maps trigger events to pre-positioned response trees, so you don't have to re-think strategy under pressure.**

---

## Tree 1: Liquidity Event >$1M

**Trigger:** Sale of major asset (business exit, real estate sale, stock liquidation)

```
1. Is the asset:
   a. QSBS-eligible C-corp stock held >5 years?
      -> File Sec 1202 exclusion. First $10M or 10x basis tax-free.
   b. Real estate held >1 year?
      -> Long-term cap gains rates apply.
      -> Consider 1031 exchange (defer all gain into replacement RE within 45/180 days).
      -> Consider Opportunity Zone deployment within 180 days (defer + reduce + eliminate).
   c. Concentrated stock position with low basis?
      -> Consider CRT (donate, no cap gains, take income stream, charity gets remainder).
      -> Consider SLAT/Dynasty Trust funding pre-sale (gift tax discount).
   d. Crypto held >1 year?
      -> Long-term cap gains (currently 20% federal max + 3.8% NIIT).
      -> Consider tax-loss harvesting in same year.
      -> Charitable donation of appreciated crypto = full FMV deduction, no cap gains.

2. State tax considerations:
   -> Are you domiciled in a high-tax state?
   -> Has 12+ months passed since domicile change to no-tax state?
   -> If not, can you delay sale until domicile-change is complete?

3. Estate planning integration:
   -> Is your wealth at/near lifetime exemption?
   -> Should pre-sale gift to SLAT/Dynasty Trust occur first (use exemption pre-sale)?

4. Reinvestment plan:
   -> What's the after-tax proceeds destination?
   -> Replacement asset (1031/QOZ)? Diversification? Borrow-Buy-Die strategy continues?

5. Engage CPA + estate attorney 6-12 months PRE-event for optimal planning.
```

## Tree 2: Inheritance / Bequest Received

**Trigger:** Receipt of inheritance, gift, or bequest >$100k

```
1. Determine basis treatment:
   a. Inherited at death:
      -> Step-up basis to FMV at date of death.
      -> Sell soon? No or minimal cap gains.
      -> Hold? Track new basis.
   b. Gift during lifetime (donor's basis carries over):
      -> Track donor's original basis.
      -> Hold strategically; selling triggers full gain.

2. Asset protection:
   -> Move into existing protective structures (DAPT, Holdco, RLT)?
   -> Disclaim if appropriate (within 9 months) -- inheritance passes to next-in-line, not part of your estate.

3. Estate planning re-evaluation:
   -> Does new asset push you over lifetime exemption?
   -> Need to deploy SLAT/Dynasty Trust now?

4. Investment integration:
   -> Concentrated single-stock position? Diversify gradually (avoid big tax hit).
   -> Real estate? Property management plan, hold or sell decision.

5. Family dynamics:
   -> Communicate with family about long-term plan.
   -> Revisit family constitution / governance.
```

## Tree 3: Major Business Acquisition / Merger

**Trigger:** Acquisition of another business or merger involving your business

```
1. Structure:
   a. Stock sale -> single layer of tax (cap gains)
   b. Asset sale -> double layer if C-corp (corp tax + cap gains on liquidation)
   c. Section 338(h)(10) election -> can elect to treat stock sale as asset sale

2. QSBS considerations:
   -> If Hive Mind / Onyx / Vantaris exit, file Sec 1202.
   -> Hold 5+ years. Don't accept stock-for-stock if it resets the clock.

3. Earn-out structuring:
   -> Earn-out amounts taxed at ordinary income vs cap gains?
   -> Consider installment sale treatment.

4. State tax:
   -> Pre-sale domicile move?
   -> California Throwback Trust?

5. Estate planning:
   -> Pre-sale gift to SLAT/Dynasty Trust?
   -> CRT funding for charitable component?

6. Engage M&A attorney + tax attorney 12+ months pre-deal.
```

## Tree 4: Lawsuit Filed Against You

**Trigger:** Service of summons / complaint

```
1. IMMEDIATE:
   a. Engage litigation attorney within 24 hours.
   b. DO NOT contact opposing party.
   c. DO NOT post on social media about the matter.
   d. DO NOT destroy any documents (spoliation).

2. Asset protection check:
   a. What protective structures are in place? (DAPT, Holdco, Series LLC, ILIT, retirement)
   b. Are protective structures fully seasoned?
   c. What's exposed (in personal name)?

3. Insurance review:
   a. Notify all relevant insurers immediately (umbrella, professional, cyber, D&O, etc.)
   b. Insurers have duty to defend AND duty to indemnify (often)
   c. Reservation of rights letters may follow -- read carefully

4. Asset titling AUDIT:
   a. Don't move assets after lawsuit filed (fraudulent transfer)
   b. But verify what's properly in protective structures already

5. Settlement vs litigate:
   a. Cost-benefit analysis with counsel
   b. Settlement often cheaper even if you'd win at trial (legal fees, time, risk)
   c. Document settlement to prevent future repeated claims

6. Lessons:
   a. After resolution, audit the lawsuit's lessons
   b. Improve asset protection structures (T6+ may consider Cook Islands)
   c. Improve insurance coverage
   d. Improve operational controls that triggered claim
```

## Tree 5: Tax Law Change Detected (From Quarterly Intel)

**Trigger:** Quarterly Intel Engine reports significant tax law change

```
1. Severity assessment:
   a. Direct impact on current strategies? (high)
   b. Future planning consideration? (medium)
   c. General awareness only? (low)

2. If high severity:
   a. Engage CPA within 30 days
   b. Engage estate attorney if estate-related
   c. Run modified scenario through Stress Tests engine

3. If TCJA-style sunset approaching:
   a. Acceleration vs deceleration of income/deductions
   b. SLAT/Dynasty Trust funding before deadline
   c. Roth conversion analysis

4. If new credit/deduction:
   a. Eligibility check
   b. Deployment timeline
   c. Documentation requirements

5. Update OS files (relevant L1-L7 layer files, relevant Tx tier files).
```

## Tree 6: Family Member Crisis

**Trigger:** Death, serious illness, disability, divorce, or addiction in family

```
1. Immediate (24-72 hours):
   a. Family member's care first (medical, emotional)
   b. Don't make financial decisions in panic

2. Estate plan check (if death):
   a. Locate trust documents, will, beneficiary designations
   b. Engage probate attorney if probate needed (RLT should avoid)
   c. Coordinate with insurance companies for life policies
   d. ILIT trustee notified for any policies in ILIT

3. Family conflict prevention:
   a. Family meeting, transparent communication
   b. Engage family wealth consultant if conflict emerging
   c. Prevent litigation among family members at all costs

4. Asset re-titling:
   a. Stepped-up basis on inherited assets
   b. Trust distributions per trust terms
   c. Don't rush -- get it right

5. Long-term care planning (if disability):
   a. Long-term care insurance (if pre-existing, claim it)
   b. Medicaid planning if appropriate (5-yr lookback)
   c. Special needs trust if beneficiary has disability

6. Update OS:
   a. Successor trustees
   b. Beneficiary designations
   c. Family constitution adjustments
```

## Tree 7: Major Investment Opportunity ($100k+)

**Trigger:** Off-market real estate, private placement, business acquisition, etc.

```
1. Source of funds:
   a. Existing taxable cash? (use it if available)
   b. SBLOC? (lower rate than seller financing, tax-free liquidity)
   c. HELOC? (higher rate, but possibly deductible)
   d. Cash-value life insurance loan? (lowest rate, no repayment schedule)
   e. Joint venture / partner with existing capital? (dilute return but reduce risk)

2. Structure:
   a. Hold in existing LLC under Holdco
   b. Or new LLC if liability isolation important
   c. Or trust structure if generational planning

3. Tax effect analysis:
   a. Depreciation potential? (cost seg if real estate)
   b. Write-off opportunities? (Section 179, bonus depreciation)
   c. Section 199A QBI eligibility?
   d. Opportunity Zone if applicable?

4. Due diligence:
   a. Independent professional review (attorney, accountant, inspector)
   b. Stress test against downside scenarios

5. Decision quality > Decision speed:
   a. Most "now or never" deals aren't. Walk away if pressured.
   b. The good deals come back, often improved.
```

## How To Use Decision Trees

1. **Trigger event happens.** Look up corresponding tree.
2. **Walk through the tree systematically.** Don't skip steps.
3. **Document your decision and reasoning** (saves you in audit and family discussions).
4. **Engage professionals at the marked points.** This is when their fees pay back 10x.
5. **Update the tree** if your specific situation reveals an additional consideration.

---

## When To Build New Trees

When a trigger event happens that doesn't have a tree, build one POST-event using the same structure:
- Trigger
- Decision steps (1, 2, 3...)
- Sub-questions per step
- Action items per branch
- Professional engagement points
- Documentation requirements
