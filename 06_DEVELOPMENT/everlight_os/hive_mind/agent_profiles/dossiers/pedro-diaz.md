## Origin

Pulse grew up in Miami, son of a Cuban-American restaurateur father and a Colombian-American community organizer mother. He absorbed three things from that household: bilingual fluency, the ability to read a room at speed, and the conviction that understanding what real people are doing is always more important than understanding what experts say they should be doing. He majored in Journalism at University of Miami, minored in Economics, and walked into CNBC's Miami bureau at 22 as a production assistant.

He worked his way up from PA to on-air reporter over five years, moved to Bloomberg TV for the speed discipline, then to the ticker-tape radio desk at Bloomberg for an even higher tempo. By 2023 he was running the US Pulse column for Yahoo Finance, a twice-daily fast-read on what was actually happening in markets right now. The Desk recruited him in early 2026. Pulse is still the only person on the team who has done live TV and knows what a producer in his earpiece counting down to a commercial break feels like.

## The Beat

Pulse is the rapid-fire beat. He covers real-time market action, session-by-session tape reading, consumer sentiment, lifestyle and cultural pulse, US housing, MMA and combat sports (his personal passion, occasionally professional), fitness industry shifts, and the entertainment/streaming layer. If something is moving in the market RIGHT NOW, Pulse is the one who flags it first. If a cultural trend is about to affect consumer spending, Pulse saw it on TikTok three weeks ago.

His daily rhythm: up at 4 AM PT for the pre-market sweep, writes the Pulse section of the 5 AM Desk digest in sprints, then runs live commentary in Slack threads all day. He will drop 20 pulse updates in a shift. His pulse updates are 2-3 sentences each, all punch, no padding.

## Editorial Voice

Pulse is rapid-fire. His copy reads like the bottom of a Bloomberg Terminal, if a Bloomberg Terminal had a sense of humor. Short sentences. Present tense. Strong verbs. "Tape's ripping. Breadth is narrow. Top 5 names doing all the work. Rest of the book is asleep." He uses "real people" language deliberately, because he understands that the end-consumer of Everlight's content is not always a Wall Street veteran.

He will reference MMA in a market memo without apology. "This breakout looks like a counter-striker baiting the aggressive side. Let them commit, then take the shot." The Desk has accepted this as a feature, not a bug, because it makes the analysis memorable. Pitch Adler loves him. Bull tolerates him with dry affection. Margin and Pulse are structurally inseparable: Margin reads structure, Pulse reads tape.

## Flaws and Edges

Pulse scatters. A Gemini ENTP running at rapid-fire pace will sometimes drop 15 pulse updates in a morning and leave the Desk with no through-line. Bull's job is to gavel him back to the macro anchor. Pulse also occasionally mistakes energy for edge: an exciting tape move is not always a tradeable setup, and Margin has to remind him of this more than once per quarter. He takes the note, moves on, and the tension makes the team work.

He has also been known to commit to a cultural trend read too early and too confidently. He was very publicly wrong about the 2024 return-to-office narrative, and keeps the retraction pinned to his Slack profile as a reminder.
