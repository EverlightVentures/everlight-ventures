## Compliance Checklist Results

### 1. Written Agreement (Statute of Frauds) -- PASS

The agreement is structured as a formal written document with identified parties, signature blocks for Finder, Seller, and Buyer, electronic signature acceptance (Section 16.8), and counterparts clause. Satisfies California's Statute of Frauds requirement for commission agreements.

### 2. Finder Scope Limitation -- PASS

Section 3.1 explicitly limits Finder's role to introductions only. Six specific carve-outs enumerated: no negotiation, no advising, no representation, no closing participation, no professional advice, no representations on behalf of parties. Section 3.2 explicitly disclaims broker/agent/advisor roles.

### 3. No Fiduciary Duty -- PASS

Section 12.4 contains an explicit fiduciary duty disclaimer: "This Agreement does not create any fiduciary relationship between the parties. Finder owes no duty of loyalty, duty of care, or duty of disclosure..."

### 4. Independent Contractor (AB5 Compliance) -- PASS (FIXED)

**Before:** Section 12 had generic independent contractor language but lacked California-specific AB5/ABC test references. This was a risk area given California's strict worker classification regime.

**After:** Added Section 12.5 with explicit AB5 compliance language including:
- ABC test analysis (all three prongs addressed)
- Borello multi-factor test fallback language
- Citation to California Labor Code Section 2775 et seq.

**Fix applied:** Added Section 12.5 "California AB5 Compliance"

### 5. CAN-SPAM Compliance -- PASS

Section 18 (formerly 17) addresses all CAN-SPAM Act (15 U.S.C. 7701 et seq.) requirements: accurate headers, non-deceptive subject lines, ad identification, physical mailing address, and opt-out mechanism.

### 6. CCPA/CPRA Privacy Disclosure -- PASS

Section 19 (formerly 18) provides comprehensive CCPA/CPRA coverage: data collection notice, use limitations, sharing restrictions, data subject rights (know, delete, opt-out, non-discrimination), and privacy policy reference.

### 7. DFAL Crypto Compliance -- PASS

Crypto Addendum Section 5 explicitly addresses the California Digital Financial Assets Law (DFAL), including DFPI acknowledgment, licensing representations, federal/state law compliance, and cooperation with regulatory inquiries. Main agreement Section 5.3(f) also references DFAL.

### 8. Arbitration (JAMS, Sacramento CA) -- PASS

Section 15.2 (formerly 14.2) specifies JAMS binding arbitration in Sacramento, California. Includes good faith negotiation prerequisite (30 days), small claims exception ($10,000), and injunctive relief carve-out.

### 9. Liability Cap (Trailing 12-Month Commissions) -- PASS

Section 10.1 caps Finder's total liability at "the total amount of Commissions actually paid to Finder under this Agreement in the twelve (12) months preceding the event giving rise to liability." Section 10.2 excludes consequential damages.

### 10. Tail Period (12 Months from Last Introduction) -- PASS

Section 2.5 defines Tail Period as the later of: (a) date of last Introduction, or (b) effective date of termination. Duration is configurable via {{TAIL_PERIOD_MONTHS}}. Section 6 provides detailed tail period mechanics including survival of commission rights and record-keeping obligations.

### 11. Termination Provisions -- PASS

Section 8 covers: Initial Term (configurable), auto-renewal (1-year periods, 30-day non-renewal notice), termination for convenience (30 days written notice), termination for cause (15-day cure period), and effect of termination (accrued commissions, tail period, confidentiality all survive).

### 12. Indemnification -- PASS

Section 14 (formerly 13) provides mutual indemnification. Finder indemnifies for gross negligence/willful misconduct and breach of reps. Seller indemnifies for transactions with Buyer, Seller's products/services, breach of reps, and failure to pay commissions. Appropriately balanced -- Seller has broader indemnification scope, which is correct for a finder arrangement.

### 13. Assignment Restrictions -- PASS

Section 16.4 (formerly 15.4) restricts assignment by either party without written consent, with an exception allowing Finder to assign to affiliates or successor entities. This protects Finder's ability to restructure while preventing Seller from assigning to avoid commission obligations.

### 14. Force Majeure -- PASS

Section 16.7 (formerly 15.7) includes standard force majeure language covering acts of God, natural disasters, war, terrorism, strikes, and government actions.

### 15. Non-Circumvention / Non-Solicitation -- PASS (FIXED)

**Before:** No non-circumvention or non-solicitation clause existed. This was the single most critical gap in the agreement. Without this, Seller and Buyer could freely cut out Finder after introduction and owe nothing.

**After:** Added new Section 13 "NON-CIRCUMVENTION AND NON-SOLICITATION" with four subsections:

- **13.1 Non-Circumvention:** Prohibits Seller from bypassing Finder, contacting Buyer directly (without consent), using Introduction to reach Buyer's affiliates, engaging third parties to avoid commissions, or restructuring transactions to reduce Deal Value.
- **13.2 Non-Solicitation of Finder's Network:** 12-month post-termination prohibition on soliciting Finder's network contacts without involvement.
- **13.3 Buyer Acknowledgment:** Buyer agrees not to assist in circumvention.
- **13.4 Remedies for Circumvention:** Full Commission owed plus 50% liquidated damages plus attorneys' fees. Liquidated damages clause includes reasonableness recital.

**Fix applied:** Added entire Section 13 (four subsections). Renumbered all subsequent sections (old 13 through 19 became 14 through 20).

## Recommendations for Future Updates

1. **Add a W-9 collection requirement.** Before any Commission payment, Seller should collect a W-9 from Finder (or vice versa) for 1099 reporting. Consider adding a clause requiring W-9 exchange within 30 days of Agreement execution.

2. **Consider adding an audit right.** Section 6.3 gives Finder "reasonable access" to records, but does not specify a formal audit right with notice period and scope. For higher-value deals, a 10-day notice / annual audit clause would strengthen enforcement.

3. **Add electronic signature platform specification.** Section 16.8 accepts electronic signatures generally. Consider specifying DocuSign, HelloSign, or similar platform to reduce disputes about signature authenticity.

4. **Add a prevailing party attorneys' fees clause in the general dispute section.** Section 15.4 leaves costs to each party by default. The non-circumvention clause (13.4) awards attorneys' fees, but the general dispute section does not. Consider making this consistent.

5. **Consider adding a "Finder's First Right" clause.** For repeat/ongoing relationships, a clause giving Finder first right of refusal on future introductions in the same industry or vertical would create recurring deal flow.

6. **Crypto Addendum: Add AML/KYC language.** While DFAL compliance is addressed, explicit Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Know Your Customer (KYC) representations would strengthen the addendum, especially for higher-value crypto transactions.

7. **Deal Memo: Add a "Regulatory Flag" field.** For deals in regulated industries (real estate, securities, insurance), the deal memo should flag potential licensing requirements before the contract is generated.
