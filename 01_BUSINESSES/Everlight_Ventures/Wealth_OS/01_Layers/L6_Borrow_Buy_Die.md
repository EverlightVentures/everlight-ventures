# L6 -- Borrow, Buy, Die
## How Billionaires Actually Live Without Paying Tax

**ProPublica's leaked IRS records (2021) showed the 25 richest Americans paid an effective tax rate of <1% on wealth growth. The mechanism is "Buy, Borrow, Die." This layer makes that mechanism available to you starting at T5.**

---

## The Three-Step Strategy

### Step 1: BUY
Acquire appreciating assets. Stock (especially QSBS), real estate, businesses. **Hold them.** Never sell.

### Step 2: BORROW
When you need cash to live on, BORROW against the assets at low interest rates. The borrowed money is NOT taxable income (loans aren't income). The interest on the loan is often deductible (margin interest, mortgage interest, business loan interest).

### Step 3: DIE
At your death, your heirs inherit the assets at "stepped-up basis" -- meaning the cost basis is reset to fair market value at date of death. **All accumulated capital gains are wiped out forever.** Your heirs can then sell at zero capital gains tax.

**The result:** You enjoyed billions of dollars of growth, never paid capital gains tax on any of it, and your heirs inherit it tax-free.

## The Borrowing Vehicles

### 1. SBLOC -- Securities-Backed Line of Credit
**The mechanic:** Pledge your taxable brokerage account as collateral. Get a line of credit at SOFR + 0.5-2% (currently ~5.5-7%). Borrow up to 50-70% of portfolio value.

**Use cases:**
- Bridge financing for real estate
- Business expansion without diluting equity
- Tax-free liquidity for major purchases
- Emergency reserve

**The math:** $1M in S&P 500 index funds. SBLOC up to $700k at 6%. Annual interest $42k. If portfolio grows at historical 10%, portfolio gains $100k while you pay $42k interest. NET $58k AHEAD on borrowed money you spent.

**Providers:** Schwab, Fidelity (Pledged Asset Line), Goldman, Morgan Stanley, your Holdco's prime broker.

**Risks:**
- Margin call if portfolio drops below maintenance threshold
- 2008-style crash forces forced liquidation
- Variable interest rates rise = your cost rises
- Mitigation: don't borrow more than 25-35% LTV, even though brokers will lend up to 50-70%

### 2. HELOC -- Home Equity Line of Credit
**The mechanic:** Borrow against home equity. Currently 7-9% interest. Tap up to 80-90% LTV combined.

**Strategy:**
- Tax-free liquidity from primary residence
- Interest may be deductible if used for "buy, build, or substantially improve" the home (TCJA limit)
- Acquisition indebtedness limit $750k (post-TCJA)

**Use carefully:** HELOC is variable-rate, putting your home at risk. Best for short-term bridge, NOT for living expenses.

### 3. Cash-Out Refi
**The mechanic:** Refinance mortgage and pull cash out. Lock in fixed rate (currently ~7%).

**Strategy:**
- Tax-free liquidity
- Fixed rate (lower risk than HELOC)
- Use proceeds for tax-favored investments (rental real estate, business)

**The 2026 wrinkle:** Rates are still elevated. A homeowner with a 3% mortgage from 2021 cash-refis to 7% = bad math unless the use of proceeds substantially exceeds the cost.

### 4. Cash-Value Life Insurance Loans
**The mechanic:** Build cash value in a participating whole life policy. Borrow against the cash value at 4-6% interest. The cash value continues to earn dividends as if you hadn't borrowed (for participating whole life).

**The "infinite banking" concept (Nelson Nash):**
- Build cash value over years (paid-up additions accelerate this)
- Borrow against the policy for major purchases (cars, real estate, business)
- Repay yourself at scheduled rate (or don't -- the loan reduces death benefit, but cash value still grows)
- Net effect: you become your own bank, capturing the interest you'd otherwise pay to a bank

**Critical:** Whole life sales are commission-driven. The wrong policy structure (high commissions, slow cash value buildup) is a TRAP. Only deploy with a fee-only advisor who structures for cash value (not death benefit), uses paid-up additions, and selects the right insurer (Penn Mutual, MassMutual, Northwestern Mutual, Guardian, NY Life are top mutual companies).

### 5. Margin / Portfolio Margin
**Margin (Reg T):** Up to 50% leverage on stocks. Interest rates broker-specific (~10% retail, 4-6% with relationships).

**Portfolio Margin:** For accounts >$110k, more sophisticated risk-based margin allowing higher leverage on diversified portfolios. Interactive Brokers offers very low rates.

**Warning:** Margin calls are forced sales at the worst time. Conservative use only.

### 6. Crypto-Backed Loans
**Providers:** Ledn, Salt Lending, Aave, Compound, MakerDAO

**The mechanic:** Pledge BTC/ETH/stablecoins, borrow USD or stablecoins at LTV 25-50%. Rates 5-12%.

**Use cases:**
- Avoid triggering capital gains tax on crypto sales
- Tax-free liquidity from crypto holdings
- Hedge bull market positions without selling

**Risks:**
- Liquidation if crypto drops past LTV threshold
- Counterparty risk (Celsius, BlockFi, Voyager all collapsed -- choose carefully)
- Smart contract risk (DeFi protocols)
- Mitigation: keep LTV very conservative (10-25%), use only audited platforms

### 7. Premium-Financed Life Insurance (T8+)
**The mechanic:** Bank loans you the premium for a large life policy. The policy's cash value secures the loan. You pay interest only (or accrue it). At death, policy pays off the loan, remainder goes to ILIT for heirs.

**The math at scale:** $50M death benefit policy. Bank lends $5M of premium per year for 5 years. You pay 6% interest only ($300k/yr). At death, $50M death benefit pays off $25M of accrued debt, $25M passes tax-free to ILIT.

**Best for:** $25M+ net worth, generational planning, ILIT funded.

### 8. Whole Life Premium Financing (T6-T8)
Smaller-scale version of #7. Use bank financing to fund a $1-5M whole life policy. Cash value secures the loan. Tax leverage + estate transfer + asset protection in one move.

## The Step-Up Basis Magic (THE Critical Endgame)

**IRC Section 1014:** When you die, the cost basis of your assets gets "stepped up" to fair market value at date of death.

**Example:**
- You bought $1M of Berkshire Hathaway in 1985.
- It's now worth $50M.
- Unrealized capital gain: $49M.
- If you sold today, federal tax: ~$11.6M.
- If you die holding it: BASIS STEPS UP to $50M. Your heirs sell for $50M and pay $0 capital gains tax.
- **The $49M of accumulated gain disappears for tax purposes forever.**

This is why the "Borrow" step exists. Selling triggers tax. Borrowing doesn't. Hold until death, basis steps up, lifetime gains tax-free.

**Watch for changes:** Biden administration proposed eliminating step-up basis. It survived. But Democratic policy proposals to eliminate it persist. Quarterly Intel Engine tracks.

## Borrow-Buy-Die ≠ Buy-Borrow-Spend

**Critical distinction:** This strategy works because the BORROWED money funds investments or essential life expenses, NOT lifestyle inflation.

**Right way:**
- Borrow to acquire more cash-flowing real estate
- Borrow to expand a profitable business
- Borrow to live during a year you're deferring gains for tax reasons
- Borrow for emergency liquidity instead of forced sale

**Wrong way:**
- Borrow to maintain a lifestyle your assets can't sustain
- Borrow to gamble (stock options, crypto leverage, casino)
- Borrow with no exit plan or repayment source

The ProPublica-disclosed billionaires (Musk, Bezos, Buffett) borrow against assets that produce dividends, rents, or interest exceeding the loan cost. Net positive cash flow even after debt service.

## Risk Management Rules

1. **Never exceed 30% LTV across total wealth.** Sleep at night. Survive market crashes.
2. **Diversify collateral.** Don't use one asset class as collateral for everything. Mix stocks, real estate, life insurance.
3. **Stress-test scenarios.** What if the S&P drops 50%? What if rates go to 12%? What if your business income halves?
4. **Maintain cash reserves.** 6-12 months of debt service in liquid cash, separate from collateral.
5. **Document the use of proceeds.** For tax-deductibility of interest, you must trace the borrowed funds to qualified uses (Form 4952 / business purpose).
6. **Avoid forced sales.** Loan terms with no margin call provisions, fixed rates where possible.

## The "Die" Step (Estate Planning Integration)

For Borrow-Buy-Die to fully work, the assets must be in a structure that:
1. Receives stepped-up basis at death
2. Avoids estate tax
3. Passes to heirs cleanly

**The integration:**
- Assets held in revocable living trust (probate avoidance, step-up applies)
- Major appreciated assets that exceed lifetime exemption: pre-funded into Dynasty Trust / SLAT (out of estate, but no step-up)
- Trade-off: estate tax avoidance vs step-up basis. Generally, post-sunset $7M exemption means anyone with $7M+ should plan around BOTH.
- Solution: keep some appreciated assets OUT of irrevocable trust to capture step-up, put others IN to avoid estate tax. Strategy varies per family.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Borrowing for lifestyle, not investments.** You become a slave to debt service. Wrong direction.
2. **Variable-rate borrowing in rising-rate environment.** SBLOC at 4% can become SBLOC at 9%. Lock in fixed where possible.
3. **Margin calls in 2008/2020-style crashes.** Conservative LTV (<25%) survives. Aggressive LTV (50%+) gets liquidated at the bottom.
4. **Whole life from a captive agent.** Commissions eat 80% of first-year premium. Built for the agent, not you. Use fee-only advisor or specialty firms (Insurance Pro Shop, Truth Concepts trained).
5. **Crypto-backed loans on collapsing platforms.** Celsius, BlockFi, Voyager, Genesis all imploded. Counterparty risk is real.
6. **Forgetting to die strategically.** Step-up basis only works if assets are properly titled. Joint tenancy: only half steps up (spouse's half). Community property: full step-up. Single asset in revocable trust: full step-up. Plan accordingly.
